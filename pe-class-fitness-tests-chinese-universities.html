Physical Education and Fitness Tests at Chinese Universities

By ORIGIN Education · 2026-11-27

Physical education is the part of a Chinese degree that international students most often discover too late. It carries real credits, it is graded, and it feeds a national fitness test that universities report upward every autumn. None of it is hard for a reasonably healthy student, but the paperwork around exemptions and retakes has hard deadlines. This guide explains what the pe class china university system actually requires, section by section.

PE is a compulsory credit, not optional recreation

Physical education is a mandatory, credit-bearing course in every Chinese undergraduate programme, normally four credits spread across the first two years and timetabled like any other class. A private gym membership cannot substitute for it, and a failing grade has to be cleared before graduation. The requirement originates with the Ministry of Education rather than with individual campuses, so international students are enrolled on the same terms as Chinese classmates.

How many contact hours it takes

Most universities schedule two periods a week of about 45 minutes each, for four semesters, which adds up to roughly 144 contact hours. Attendance is part of the mark, and three unexcused absences in a semester commonly wipes out the participation component entirely. Read the department noticeboard in week one, because elective sport choices close early.

Choosing a sport that suits you

Students pick from a menu that usually includes basketball, badminton, table tennis, swimming, aerobics and martial arts, with tennis or football at larger campuses. Places are allocated by lottery or by sign-up order, so popular options fill within hours. Choose a sport you can already play: the grade rewards attendance and improvement more than raw ability.

What the national fitness test actually measures

The National Student Physical Health Standard is a single battery of tests that every enrolled student sits once a year, usually in October or November, and the results are uploaded to a provincial database. It covers body mass index, vital capacity, a 50-metre sprint, a standing long jump, a seated forward bend, an endurance run and one strength item, with slightly different norms for men and women.

Where the pass line sits

Each item is scored out of 100 and the weighted total is your fitness score. Sixty is a pass, 75 rates as good and 90 as excellent, and the official standard treats anything below 50 at graduation as grounds for a certificate of completion rather than a degree. That last clause is why universities chase students who skip the test.

How PE marks are calculated

A PE course mark normally combines attendance, in-class performance, a skills test in your chosen sport and your fitness score, with the fitness element weighted somewhere between 20 and 40 per cent. Sixty is the pass mark. Whether that grade enters your GPA depends on the university: some treat PE as a pass-or-fail course, others count it like any other credit.

Continuous assessment versus the final test

Athletic ability counts for less than most students assume. Attendance, dressing appropriately, and turning up on time are usually worth more than a fast 1,000 metres, and the skills test is judged against your own starting point. A student who cannot swim but attends every session and improves steadily passes more reliably than a natural athlete who skips class.

Medical exemption and the health class route

Students with a documented condition can be moved into a health class, a lighter programme of walking, stretching and theory that still awards the credit. The route needs a hospital certificate, a review at the campus clinic and approval from your college office, and it is usually renewed each semester. A note produced after the deadline is normally refused.

What the certificate has to say

The medical note must name the condition, state that strenuous exercise is contraindicated and carry a hospital stamp, typically from a secondary A-grade hospital or above. A doctor's handwritten line saying "avoid sport" is often rejected. Ask for the diagnosis in the standard wording the campus clinic recognises, and submit it in the first two weeks of term.

Fitness test failure, retakes and graduation

Failing the fitness test does not automatically fail the PE course, but it does trigger a retake, usually scheduled in the following spring, and repeated failure can hold up graduation because universities must report a compliant cohort to the province. The retake is a second attempt at the same battery with the same thresholds, not a separate easier test.

What a normal PE week looks like

Expect a warm-up run, a block of drills for your chosen sport, and a short closing session, all outdoors unless you have chosen swimming. Classes run in the first half of the day at many campuses, which means a 7:50 start for an 8:00 session. The rhythm is predictable and the grading is transparent, which is why the course is rarely the problem students fear.

Attendance rules worth knowing

  • Arrive in sports shoes and kit; jeans and sandals are commonly refused entry.
  • Answer the roll call yourself, since proxy attendance is treated as an integrity offence.
  • Bring a water bottle and a towel, as campus facilities rarely provide either.
  • Ask for a leave slip in advance if you must miss a session for a competition or a field trip.
  • Keep every leave slip until the semester ends, because records are contested at grading time.

Practical advice for students who dislike sport

Treat the fitness test as a calendar item, not an exam. Train for eight weeks before it with three short sessions a week, focus on the endurance run because it carries a fifth of the score, and practise the standing long jump technique once rather than relying on strength. Students who walk into the test cold lose most of their points on pacing, not on fitness.

If the compulsory credit still looks like a risk, talk to the college office before the exemption window closes rather than after a fail. The rules are published, the deadlines are fixed, and almost every problem here is administrative rather than athletic. Planning the four semesters early is what keeps a PE credit from becoming a graduation problem, and it is worth ten minutes in your first week in China.
