Tuition Refunds and Withdrawal Policy at Chinese Universities

By ORIGIN Education · 2026-11-25

Withdrawing from a Chinese university is a paperwork chain, not a single decision. The academic office closes your student record, the finance office calculates what comes back, the dormitory inspects your room, and the exit-entry administration is told you are no longer enrolled. Each step has its own clock, and the money you get back depends on which step you reach before the semester's cut-off.

Who actually sets the refund rule

Refund percentages are set by each university, not by a national schedule. The Ministry of Education's Order No. 41, in force since 1 September 2017, governs how a withdrawal is processed and recorded, but the money side follows the fee policy printed in your admission package. Two students at different universities can receive very different amounts on the same day of term.

What Order No. 41 does and does not cover

Order No. 41 — the Provisions on the Administration of Students in Regular Higher Education Institutions — sets out the recognised grounds for withdrawal, the approval chain inside the university, and the requirement that the decision be recorded and reported upward. It says the student's file must be handled properly and that fees are settled according to the institution's own regulations. It does not fix percentages.

Where the real numbers live

Look for a document called the enrolment notice or the international student handbook. The refund clause is usually one short paragraph near the payment instructions, and it is often the only place the percentages appear. Ask the international office for it in writing before you pay a deposit.

The refund calendar most universities publish

Most Chinese universities use a stepped schedule tied to teaching weeks rather than calendar dates, because the semester start moves with the Spring Festival. The pattern below is the common one at provincial universities for self-funded international students; the exact bands vary, so treat it as a template to check against your own contract.

The mid-term cliff catches people. A student who decides in week nine that the programme is wrong usually gets nothing back, which is why the decision to leave is worth making in the first fortnight.

Tuition, accommodation and deposit are refunded separately

These are three different pots of money with three different owners, released on different schedules. Tuition goes back through the finance office. Accommodation is handled by the dormitory management centre and calculated monthly, not by teaching week. The room deposit is released only after a physical inspection.

The deposit inspection

A staff member walks the room with you and checks the furniture inventory, the wall and the bathroom fittings. Missing or broken items are deducted at replacement cost, which is why the inventory sheet you signed in September is worth photographing. Deposits typically run from RMB 500 to RMB 2,000 and come back in 5 to 30 working days.

What is never refunded

Application fees, JW202 processing fees, insurance premiums already paid to the insurer, and visa or residence permit fees are gone. So is the medical examination you paid for, because that is a service delivered rather than a deposit held.

The paperwork that starts the clock

Nothing is calculated until the withdrawal is formally approved, and approval needs signatures in a fixed order: adviser, department head, academic affairs office, finance office, then the dormitory. Missing one signature is the most common reason a refund sits in limbo for a whole semester while the student has already left the country.

  1. Collect the withdrawal application form from your school or department office.
  2. Get your academic adviser or class teacher (辅导员) to sign, confirming no outstanding coursework disputes.
  3. Have the department head countersign and forward it to the academic affairs office (教务处).
  4. Take the approved form to the finance office (财务处) for the refund calculation.
  5. Complete the dormitory checkout sheet and return the room key and card.
  6. Return library books and any laboratory or sports equipment.
  7. Collect the final settlement statement, which states the amount and the payment date.

Photograph every signed sheet: that bundle is your evidence if the money does not arrive.

What withdrawing does to your visa

Your residence permit is tied to your student status, so it does not survive withdrawal. Under the Measures for the Administration of Schools' Admission and Cultivation of International Students — a joint order of the Ministry of Education, the Ministry of Foreign Affairs and the Ministry of Public Security, effective 1 July 2017 — the university must report a change in your status to the local exit-entry administration, normally within ten days.

You then have a short window to leave China or apply for a different permit category. Do not assume you can stay until the printed expiry date; once the school files the report, that permit can be cancelled early. Book nothing non-refundable until exit-entry stamps your new status.

If you hold a CSC or Confucius scholarship

Government scholarship students face a different arithmetic because most of the money is not tuition at all. The Chinese Government Scholarship pays a monthly stipend — RMB 2,500 for undergraduates, RMB 3,000 for master's students and RMB 3,500 for doctoral students under the current standard — plus tuition, accommodation and insurance paid to the institution.

Withdrawal ends the stipend from the month of departure, usually calculated by day. Tuition already transferred is recovered between the university and the scholarship council, so a CSC student who leaves in October should expect a prorated living allowance and no cash tuition refund.

How the money actually comes back

International refunds are slow and usually return by the same route the money arrived. A wire transfer goes back as a wire transfer, so the receiving bank, the intermediary bank and the currency conversion each take a cut, and you may get back less than the RMB figure on the statement.

Cross-border refunds also touch foreign exchange rules: the RMB 50,000 annual individual purchase quota applies to conversions, and a refund reconverted to foreign currency must be documented. Expect 30 to 60 working days and the original remittance receipt.

A checklist before you sign anything

  • Confirm the refund percentage in writing, with the date it was calculated.
  • Photograph the room inventory and meter readings on the day you hand back the key.
  • Ask for a settlement statement stamped by the finance office, not a verbal figure.
  • Check whether the refund goes to a Chinese account or the overseas account, and keep that account open until it lands.
  • Ask the international office for the exact date they will report your status change to exit-entry.
  • Keep the JW202 and admission letter copies in case you reapply elsewhere.

A clean withdrawal is about sequence. Signatures in the right order and a stamped settlement turn a stressful exit into a two-month task with a predictable end date.
