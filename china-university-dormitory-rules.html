Dormitory Rules at Chinese Universities: Curfew, Guests and Inspections

By ORIGIN Education · 2026-11-14

University dormitories in China are administered like small institutions, with a gate, a roster, a warden and a written record of everything that happens in the corridor. Most international students learn the rules in the first month by being corrected. The ones who read them first avoid the two outcomes that cost money: confiscated appliances and a conduct note that follows them into the scholarship review.

Why Dormitory Rules Carry More Weight Than Students Expect

Dormitory conduct is recorded and reported, and those records are read at the annual scholarship review. A single written warning is usually harmless, but repeated late returns, unregistered guests or failed inspections become a conduct entry a committee can weigh. The dormitory is the one part of student life where behaviour is documented by default.

What differs from other countries is enforcement: a gate with a card reader, a warden on each floor, and an inspection sheet that is signed and filed. Nothing is informal, so nothing is forgotten.

The Curfew and How the Gate Card Works

Most international student dormitories close their gates between 22:30 and 23:30, and entry after that hour requires the card to be swiped at the warden's desk with a reason recorded. The exact time is printed on the noticeboard inside the entrance and in the accommodation contract, and it can differ between buildings on one campus.

The gate card is also the attendance record. Swiping in and out generates a log the international office can read, which is how a student who has not returned for three days is identified. Leaving the card with a friend, a practice that circulates every year, is treated as a serious violation.

Late return procedures

Returning after curfew is allowed, not punished automatically. The student signs a late-entry book, states the reason, and the entry reaches the international office weekly. Repeated late returns for the same reason, such as a laboratory that runs past midnight, can be authorised in advance with a departmental letter.

Guests, Overnight Stays and the Registration Rule

Overnight guests are prohibited in essentially every dormitory, regardless of gender, and visitors must sign in at the gate and leave before the curfew. The ban is administrative rather than moral: the building's occupancy record has to match the police registration list, and an unregistered person in a room breaks that match.

A first recorded overnight guest usually produces a written warning; a second produces a formal conduct entry; a third can lead to a room change or loss of the dormitory place, which converts a subsidised room into a market rent.

The 24-hour police registration

Under Article 39 of the Exit and Entry Administration Law, a foreign national must register their residence with the local public security bureau within 24 hours of arrival. Students living on campus are registered in bulk by the university, which is why the passport is collected at check-in. A student who moves off campus must register the new address personally, within the same 24 hours.

Hotels do this automatically. Staying with a friend in a private home does not, and the host is legally responsible for the registration. A weekend trip that skips this step surfaces at the next visa renewal rather than at the time, which is why it catches students by surprise.

Electrical Appliances and What Gets Confiscated

Dormitory rooms are wired for lighting, laptops and a kettle, not for heating or cooking. Induction cookers, electric heaters, rice cookers and any appliance with an exposed heating element are banned in almost every building, and inspections target exactly these items because they cause dormitory fires.

Confiscation is immediate and the item is usually not returned until the end of the academic year, with a fine attached at some universities. A shared kitchen exists in most international student buildings, and using it is the compliant way to cook. Extension leads are tolerated; daisy-chaining three of them is not.

Room Inspections and the Hygiene Score

Rooms are inspected on a published schedule, usually monthly, by the floor warden and a student representative. The inspection scores cleanliness, waste disposal, appliance compliance and fire safety, and the score enters the comprehensive assessment record used for scholarship review and for room allocation next year.

How often they run

Monthly routine inspections are standard, with additional checks before holidays and at the start of each semester. Spring Festival and the summer vacation trigger a safety inspection, because empty rooms and unattended appliances are the risk window. Routine checks give a day of notice; safety checks give none.

What a bad score costs

A single low score is corrected and forgotten. Two consecutive low scores produce a conduct note, and three can cost the dormitory place at the end of the year, which for a scholarship holder means losing the accommodation component of the award. A score can be appealed within a week, and photographs taken that day are the evidence that works.

Moving Out, Damage Deposits and Refunds

Leaving a room cleanly is a process with documents, and the deposit is released only when the paperwork matches the inventory. Six steps keep the refund routine, and all six are easier before the last week of the semester than during it.

  1. Give written notice to the international office at least one month before the departure date.
  2. Request the room inventory sheet signed at check-in and compare it item by item.
  3. Photograph every wall, surface and fixture on the day the room is emptied.
  4. Settle outstanding utilities, since electricity is metered separately in many buildings.
  5. Return the gate card and the key in person and obtain a receipt.
  6. Ask for the deposit refund in writing with a date, rather than accepting a verbal promise.

When Rules Conflict With Your Timetable

Laboratory work, clinical rotations and library deadlines do not respect a 23:00 gate, and the compliant solution is a written authorisation obtained in advance rather than a conversation at the desk at midnight. Departments issue such letters routinely for students on late practicals, and once the international office informs the warden, the late entries stop counting.

The same logic applies elsewhere in the building. Almost every rule has an approval route, and it is faster before the event than after. Students who treat the warden and the international office as people to inform rather than to avoid spend four years without a conduct entry, which is what matters at the scholarship review.
