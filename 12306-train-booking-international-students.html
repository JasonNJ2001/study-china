Booking Train Tickets in China with 12306 for International Students

By ORIGIN Education · 2026-11-28

China's rail network carried about 4.5 billion passenger journeys in a single year, and almost all of them are booked through one official system. For international students that system is now genuinely usable in English, but the details that cause failures are specific: name format, passport verification, the presale window and the refund timetable. Get those right and trains become the cheapest reliable way to move around.

Registering on 12306 with a passport

Registration on 12306 requires your passport number, name exactly as printed in the machine-readable zone, nationality and a mobile number. Foreign passports are accepted, but the account is only fully usable once identity verification is complete, which can be done in the app or in person at any station ticket counter. Verification usually clears within minutes, though it can take up to 24 hours.

The most common failure is a name mismatch. Chinese booking systems treat your surname and given names as separate fields and are unforgiving about middle names, hyphens and spacing. Copy the name from the passport's machine-readable zone rather than the visual page, and keep the same format for every ticket you buy, because the name on the ticket must match the passport used at the gate.

Using the English interface

The official app and 12306.cn both offer English, and the English pages accept passport details without switching to the Chinese version. Station names appear in pinyin, which is easier than it sounds once you know that Beijing South is Beijingnan and Shanghai Hongqiao is Shanghaifengqiao. Search by the pinyin station name rather than the city name when a city has several stations.

The 15-day presale window

Tickets go on sale 15 days before departure, with the exact release time varying by station, typically between 08:00 and 18:00. Popular routes at the start of a holiday period sell out within minutes of release, and the seats that remain afterwards appear intermittently as other passengers cancel. The system also offers a waitlist function that works for verified accounts.

Plan around two calendar events that empty entire routes: the Spring Festival travel period, roughly 40 days around Chinese New Year, and the National Day week from 1 October. If your journey falls inside either, book on the release day itself rather than a week later.

When to buy for a semester start

September and late February are the two busiest arrival windows. Students flying into Shanghai or Beijing and travelling onward by train should book the connecting leg as soon as their flight date is fixed, not after landing. A ticket bought on arrival for the same day often means standing in a hard-seat carriage for eight hours.

Reading the train numbers

The letter before the number tells you what kind of train you are booking and how fast it travels: G and D are high-speed, C is intercity, and Z, T and K are conventional. This matters more than the departure time, because a cheaper-looking option can add six hours to a 1,200 km journey.

  • G trains are the fastest high-speed services, running at 300 to 350 km/h on dedicated lines.
  • D trains are high-speed too, usually 200 to 250 km/h, and often slightly cheaper than G.
  • C trains are short intercity services, common between neighbouring cities.
  • Z, T and K trains are conventional overnight services with sleepers and much lower fares.

For a 1,200 km journey, a G train takes about five hours and a K train about fifteen. The overnight Z train is the compromise: you sleep through the distance and save a hotel night, and a hard sleeper berth typically costs less than a high-speed second-class seat.

Choosing a seat class

Second class is the default and accounts for most of the capacity: five seats across, 3+2, with reasonable legroom and a power socket under the seat. First class gives four seats across and a quieter carriage for roughly 60 percent more. Business class on a G train is priced closer to a short-haul flight and rarely worth it for students.

On overnight trains the choice is between hard sleeper (hard in name only, six berths per compartment) and soft sleeper (four berths, a closing door). Hard sleeper is the best value on the network, and lower berths cost slightly more than upper ones because they are easier to sit up in.

Refunds, changes and missed trains

Refund fees depend entirely on how early you cancel and are charged as a percentage of the fare, not as a flat fee. More than eight days before departure costs 5 percent of the fare; between 48 hours and eight days costs 10 percent; under 48 hours costs 20 percent.

Changing rather than refunding

One change of date or train is free per ticket, which makes a change almost always better than a refund followed by rebooking. The free change applies to the same origin and destination.

If you miss the train, the ticket is not automatically dead. Staff at the departure station can usually move you to a later service on the same route on the day, subject to availability. Ask at the ticket office rather than at the gate, and bring the passport the ticket was issued against.

Boarding with a passport

Paper tickets are no longer issued for most services. You board by swiping the passport at the automatic gate, or by showing it to staff if the gate rejects the chip. Arrive 25 to 30 minutes before departure: security screening at the station entrance takes time, and the gate closes three to five minutes before the train leaves.

Security screening and luggage

Luggage goes on racks above the seat and at the carriage ends. There is no weight limit in practice, but oversized items are awkward on packed carriages. Keep your passport in a pocket rather than a bag, since it is checked at the gate, again on board, and sometimes on arrival.

Practical habits for your first booking

  • Verify your passport in the 12306 app before you need to travel, not on the day.
  • Book exactly when the 15-day window opens for holiday routes.
  • Prefer a free change over a refund; the fee difference is substantial.
  • Screenshot the booking confirmation, since station wifi is unreliable at gates.
  • Carry the physical passport even if you have a photo, because no digital copy is accepted.

Train travel is the part of student life in China that rewards preparation most directly. The system is fast, punctual and cheap once your account is verified and you understand the presale rhythm. Book early, arrive early, and the journey stops being the stressful part of the semester.
