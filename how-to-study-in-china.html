How to Apply to Chinese Universities — Step-by-Step

By ORIGIN Education · Updated for the 2027 intake · 6 min read

China is now one of the world's largest destinations for international students, with English-taught programs expanding every year. The application process is more standardized than many expect — but the timelines are tight and the document requirements are strict. This guide walks through it in order.

1. Confirm your eligibility

Eligibility comes down to three checks that reject more applications than any other item: a diploma at the level you are applying for, language proof matching the teaching language, and a passport with six months of validity left. Scholarship routes add an age ceiling — 25 for bachelor's, 35 for master's, 40 for doctoral — and that ceiling, not academic strength, is what rules out many older applicants.

  • A high school diploma (for bachelor's) or a bachelor's degree (for master's/PhD).
  • Proof of language ability — HSK for Chinese-taught programs, or IELTS/TOEFL for English-taught programs.
  • A valid passport with at least 6 months of remaining validity at the time of application.

2. Shortlist 3–5 universities

Don't apply to one school and hope. Build a balanced list of reach, match, and safety universities. See our Top Universities in China guide for a curated starting point. Note each school's application window — they vary by institution and by intake (spring vs. autumn).

3. Prepare the core documents

Six documents come up in every Chinese university application: the online form, notarised transcripts, a 500 to 1,000 word study plan, two recommendation letters for graduate study, a passport scan with photo, and the physical examination form. The bottleneck is almost never writing — it is notarisation and translation, which can take three to four weeks in some countries, so start that before anything else.

  • Completed online application form
  • Academic transcripts (notarized + translated)
  • Study plan / personal statement (500–1000 words)
  • Two recommendation letters (for graduate study)
  • Passport scan, photo, and physical examination form
  • Language certificate (HSK or English test)
Start the notarization and translation early — this step alone can take 3–4 weeks in some countries.

4. Submit through the right portal

There are two routes, and they are not interchangeable. The university's own portal is used for direct application and for its own scholarships; the CSC portal at campuschina.org is used only for government scholarships, and most applicants file both in the same cycle. Missing which route a scholarship belongs to is the most common reason a strong application never gets read.

  • University direct portals — each school has its own international-student application system.
  • CSC Scholarship portal (Type A/B) — used when you apply for a Chinese Government Scholarship. See the CSC Scholarship guide.

5. After admission: visa & arrival

Once the Admission Notice and the JW201 or JW202 form arrive, you apply for a student visa at a Chinese embassy or consulate. Which visa you get is decided by programme length, not by choice: more than 180 days takes X1, 180 days or less takes X2. The full comparison is in our China Student Visa guide.
