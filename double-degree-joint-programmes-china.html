Double Degree and Joint Programmes in China: What You Actually Get

By ORIGIN Education · 2026-11-05

A double degree in China is one of two very different products. It is either a joint institute approved by the Ministry of Education that awards a Chinese degree and a foreign degree, or a programme-level agreement inside an ordinary Chinese university that awards one degree with some foreign coursework attached. The difference decides tuition, recognition and what an employer abroad can actually verify.

What a Sino-foreign joint programme legally is

Chinese regulations recognise two forms of cooperation in education: a joint institution, which is a separately incorporated campus with its own degree-awarding power, and a joint programme, which is a single major hosted inside an existing Chinese university. Both require Ministry of Education approval before they may enrol students or issue qualifications. The approval number, not the recruitment brochure, determines whether the paper you receive is recognised.

Joint institutes versus programme-level agreements

A joint institute is a legal entity: its own campus, senate, admissions office and graduation certificate. A programme-level agreement sits inside a parent university, so you are registered as a student of that Chinese university and take a defined block of foreign-delivered modules, often concentrated in the third and fourth years, with the foreign partner contributing curriculum rather than a degree.

What 2+2 and 3+1 actually deliver

The numbers describe where you physically study, not how many degrees you earn. A 2+2 arrangement usually means two years in China and two at the partner campus abroad, and only students who complete the outbound leg receive the foreign degree. Staying in China for all four years typically yields the Chinese degree alone, which is why the mobility rules deserve reading before you accept an offer.

Which Chinese campuses award a second degree

Five Sino-foreign universities currently issue a foreign bachelor's degree to international students alongside their Chinese qualification: Xi'an Jiaotong-Liverpool University in Suzhou, the University of Nottingham Ningbo China, Duke Kunshan University, NYU Shanghai and Wenzhou-Kean University. Each holds its own admissions process, tuition schedule and English-medium teaching; none is a faculty tucked inside a larger Chinese university.

What the foreign degree is worth outside China

The foreign parchment is the partner university's own document, not a Chinese degree carrying a foreign logo. Liverpool, Nottingham, Duke, NYU and Kean print the same certificate for their Chinese-campus graduates as for their home-campus graduates, so a foreign employer or graduate school verifies the transcript through the partner's registry rather than through a Chinese database.

Where the difference shows up

Two places, mainly. Graduate admissions offices check whether the transcript is issued by the partner institution itself, and visa or licensing bodies check whether the awarding body is accredited in its home country. Both tests are passed by the five institutes above and by very few programme-level agreements, because a programme that only borrows curriculum produces no foreign transcript at all.

Tuition and the real cost gap

International undergraduate tuition at a Sino-foreign university typically runs RMB 60,000 to 120,000 a year, against RMB 20,000 to 30,000 at an ordinary public Chinese university teaching the same subject in English. The gap buys smaller classes, a foreign transcript and, in most cases, a compulsory or heavily encouraged year abroad that carries the partner's own fees.

  • Tuition at the Chinese campus, billed in RMB and payable each semester
  • Accommodation on campus, usually RMB 3,000 to 12,000 a year for a shared room
  • The outbound year at the partner campus, charged at that campus's international rate
  • Compulsory commercial health insurance, roughly RMB 800 a year
  • Visa extension and residence permit fees after each return to China

The year abroad is the hidden line item

A 2+2 structure means two years billed at British or American rates, which can exceed the entire Chinese portion of the degree. Ask for the outbound-year fee schedule in writing before accepting, and confirm whether the year is guaranteed or competitive, because a programme that admits forty students and sends fifteen abroad has a different real cost from the one on the website.

Admission requirements for international students

Entry is decided on high school transcripts plus English proficiency, usually IELTS 6.0 to 6.5 or an equivalent, and some campuses add an interview or a writing sample. Chinese language ability is not required for English-medium programmes, but a HSK certificate helps for mandatory language modules and for the internship semester that most of these campuses place in year three.

Documents that take the longest

The bottleneck is rarely the application form. It is the notarised and translated high school diploma, the sealed transcript, and the financial guarantee letter that the visa stage will later require again. Start those in the September before the intake, since a September 2027 start means applying between October 2026 and March 2027 for most of these campuses.

The approval check that decides recognition

Every approved joint institution and programme appears on the Ministry of Education's supervision platform, which currently lists more than 2,300 entries. If a programme is absent from that database, its graduates cannot obtain a certification report for the foreign qualification, and the second degree becomes a private certificate with no official standing in China or abroad.

How to run the check in ten minutes

Search the platform for the Chinese university name, then for the major, and compare the approval number on the site with the one printed on your offer letter. A programme operating under a lapsed approval, or under a partner name that does not match the registry, is the single most common source of complaints from graduates three years later.

Who should and should not take this route

Take it if you want an English-taught degree in China that a foreign employer can read without explanation, and if the outbound year is affordable and guaranteed. Skip it if your goal is the lowest-cost Chinese degree, if you need a Chinese-language immersion environment, or if the programme you are considering has no entry on the ministry platform.
