Scholarships for African Students in China: CSC, Provincial and University Schemes

By ORIGIN Education · 2026-11-06

Scholarships for African students in China are funded at three levels: the CSC bilateral programme allocated through Chinese embassies, provincial schemes such as Jiangsu or Zhejiang, and university awards paid from institutional budgets. The bilateral route is the most generous and the most competitive, provincial awards are usually partial, and university awards are the least advertised. Africans made up 81,562 of China's 492,185 international students in 2018, the second-largest continental group.

The three funding levels

Each level has a different application channel, and applying to the wrong one wastes a cycle. The CSC bilateral award is negotiated country by country through the Chinese embassy, provincial scholarships are applied for directly to the province, and university awards are decided by the institution after admission. Deadlines do not align, so a student can pursue two levels in parallel with one document set.

How the bilateral quota works

China signs education agreements with most African governments, and the quota of places per country is set in those agreements rather than by the university. The Chinese embassy in Nairobi, Abuja, Addis Ababa or Pretoria publishes the annual call, and the host ministry of education often runs a parallel nomination. Quotas are small, in the tens per country, which is why the embassy channel is the hardest of the three to win.

What each level actually pays

CSC bilateral awards are full funding: tuition, a dormitory place or housing allowance, medical insurance and a monthly stipend of RMB 2,500 for bachelor, 3,000 for master and 3,500 for doctorate. Provincial awards typically cover tuition and part of the stipend, and university awards range from a full package to a 50 percent tuition waiver with nothing else.

The hidden costs in a partial award

A tuition waiver still leaves accommodation at RMB 600 to 1,200 a month, insurance at RMB 400 to 800 a year, the residence permit fee and food. A realistic floor for a partial-award student in Nanjing or Wuhan is RMB 1,500 a month, which is why applicants should treat a 50 percent waiver as a discount rather than a scholarship.

The FOCAC commitment behind the numbers

The Forum on China-Africa Cooperation is where the totals come from: at the 2018 Beijing summit China committed 50,000 government scholarships and training places for African candidates over the following three years. Those commitments are delivered through the CSC bilateral channel, which is why African quotas grew faster than quotas for other regions in that period. The 2024 summit renewed the training and scholarship commitments on a similar scale.

Documents, and why African files fail

Verification is the bottleneck, not grades. Chinese universities require notarised degree certificates and transcripts, and increasingly a verification report from the issuing country's education authority, because forged secondary certificates from a small number of markets have made admissions offices cautious. A file that starts in January has time to clear verification; a file that starts in March usually does not.

  • Notarised degree certificate and full transcripts, with translation
  • CSC online application form, printed and signed
  • Two recommendation letters on university letterhead
  • Study plan or research proposal, 500 to 1,000 words
  • Foreigner Physical Examination Form, completed within six months
  • Police clearance certificate, notarised
  • Passport valid for at least six months beyond the intended stay

The two-year master's trap

A master's applicant with no Chinese is often placed in a one-year Chinese language course before the degree starts, and the CSC stipend covers that year. The total commitment becomes three years, not two, and the age ceiling is checked at enrolment. Applicants who already hold HSK 4 skip the year and finish inside the original timeline.

Choosing a programme that fits the award

The award decides the university list more than the reverse, because bilateral placements are assigned to institutions with quota capacity. Medicine, engineering and agriculture absorb most African enrolments, and around 45 Chinese universities are approved to teach the MBBS in English, which is a shorter and clearer list than the general programme catalogue.

Language of instruction as the real filter

Chinese-taught programmes require HSK 4 at 180 of 300 for science and engineering and HSK 5 at 180 of 300 for humanities, and that requirement sits above any scholarship. English-taught programmes remove it but narrow the list to a few hundred options, concentrated in engineering, business and medicine. Choosing the language first avoids applying to programmes that cannot admit you.

A sequence that works

Start with the embassy call, which opens between January and March, and prepare notarised documents in December so the file is ready when it appears. Apply to one provincial scheme in parallel as a fallback, because provincial deadlines fall later, and accept only one offer, since Chinese government awards cannot be held together.

Applicants who want the three levels compared against their own grades, budget and language level can check current programme and funding options before committing to a channel.
