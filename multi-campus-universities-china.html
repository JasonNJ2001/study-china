Multi-Campus Universities in China: Which Campus Will You Study At

By ORIGIN Education · 2026-11-23

A Chinese university brochure usually shows one gate, one library and one lake. The institution behind it may run seven campuses in four cities, and the one that admits you might be an hour outside town with a newer dormitory and a much smaller library. Campus assignment is printed on documents applicants rarely read carefully.

Why Chinese universities have several campuses

Chinese universities expanded through mergers in the 1990s and 2000s, and every absorbed college left behind a campus. Zhejiang University now lists seven campuses, Shandong University runs eight across Jinan, Qingdao and Weihai, and Sun Yat-sen University operates five across Guangzhou, Zhuhai and Shenzhen. Jilin University has six in Changchun, and Sichuan University three in Chengdu.

Mergers explain the old campuses; enrolment growth explains the new ones. Since the mid-2000s, provincial governments have built university towns (大学城) on city edges and moved first- and second-year undergraduates there, keeping the historic campus for postgraduates and continuing education.

Old campus, new campus, international campus

Three types matter to an applicant. The historic campus is central, walkable and short of dormitory space. The new campus is large, cheap and far. The international campus is a separate site built for English-taught programmes, usually with the best rooms and the highest fees.

Where first-year students are actually sent

Many universities move first- and second-year undergraduates to a suburban campus, and international students are often placed there deliberately because the newer buildings meet the accessibility and wiring standards that the old ones do not. Zhejiang University's International Campus in Haining opened in 2016 and hosts most international undergraduates, roughly half an hour from Hangzhou by intercity rail.

The medical and arts exception

Clinical medicine and fine arts stay on the oldest central campus, because the teaching hospital or the concert hall is there. A student admitted to medicine should expect the downtown site, not the new town.

How to find out which campus you are on

Check three documents: the admission letter (录取通知书), the JW202 form that supports your visa application, and the registration notice (报到须知) sent with the admission package. The campus name appears in Chinese on the admission letter and is often dropped from the English summary, so ask the international office to confirm it in writing before you book flights.

What a written confirmation should contain

Ask for the campus name in Chinese characters, the street address, the nearest metro or rail station, and the name of the dormitory building. An email that says "our university campus" tells you nothing; one that says 紫金港校区 tells you everything.

What a remote campus costs you

A suburban campus changes your monthly budget more than your tuition does. Dormitory fees are lower, often CNY 800 to 1,200 a year against CNY 1,500 or more downtown, but a taxi into the city costs CNY 60 to 120 each way, and a weekend habit becomes a real line item.

What is usually on site

New campuses typically have a canteen, a convenience store, a sports ground and a parcel station, and little else. Entertainment, hospitals, cheap restaurants and part-time work are downtown, which matters for students who plan to teach English or intern during term time.

Independent colleges are not campuses

A 独立学院 is a separate legal entity with its own diploma, even when its name includes a famous parent university. In May 2020 the Ministry of Education required all independent colleges to convert into public, private or terminated institutions by the end of that year, so any brochure still selling one should be checked against the ministry's published list before you pay a deposit.

How to tell the difference in ten minutes

Search the institution's Chinese name on the ministry's list of accredited higher-education institutions (全国高等学校名单). If the name is absent, it is not a university the ministry recognises, whatever the brochure says.

Transport between campuses

Most multi-campus universities run free or subsidised shuttle buses, typically two or three departures a day in each direction, and they fill up before exams. A student who misses the last bus pays for a taxi or sleeps in a study room.

Questions to settle before you accept

Campus location is negotiable only before enrolment, so ask now, in writing, and keep the reply in the same folder as your admission letter. These five questions take a university ten minutes to answer and can save you a year of commuting, so send them to the international office rather than to an agent.

  1. Which campus will I register at, in Chinese characters?
  2. Is my programme taught there for all four years, or does it move after year two?
  3. Which dormitory building is assigned to international students, and how many share a room?
  4. How far is the nearest hospital and metro station, in kilometres?
  5. Is the shuttle bus free, and how many times a day does it run?

A university that answers all five in one email is organised. One that answers vaguely is telling you something about the next four years, too.
