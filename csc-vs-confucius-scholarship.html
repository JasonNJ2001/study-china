CSC vs Confucius Institute Scholarship: Which Chinese Scholarship Fits You

By ORIGIN Education · 2026-11-06

A CSC scholarship pays for a full degree in any subject, while the Confucius Institute Scholarship pays for Chinese language study or teacher training and almost nothing else. The CSC covers tuition, dormitory and a monthly stipend of RMB 2,500 to 3,500 for the whole programme. The Confucius award runs one semester to four years and is administered by the language-teaching centre, not the scholarship council.

What each scheme is for

The Chinese Government Scholarship is the state's degree-funding instrument, allocated through Chinese embassies, provincial education departments and universities, and it covers any discipline from engineering to medicine. The Confucius Institute Scholarship, renamed the International Chinese Language Teachers Scholarship in July 2020, exists to produce Chinese-language learners and teachers. One funds a career; the other funds a language.

The 2020 renaming matters

Since July 2020 the award has been administered by the Centre for Language Education and Cooperation rather than the old Hanban structure, and the application portal changed with it. Older guides still describe the Confucius Institute Scholarship as it worked before that date, including institutional nomination by a Confucius Institute. Some nomination routes remain, but direct application is now normal.

Eligibility side by side

The CSC sets age ceilings by degree level and requires a completed secondary or previous degree, while the Confucius award is built around HSK scores rather than academic transcripts. A 30-year-old applicant with no Chinese can only take the CSC route. A 22-year-old with HSK 4 and no degree can take the language route, which the CSC will not fund.

The age rule catches older applicants

A master's applicant who turns 36 before September is ineligible for the CSC at master's level, whatever the academic record. The practical workaround is a doctoral application, where the ceiling is 40, or a provincial scholarship with its own criteria. Age is checked against the enrolment date, not the application date.

Money, and what is not covered

The CSC stipend is paid monthly into a Chinese bank account and starts from the month of registration, not from arrival, and the award also covers tuition and a dormitory place or a housing allowance where no dormitory is available. The Confucius award follows the same structure at the language-programme level, and both schemes exclude international airfare in almost all cases.

The costs students underestimate

Neither award covers the visa fee, the health examination, insurance top-ups for treatment outside the university clinic, or field trips. A realistic personal budget is RMB 800 to 1,500 a month in a second-tier city, mainly food and phone. Students who budget zero for this find the stipend does not stretch, because the dormitory place is the only housing cost covered.

How to apply, and where files fail

The CSC file goes through a Chinese embassy or a university, depending on the programme type, and the two channels have different deadlines within the same January-to-April window. Embassy channels require a medical form, police clearance and notarised certificates; university channels often accept scanned copies first. Incomplete files are rejected without correspondence, so the checklist matters more than the essay.

  • Application form printed from the CSC online system, signed
  • Notarised highest degree certificate and full transcripts
  • Two recommendation letters from professors, on letterhead
  • Study plan or research proposal, in Chinese or English per programme
  • Foreigner Physical Examination Form, within six months
  • Police clearance certificate, notarised, for applicants already employed
  • HSK certificate where the programme is taught in Chinese

The one-scholarship rule

Chinese government scholarships cannot be held concurrently, and the system cross-checks passport numbers across schemes. Accepting a Confucius award after a CSC offer has been confirmed results in the CSC place being withdrawn, usually after registration, when it is too late to reapply. Decide before signing the acceptance form.

Which one to choose

If the goal is a degree leading to a career outside Chinese-language teaching, the CSC is the only one of the two that funds it, and its stipend is higher at postgraduate level. If the goal is fluency, a Chinese-teaching career or a low-risk one-year test of living in China, the language scholarship is easier to win. Applying to both in the same year is allowed only if neither acceptance form is signed.

The hybrid route

A student who takes a one-year language scholarship and reaches HSK 5 can apply for a CSC degree the following year with a stronger file, since the language year counts as preparation rather than a gap. This is the most common path for applicants from countries where HSK testing is limited to two sittings a year, and it converts a language award into a degree funded by the other scheme. Applicants weighing the two timelines can compare the current programme lists and deadlines before choosing a channel.
