Examination Formats and Rules at Chinese Universities

By ORIGIN Education · 2026-11-27

Chinese examination halls are quiet, formal and run to a printed protocol. The rules are not improvised by individual teachers: they sit inside a national regulation on student management, and invigilators apply them with little discretion. Most international students who lose marks do so through a procedural mistake rather than ignorance of the subject. This guide sets out the exam rules china university students actually meet.

Closed-book is the default, and it is enforced literally

The overwhelming majority of Chinese undergraduate examinations are closed-book, written, and invigilated by two staff members per room. No notes, no formula sheets and no dictionaries are permitted unless the course outline says otherwise in writing. Where a formula sheet is allowed it is printed by the department and handed out in the hall, so students do not bring their own.

What a written paper looks like

Papers are usually a mix of multiple choice, fill-in-the-blank, short answer, calculation and one or two long essay questions, with the weighting printed on the cover. Total marks are 100 and 60 passes. Time is 120 minutes for most courses and 150 to 180 minutes for core subjects, and the invigilator announces the time remaining at 30, 15 and 5 minutes.

Open-book, take-home and computer-based formats

A minority of courses use open-book or take-home papers, mostly in law, management and humanities seminars, and language and computing courses increasingly run on computer. Open-book does not mean easier: the questions are designed so that locating the answer is not the same as applying it. Confirm the format from the course outline rather than from rumour.

Computer-based tests and their failure modes

Computer-based examinations are sat in a proctored laboratory with the network cut and a lockdown browser installed. Log in with your student number exactly as registered, because a mistyped digit locks you to a neighbouring account. Save every ten minutes, and tell the invigilator immediately if the machine freezes, as the clock does not stop.

What you must bring, and what will be taken from you

You must bring your student card and a second identity document, usually a passport for international students, and both are checked against the seating list. Phones must be switched off and placed in the bag at the front of the room, not kept in a pocket. A phone that rings in the hall is treated as a possession issue even when nothing is looked up.

Late arrival and early departure

The standard rule is that entry is refused 15 minutes after the paper starts and that nobody may leave in the first 30 minutes, though both figures vary slightly by faculty. If you are late, telephone the college office rather than running to the hall, since a documented transport problem is sometimes accommodated and an unexplained late arrival is not.

Cheating: a disciplinary code, not a course policy

Academic misconduct in China is handled under the Ministry of Education's regulation on student management, order number 41, in force since 1 September 2017. Article 52 lists conduct that can lead to expulsion, including sitting an examination for another person, organising cheating and using communication devices. Penalties run from a record of demerit to expulsion.

What the penalty actually costs

A cheating finding is recorded in the student file and reported to the provincial education authority, which means it follows you into any later verification of your degree. It can also void the credits for that course and delay graduation by a year. Copying a neighbour's answer, carrying a prepared slip or using a smart watch all fall inside the same category.

Deferred exams are the only legitimate way to miss one

If illness or a genuine emergency prevents attendance, you apply for a deferred examination before the paper, with a certificate from a secondary A-grade hospital or above for medical cases. Approval comes from the college, not the lecturer. The deferred paper is normally sat at the start of the next semester and is marked on the same scale as the original.

The applications that get refused

Retrospective applications, certificates from small private clinics, and requests based on a booked flight are routinely refused. So are requests submitted after the paper has started. If you fall ill on the morning of an examination, go to the campus clinic, obtain a stamped record the same day, and notify your college counsellor by message and email.

Make-up exams and what a 60 means

A failed course triggers a make-up examination, held in the first two weeks of the following semester, and the recorded mark for a make-up pass is usually capped at 60 regardless of the actual score. Fail the make-up and the course must be retaken in full, which can push graduation back by a semester and change your visa timeline.

How continuous assessment changes the arithmetic

Most courses split the final mark between coursework and the examination, commonly 30 to 40 per cent for attendance, assignments, presentations and quizzes, and 60 to 70 per cent for the paper. That split decides strategy: a student carrying 38 out of 40 needs 22 from the paper, while a student who skipped assignments needs almost full marks to pass.

Where the coursework marks come from

Attendance, weekly problem sets, laboratory reports, group presentations and mid-term tests are the usual components, and each is recorded by the lecturer rather than centrally. Ask in week one what the weighting is and keep your own tally, because disputes at the end of term are settled against the lecturer's record, not yours.

A practical checklist for exam week

  1. Confirm the date, room and seat number from the official timetable, not from a class group chat.
  2. Check both identity documents are valid and carry the same name spelling.
  3. Arrive 20 minutes early and locate the room the day before.
  4. Switch the phone off and leave it in your bag at the front.
  5. Write your name and student number on every page before starting.
  6. Answer the highest-mark questions first if time runs short.

The system rewards preparation and procedure in equal measure, and the procedural half is entirely learnable in an afternoon. Know the format, carry the right documents, apply for a deferral before the paper rather than after, and treat the 60 threshold as a target with margin rather than a line to be approached.
