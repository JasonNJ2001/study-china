Graduation Ceremony and Collecting Your Degree Certificate

By ORIGIN Education · 2026-11-27

Graduation in China is a tightly scripted public event followed by a slow administrative tail. The ceremony takes an afternoon; the certificates, transcripts and electronic registration that prove you graduated take weeks and sometimes months. Students who leave the country the day after the photographs are the ones who end up paying a courier to send documents abroad. This guide covers the graduation ceremony china university system end to end.

Graduation is a fixed seasonal event, not a flexible one

Chinese universities hold commencement in the last week of June or the first days of July, after examinations and thesis defences are complete and before the summer vacation begins. The date is set by the university calendar and cannot be moved for an individual student. If your family plans to attend, they should book flights for a three-day window rather than a single date.

Why the timing is rigid

The ceremony is tied to the academic calendar, to the provincial education department's reporting schedule and to the printing of certificates, which happens in batches. A university cannot issue one student's degree early because the electronic registration file is submitted for the whole cohort at once. Book travel around the published date.

What you wear, and why the colours mean something

Academic dress in China follows a national standard issued by the Academic Degrees Committee of the State Council in 1994, which fixes the gown colour by degree level and the hood colour by discipline. Bachelors wear black, masters wear a deep blue, and doctorates wear red and black. The coloured hood identifies the field, not the university.

The tassel and the moment it is turned

The tassel starts on the right side of the cap and is moved to the left by the president or dean during the ceremony, a gesture borrowed from Western practice and now standard at Chinese universities. Rental gowns are available on campus for around 30 to 80 yuan and must be returned the same day.

The ceremony itself, step by step

The programme usually opens with the national anthem, followed by speeches from the president, a faculty representative and a student representative, then the reading of the list of graduates by college. Students cross the stage in groups, receive a certificate folder, have the tassel turned and pose for an official photograph. The whole event runs two to three hours.

What you actually receive on stage

The folder handed over on stage is a ceremonial cover and usually empty. The real certificates are issued later by the college office against your student card and a signature, precisely so that they cannot be lost in a crowd of several thousand people.

Two certificates, and they are not interchangeable

Chinese graduates receive a graduation certificate confirming completion of the programme and a separate degree certificate confirming the award, and employers and universities abroad often ask for both. The graduation certificate is issued by the university; the degree certificate is issued under the authority of the Academic Degrees Committee. Losing either means a formal replacement process, not a reprint.

What each document proves

The graduation certificate records that you completed the required credits and were permitted to graduate. The degree certificate records that the academic degree was conferred, with the discipline named on it. A student who completes the programme but fails the thesis defence may receive the first without the second, which is why both are checked separately by foreign admissions offices.

Collecting in person, by proxy or by post

Certificates are issued in person at the college office against your student card, normally within one to three weeks of the ceremony. If you have already left China, you can authorise a proxy with a signed letter, a copy of your passport and a copy of theirs. Many universities also courier documents abroad by EMS or DHL at the student's cost, but only after an online request.

The proxy letter that gets accepted

A letter that is accepted carries four elements, and offices check all of them before releasing anything:

  • the proxy's full name and passport number
  • the documents they are authorised to collect
  • your handwritten signature beside a copy of your passport page
  • one sentence allowing the proxy to sign the receipt on your behalf

Some colleges require the letter to be notarised if it was signed outside China, so ask the office which version they accept before posting anything, and send it by a tracked service.

Electronic registration and verification after you leave

Every Chinese qualification is registered electronically, and the graduation record appears on the CHSI platform, operated by the national higher education student information service, usually in July for a June graduation. Employers and foreign universities verify degrees there rather than from the paper certificate. The record can take four to eight weeks to appear after the ceremony.

What to do if the record is missing

If your record has not appeared by September, contact the registrar rather than the college, quoting your student number and the date of graduation. Missing records are almost always a data-submission delay rather than a problem with your degree. Keep a scanned copy of both certificates, since verification requests frequently arrive years later when the originals are in a drawer.

Transcripts, sealed envelopes and authentication

An official transcript is printed on university paper, stamped by the registrar and sealed in an envelope that is signed across the flap. Foreign universities require the seal to be intact, so ask for three copies and keep one unopened. For use in countries party to the Hague Apostille Convention, Chinese public documents have been eligible for apostille since November 2023.

Copies, translations and cost

Most registrars issue English transcripts for a small fee per copy, typically 20 to 50 yuan, and take three to five working days. A notarised translation is a separate service, charged by the page. Order them before you leave: obtaining them from abroad means a proxy, a courier and a signature, and the process can take a month.

If your flight leaves before the ceremony

Students who must leave early can usually attend a college-level send-off, collect certificates by proxy and receive the official photographs digitally. Ask the college office to note your departure date on file, because the certificate-issuing window is normally fixed and a written request made in advance is far easier to accommodate than one made after the event.

Handle the ceremony as the celebration it is, and handle the paperwork as the legal event it also is. Confirm the certificate date, order extra transcripts, check CHSI in July, and leave a trusted proxy with a properly worded letter. Those four steps cost an afternoon and save the months of courier traffic that follow an early flight home.
