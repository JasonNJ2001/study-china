Entrance Exams and Interviews for Self-Funded Applicants at Chinese Universities

By ORIGIN Education · 2026-11-15

Most international applicants to Chinese universities are admitted on documents alone, which is why the rumour persists that no test exists. It is only half true. A growing number of programmes, especially in medicine, art and business, now run their own written paper or interview before issuing an admission letter and the JW202 form you need for a student visa.

Who Sits an Entrance Exam and Who Does Not

Self-funded bachelor applicants are usually admitted on transcript, language certificate and a personal statement, with no test. Universities add an entrance exam or interview when a programme has more qualified applicants than places, when the subject requires demonstrated skill, or when a professional body accredits the degree. Medicine, art, design, music and some business masters are the common cases.

The rule of thumb: the more competitive the programme and the more it depends on hands-on ability, the more likely you will be tested. Language-year and foundation programmes almost never test beyond a placement exam taken after arrival.

The Document-Only Route: What Actually Gets You Admitted

On the document-only route, four items decide the outcome: a high school transcript with a passing average, proof of language ability, a study plan that names the programme and the reason for it, and a passport valid well beyond the programme's end date. Nothing else moves the decision much, and extra certificates rarely compensate for a weak transcript.

For Chinese-taught degrees the language proof is normally HSK level 4 with at least 180 points, and many universities now ask for HSK 5 for humanities and medicine. For English-taught degrees, IELTS 6.0 or TOEFL 80 is the common floor, though universities increasingly accept a school-leaving qualification taught in English.

How a University-Set Written Exam Works

A written entrance exam set by a Chinese university is usually short and subject-specific: mathematics and biology for medicine, drawing for architecture and design, and a general reasoning paper for business. It is marked by the receiving school rather than a national body, so the format varies between universities and even between years at the same one.

Papers typically run 90 to 120 minutes and are sat either on campus or at a partner institution abroad. Where a candidate cannot travel, most universities will substitute a supervised online paper or waive the test in favour of a portfolio. Ask which applies before you book a flight; the answer decides whether you need a short-stay visa.

Subject content to expect

Medicine papers test biology, chemistry and basic mathematics at the level of a strong secondary school leaver, with questions written in English. Engineering papers lean on mathematics and physics. Art and design tests are practical: a still-life drawing under time pressure, or a design brief completed in two hours.

Remote versus on-campus sittings

A supervised online paper is accepted by most universities that test, but a practical drawing or music audition usually requires attendance. If offered a choice, take the on-campus version when the trip is affordable: it doubles as a campus visit before you accept.

The Interview: Format, Language and Typical Questions

Interviews are usually 15 to 30 minutes with a panel of two or three staff, conducted online through WeChat, Feishu or Zoom, and held in English unless the programme is taught in Chinese. The panel is checking three things: whether your academic background matches the programme, whether your plan is specific, and whether you can follow a conversation in the language of instruction.

Typical questions are predictable. Why this university, why this city, what you studied last year, which part of the field interests you, how you will fund the years not covered by a scholarship, and what you will do after graduation. Answers that name courses, laboratories or supervisors perform far better than answers about China being a great country.

Handling a weak spot

If your grades dipped in one year, say what caused it and what changed. Panels are used to applicants who hide a gap year or a failed subject; a short, factual explanation plus evidence of recovery reads as maturity, while an evasive answer reads as a risk. Have one sentence ready for the weakest line on your transcript.

Timeline, Fees and the Documents That Follow

Exam and interview invitations arrive after the application is complete, so the date you submit sets everything else. A December submission typically produces a February to April test slot, an admission letter in May, and the JW202 form four to six weeks before term starts. A June submission often lands in a compressed August queue.

Fees are modest: most universities charge 200 to 800 yuan for an entrance exam or interview, and some waive it for scholarship applicants. The costly item is timing, because a late admission letter leaves little room for the visa, the medical check and the flight.

The visa clock

A programme longer than 180 days requires an X1 visa, which is converted into a residence permit within 30 days of arrival. The 180-day threshold is set by the programme length stated on your admission letter, not by the degree level, so a one-year language course also takes X1.

How to Prepare in Four Weeks

Four weeks is enough if you work from the programme's own material rather than generic test prep, and if you start the paperwork in parallel. The sequence below keeps preparation proportional to what the panel actually scores, and leaves room for the medical check, which runs on its own timetable.

  1. Request the exam syllabus or past paper from the international office in writing.
  2. Rehearse the six standard interview questions aloud, in the language of instruction.
  3. Prepare a two-minute account of your study plan that names specific courses.
  4. Collect the medical check form and police clearance before the invitation arrives.
  5. Book the visa appointment the day the admission letter is issued.

Mock interviews with a paid agent are rarely worth the fee, because the questions are public and the panel is checking sincerity rather than polish. What does help is recording yourself once and watching it back: filler phrases, unclear reasons and unanswered questions become obvious within a minute.

If you are applying to several universities with different test formats, have the admission letter and the visa timeline checked against the intake calendar before you commit to one offer.
