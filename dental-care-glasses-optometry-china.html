Dental Care, Glasses and Optometry in China for International Students

By ORIGIN Education · 2026-11-19

Dental care and eye care sit in an odd place in the Chinese health system: excellent clinical capacity, low public-hospital prices, and almost no insurance cover for either. Students arrive expecting either American prices or a queue, and find neither. What they find is a booking system, a price list and a decision about public hospital versus private clinic.

The short answer on cost and access

Public stomatology hospitals in major cities charge roughly CNY 150–300 for scaling, CNY 200–600 for a composite filling and CNY 800–2,500 for a root canal, which is a fraction of Western private prices. Access depends on booking through the hospital's WeChat account or the 114 appointment line, and waits for non-urgent treatment run from a few days to three weeks.

For foreigners, the practical difference is language rather than price. Public hospitals treat walk-in patients who speak Chinese; international clinics solve the language problem and charge three to five times more for the same procedure. Most students settle on public hospitals for routine work and a private clinic only for emergencies outside clinic hours.

Why dental is not covered by student insurance

The student tier of 城乡居民基本医疗保险, the resident social insurance scheme, costs roughly CNY 300–400 a year and covers hospitalisation and some outpatient treatment, but excludes orthodontics, implants, crowns and routine scaling. Extractions for medical reasons are sometimes reimbursable; cosmetic work never is. Budget dental care as an out-of-pocket cost from day one.

China's 2023 centralised procurement of dental implants cut public-hospital implant prices by roughly half, capping the service fee and standardising the implant system. That reform is why a single implant in a provincial stomatology hospital now costs less than half of what it did in 2021, and it is the single biggest cost change in Chinese dentistry in a decade.

How to book a dental appointment

Booking runs through three channels: the hospital's own WeChat mini-programme, the 114 telephone and app service, and in-person registration at the front desk on the day. Specialist dental hospitals release appointments about a week ahead, and popular slots for evening clinics disappear within minutes, so set a reminder rather than trying casually.

At the appointment, expect a registration fee of CNY 10–50 for a standard consultation, higher for a specialist or expert clinic. Bring your passport and student card, and ask the reception desk for the international patient window if the hospital has one, which most provincial capitals do.

What to say at the desk

Learn five phrases and the visit becomes manageable: 洗牙 for scaling, 补牙 for a filling, 拔牙 for extraction, 根管治疗 for root canal, and 疼 for pain. If you need an X-ray, the term is 拍片, and a panoramic film costs CNY 80–150. Written translation apps work well for consent forms, but not for a dentist talking through a treatment plan while you are in the chair.

Emergency dental care at night

Dental pain outside clinic hours is handled by the emergency department of a major stomatology hospital, and larger cities keep one open overnight. Expect to pay an emergency registration fee of CNY 50–100 plus treatment, and expect a wait, because emergency dental capacity is thin everywhere.

What counts as an emergency

Keep two numbers saved: the nearest stomatology hospital's emergency line, and your university's international student office, which often has a staff member on call for hospital visits. Neither replaces insurance, but both shorten a bad night.

Glasses and contact lenses

Optometry in China is fast, cheap and accurate enough for most prescriptions. A hospital eye test costs CNY 30–100 and includes refraction and, if needed, a fundus check; optical chains offer free testing when you buy frames, though the equipment and time spent are usually better in a hospital. Single-vision lenses run CNY 150–800 and frames CNY 200–1,500, with progressive lenses at CNY 800–3,000.

Turnaround is the part that surprises visitors: most shops cut and fit single-vision lenses in one to three hours, and some do it while you wait. Bring your prescription if you have one, or your current glasses for measurement, and specify the index you want, since shops default to the cheapest 1.56 lens which is thick at higher prescriptions.

Buying cheap in Danyang

Danyang in Jiangsu province produces a large share of the world's spectacle lenses, and its optical market sells frames and lenses at prices well below retail in Shanghai or Beijing. Students within reach of the Nanjing–Danyang rail line routinely make the trip for a second pair, with a full pair often costing CNY 200–400.

Contact lenses and solution

Contact lenses require a prescription in principle, and hospital eye clinics will fit and teach insertion for CNY 100–200 on top of the test. Daily disposables cost CNY 3–6 per lens online, and solution runs CNY 30–60 a bottle. Buy from a licensed optical chain or a hospital shop rather than an unverified marketplace seller, because counterfeit lenses are a real category of product in China.

Building a simple health budget

Dental and eye costs are predictable if you plan them and unpredictable if you do not, because both fields price by procedure rather than by visit. The six steps below assume a two-to-four-year stay and a student insurance policy that excludes cosmetic dentistry. Each step costs little now and prevents a four-figure bill later:

  1. Book a scaling appointment in your first semester, so you have a baseline and a dentist you trust.
  2. Get a full eye test and keep the written prescription, even if you do not buy glasses that day.
  3. Set aside CNY 1,000–2,000 a year for dental and optical costs, which is realistic for most students.
  4. Ask your university which hospital its student insurance designates, because reimbursement outside the designated network is limited.
  5. Keep receipts and diagnosis certificates, since some provincial schemes reimburse extractions with documentation.
  6. Buy travel insurance for your first month, before student insurance activates.

The system is affordable and clinically solid once you know where to go and what to ask for. The expensive mistakes are all timing errors: waiting until a tooth needs a root canal, or buying glasses in a tourist district without a prescription.
