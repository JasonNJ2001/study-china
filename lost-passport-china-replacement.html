Lost or Stolen Passport in China: Replacement, Police Report and Visa Transfer

By ORIGIN Education · 2026-11-22

A lost passport in China is a two-part problem: you need a new document, and you need the visa or residence permit printed inside the old one to stay valid. The Public Security Bureau handles the police report, your embassy issues the replacement, and the Exit-Entry Administration transfers the permit. Doing those three in the wrong order can cost you three extra weeks.

Report the loss to the police within 24 hours

A police report is the document every later step depends on, and most cities expect it within 24 hours of you noticing the loss. Go to the Public Security Bureau station nearest to where it happened, ask for the case receipt (报案回执) and a written statement of loss, and keep the originals. Your embassy and the Exit-Entry Administration will both ask for them.

Take a Chinese-speaking friend, because station officers rarely speak English and the difference between lost and stolen changes the report wording.

What the police report must contain

Check that the paper names your full passport number, the date and place of loss, and the case number. A report that says only "documents lost" without the passport number is usually rejected at the embassy counter, and returning to the station to correct it costs another day.

Stolen versus lost: why the wording matters

If the passport was taken, insist on the theft wording and ask for a copy of the case filing. Insurance claims and some embassy processes require evidence of theft rather than negligence, and a report written as "mislaid" can void a travel insurance claim worth several hundred dollars.

Replace the passport at your embassy or consulate

Your embassy, not the Chinese government, issues the replacement, and processing ranges from a same-day emergency travel document to four weeks for a full biometric passport. Book online, bring the police report, photographs, a copy of the old passport if you have one, and proof of student status. Fees are set by your own government, not by China.

Most embassies in China sit in Beijing, with consulates in Shanghai, Guangzhou, Chengdu, Shenyang and Wuhan. Students in smaller cities often must travel, so check whether your embassy accepts courier delivery of the new passport before booking a train.

Emergency travel documents versus full passports

An emergency document usually gets you home but is not always accepted for a Chinese visa or residence permit. Ask the consular officer directly whether the document they plan to issue can carry a Chinese residence permit; if the answer is no, wait for the full passport.

If your embassy is not in your city

Allow one working day for travel each way plus the appointment itself. Ask the international office for a leave letter, because some universities require approval before you leave the city during term, and keep every boarding pass for your insurance claim.

Transfer the residence permit to the new passport

A residence permit is tied to the passport number it was printed against, so a new passport leaves you legally present but administratively unmatched. Report the change to the Exit-Entry Administration and apply for a reissue, bringing the new passport, the police report, a university letter and photographs. The fee is modest, and processing takes five to ten working days.

Do not wait for a holiday or the end of term. A permit that does not match your passport can stall a bank account, a hotel check-in or a domestic flight booking, and border officers at exit can question the mismatch even when your visa is technically still valid.

Documents the Exit-Entry Administration will ask for

Bring the new passport, the old passport if recovered, the police report, a registration form from your university, a recent photograph and the accommodation registration slip from your dormitory or landlord. Missing the accommodation slip is the most common reason for a second visit.

Exit China before the new passport arrives

If your flight leaves before the replacement is ready, the Exit-Entry Administration can issue a single-entry exit visa that lets you leave legally and re-enter later on a fresh X1 or X2. Bring the police report, an embassy letter confirming the replacement application, your flight booking and a university approval letter. Processing takes about five working days in most cities.

The exit visa ends your current residence status. You cannot use it to return to China and resume study without a new visa, so do not treat it as a travel convenience for a weekend trip.

When an exit visa is the cheaper option

For a student whose course ends within weeks, or whose embassy cannot replace a passport for a month, leaving on an exit visa and reapplying from home is often faster and cheaper than waiting in China with an unmatched permit.

Costs and timeline at a glance

The full recovery takes between two days and six weeks, and almost all of that spread comes from embassy processing rather than from Chinese procedures. Prevent the next loss

Most repeat losses happen in the first month, when students carry the passport everywhere because they assume they must. In practice you need the original only for police registration, bank opening, SIM card registration and visa business. For campus access, hospital registration and hotel check-in, a phone photo of the data page is usually enough.

Scan the data page, the visa page and the residence permit, then store them in two places: a cloud folder and an email to yourself. Keep one paper photocopy in your dormitory and give another to the international office. Carry the passport in an inside pocket rather than a backpack's outer compartment, and never leave it in a checked bag or a coat at a restaurant.

What the university can and cannot do

The international students office can write your support letter, send a staff member with you to the Exit-Entry Administration and sometimes advance the fee, but it cannot certify your identity to your embassy or issue any travel document. Ask them on day one regardless: they handle this several times a year and know which office has the shortest queue.

Ask for three documents, because officers verify rather than listen:

  1. A stamped enrolment certificate from the international office.
  2. The accommodation registration slip issued by your dormitory or landlord.
  3. A named staff contact an officer can call during office hours.
