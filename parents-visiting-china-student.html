Parents Visiting You in China: L Visa, Hotels and Campus Rules

By ORIGIN Education · 2026-11-22

A parent's visit to a student in China goes smoothly or badly depending on three things decided before they fly: the visa category, the address they will sleep at, and whether anyone explained the registration rules. Get those right and the trip is a holiday. Get them wrong and a two-week visit can end at a police station counter on day one.

Your parents need an L visa, not the Q2

Parents visiting a foreign student in China apply for an L tourist visa, because the Q2 family-visit visa exists only for relatives of Chinese citizens and permanent residents. The L visa is normally issued for 30, 60 or 90 days per entry with validity of three months to ten years depending on nationality, and it cannot be converted into a residence permit inside China.

The common mistake is assuming a family relationship creates an entitlement. It does not: your parents are tourists in the eyes of Chinese immigration, and they must satisfy the same conditions as any tourist, including an itinerary and a place to stay.

How long they can actually stay

The number of days per entry is printed on the visa and is not the same as its validity. A ten-year L visa with 60 days per entry allows 60 days in China per visit, and overstaying by even one day triggers a fine of CNY 500 per day up to CNY 10,000.

The invitation pack to send them

Chinese embassies ask for an invitation letter, a copy of your passport and residence permit, proof of student status, and the visitor's own flight and hotel bookings. Send one PDF with everything in order, because consulates reject incomplete applications rather than asking for missing documents.

Include these five items, and check the embassy's own checklist for your parents' nationality before sending:

  1. An invitation letter signed by you, naming your address, phone number and relationship.
  2. Copies of your passport data page, visa page and residence permit.
  3. An enrolment certificate stamped by the international students office.
  4. Their return ticket and hotel booking for at least the first three nights.
  5. Proof of the family relationship, such as a birth certificate with a translation.

Give the invitation letter a real address. A dormitory room number is acceptable, but a vague campus address invites a request for more documents.

The 240-hour transit option and its limits

Since 17 December 2024, citizens of 55 countries can transit China visa-free for up to 240 hours through 60 approved ports, which covers a ten-day visit if they arrive and depart on international tickets. The limits are strict: they must stay inside the permitted region, cannot work or study, and must exit through an approved port.

This route suits a short first visit, not a two-week tour. The permitted area is usually the province or municipality of entry plus a defined group of neighbouring provinces, so a plan that includes Beijing and Shanghai on the same transit may not qualify. Confirm the port and the region before booking.

Registration: the 24-hour rule

Every foreign visitor must be registered with the local police within 24 hours of arrival, and a hotel does this automatically when it scans the passport at check-in. Parents staying in your dormitory or a private flat must register at the police station themselves, bringing the landlord's details and the guest's passport.

Skipping registration is not a formality. Fines run to CNY 2,000 for the host and the guest, and an unregistered stay can complicate a future visa application. If your parents arrive late at night, register the next morning and keep the receipt.

When the hotel does it for you

Any licensed hotel, including budget chains, registers guests automatically. Keep the registration slip the hotel prints: police can ask for it during spot checks, and it is useful evidence if a visa extension is ever requested.

Campus access and dormitory rules

Most Chinese universities let visitors through the gate with a passport and a student escort but forbid overnight guests in dormitories, and enforcement tightened after 2020. Book a hotel within walking distance instead: campus-adjacent hotels in provincial capitals typically cost CNY 200 to 450 a night, and reception handles the police registration for you.

Do not plan on sneaking a parent into a dormitory. Dormitory staff log visitors, roommates report them, and the penalty falls on the student's record rather than the guest's, which is a bad trade for one night's saving.

Getting a visitor pass

Some campuses require a visitor QR code obtained through the university's app or WeChat account, usually sponsored by the student. Register the visit a day ahead, and carry the parent's passport, because gate scanners read the document rather than the face.

What a two-week visit costs

A two-week visit for two parents typically lands between CNY 12,000 and CNY 25,000 excluding international airfare, and accommodation is the variable that moves most. The estimates below assume a provincial capital in autumn, two people, and ordinary restaurant meals rather than hotel dining.

Two costs surprise visitors: attraction tickets for foreigners are priced the same as for Chinese visitors but require passport registration, and museum entry is free but needs a timed reservation made days in advance.

Health care and insurance for older visitors

Chinese hospitals require payment at the time of treatment for foreign patients, and a single emergency visit with imaging can run CNY 2,000 to 5,000 before any admission. Buy travel insurance with medical evacuation cover before departure, and declare pre-existing conditions, because most policies exclude them silently.

Teach your parents two sentences in Chinese before they arrive: 我需要看医生 (I need to see a doctor) and 最近的医院在哪里 (where is the nearest hospital). Save your phone number, your university's emergency line and your insurance policy number in their phone's lock screen notes.

Prescriptions and medication

Bring a supply of regular medication in original packaging with a copy of the prescription. Some common drugs are controlled in China, and a customs officer who finds loose pills without documentation will treat them as unregistered medicine.

Best months to visit

Late September to early November and April to May give the best weather and the fewest clashes with the academic calendar, while the first week of October and the six weeks around Chinese New Year are the worst. Avoid exam periods, and check the calendar for the two-week military training block that occupies most first-year students in September.

If the visit must coincide with a holiday, book trains and hotels four to six weeks ahead. Chunyun, the 40-day travel rush around Chinese New Year, sells out high-speed rail seats on popular routes within minutes of release.
