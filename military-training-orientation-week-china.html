Military Training and Orientation Week in China

By ORIGIN Education · 2026-11-27

Every September, Chinese campuses fill with students in camouflage uniforms standing in formation before breakfast. This is military training, and it is the single most visible difference between starting university in China and starting anywhere else. For international students the experience ranges from full participation to complete exemption with a substitute course. This guide explains what the military training china university requirement involves and how to find out which version applies to you.

Military training is a two-credit course, not a summer camp

Military training is formal coursework governed by a national syllabus, worth two credits and delivered over two to three weeks before the teaching semester begins. The current framework comes from the 2019 syllabus issued by the Ministry of Education together with the Central Military Commission's national defence mobilisation department, which sets military theory at 36 classroom hours and military skills at no fewer than 112 hours.

The two halves of the course

The classroom half, military theory, covers national defence policy, the history of the armed forces and modern warfare, and is examined like any other subject. The outdoor half is the part everyone photographs: drill, marching, formation, and living routines run by serving soldiers or by students who trained the previous year. Both halves must be passed for the credits.

How long it lasts and what a day looks like

Training typically runs 14 to 21 days, with a flag-raising ceremony on the first morning and a parade review on the last. Days start around 6:00 with a run or standing drill, pause for breakfast at 7:30, resume at 8:30 for marching practice, break for lunch and a rest period, and continue until late afternoon, with evening singing practice in the first week.

Standing drill, marching and the rest

Standing at attention for 20 to 40 minutes at a time is the core exercise, and it is harder than it looks in September heat. Marching in step, about-turns and saluting follow, then squad-level competition. Evenings are given to marching songs, which are learned by volume rather than by ear, and to inspections of dormitory tidiness where bedding is folded into a block.

Are international students required to attend?

In most universities international students are exempt from outdoor military training and are placed in an orientation programme instead, but the policy is set locally and a minority of institutions expect light participation such as formation drills or a closing parade. There is no national rule that exempts foreign nationals, so the only reliable answer comes from your own international office in writing.

How to confirm your status

Ask the international students office during registration, in person, and request the answer by email so you have a record. The question to ask is specific: whether military skills training is waived for your cohort, and what course replaces the credits. Offices answer this daily and a written reply settles the matter in a way that a rumour from a senior student does not.

Orientation week: the part that actually matters

Orientation week, usually the seven to ten days before classes, is where the practical business of being a student in China gets done: residence registration, the residence permit application, health checks, campus card collection, library activation and a briefing on academic rules. Skipping it to travel is the most common first-month mistake, because the residence permit window is legally fixed at 30 days.

The sessions worth attending

  • The residence permit briefing, which explains the 30-day filing deadline after entry.
  • The academic regulations session, covering grading, attendance and academic integrity.
  • The campus safety and fire drill, which is compulsory at most institutions.
  • The library and network session, where you activate the account you need for everything else.
  • The college meeting with your adviser, where course selection is confirmed.

Attendance, discipline and what counts as a refusal

Training is graded on attendance and conduct, and absence without an approved reason is recorded by the squad leader rather than by the academic office. Refusing an instruction is treated as a discipline matter at some institutions even when the student is exempt from the physical component. If you are unwell, report it to the instructor and the clinic the same morning rather than simply not appearing.

Medical exemptions and how they are granted

Exemption on health grounds needs a hospital certificate describing a condition that makes strenuous activity unsafe, such as a cardiac condition, severe asthma or a recent fracture. The certificate is reviewed by the campus clinic and then by the college. Students placed in this category usually join the indoor theory sessions and are excused from drill, and the record is kept with the student file.

What to bring and how to prepare

Bring two pairs of dark sports socks, a wide-brimmed hat if permitted, high-factor sunscreen and a large water bottle. Most uniforms are issued by the university and are thin, so a cotton undershirt helps. Break in your shoes a week beforehand, since blisters cause more absences than heat does. Keep a small towel in your pocket for the morning session.

Heat, hydration and the realistic risks

September in Nanjing, Wuhan or Guangzhou regularly exceeds 33 degrees Celsius, and most sessions run outdoors regardless. Drink water before you feel thirsty, eat breakfast even at 6:00, and tell the instructor immediately if you feel dizzy. Campus clinics see a predictable wave of heat exhaustion cases in the first three days, almost all of them students who skipped breakfast.

If training is waived, what replaces it

Where the outdoor component is waived, universities typically substitute a Chinese society and culture course, a campus service assignment, or additional language hours, and the two credits still have to be earned. The substitute is assessed by attendance and a short written report. Confirm the substitute in writing as well, because a missing credit surfaces at graduation rather than in the first term.

The training period is short, uncomfortable and socially useful: it is where classmates learn each other's names before the academic pressure starts. Treat the paperwork as seriously as the drill, keep every certificate, and confirm your exemption or participation in writing during orientation week. Doing that in the first fortnight is what keeps a two-week inconvenience from turning into a graduation query.
