Renting Off-Campus in China: Deposits, Utilities and Moving Out

By ORIGIN Education · 2026-11-11

Renting outside campus in China is cheaper than a dormitory in some cities and far more expensive in others, and the difference is rarely the headline rent. It is the deposit structure, the agency fee, the utility tariff and the exit inspection, each of which can add or subtract a month's rent.

This guide walks through what a Chinese lease actually commits you to, what the utilities cost, and how to leave with your deposit intact.

What one deposit and three months means in practice

The standard Chinese lease is 押一付三: one month's rent held as a refundable deposit, plus three months' rent paid in advance and renewed quarterly. Shorter stays or cheaper cities often use 押一付一. A landlord demanding more than three months up front on a one-year lease is a warning sign, not a discount.

Deposit sizes by city

In Beijing, Shanghai and Shenzhen, a one-bedroom near a major university runs 4,000 to 7,000 RMB a month, so the deposit alone is a serious sum. In Wuhan, Chengdu, Xi'an or Nanjing the same flat is 1,800 to 3,500 RMB, and 押一付一 is common. Ask for the deposit to be written into the contract as a number, not a phrase, because "one month" is disputed when rent changes mid-term.

Cash versus transfer

Pay by bank transfer or Alipay with a note stating the purpose, never in cash. Chinese small-claims courts accept transfer records as evidence, and a landlord who insists on cash is removing your only proof of payment.

Agency fees and who actually pays

Agency commission in most Chinese cities is 35 per cent of one month's rent, paid by the tenant at signing, and it is negotiable in a soft market. Landlords advertising directly on listing platforms usually still route through an agent, so budget the fee even when the advert says 房东直租.

The fee buys a contract template, a background check on the landlord, and someone to call when the water heater fails. It does not buy legal representation. Read the contract yourself, and if it is more than three pages of Chinese, pay a translator 100 to 200 RMB to read it with you before you sign.

Utilities: residential versus commercial tariffs

Chinese utility pricing splits into residential (民水民电) and commercial (商水商电) tariffs, and the gap is roughly double. Residential electricity runs about 0.5 to 0.6 RMB per kilowatt-hour and water about 3.5 to 5 RMB per cubic metre; commercial electricity is closer to 1.0 to 1.2 RMB per kilowatt-hour. Serviced apartments and converted office buildings bill at the commercial rate.

Reading the meter at handover

Photograph the electricity and water meters on the day you get the keys, with the reading legible, and send the photos to the landlord in a message. Chinese flats usually use prepaid electricity: you buy credit through a WeChat mini-program or at a convenience store, and the flat goes dark when the balance hits zero. A summer with air conditioning can consume 300 to 500 RMB of electricity a month in a poorly insulated flat.

Internet and gas

Broadband is separate and costs 60 to 120 RMB a month for 100 to 300 Mbps, installed by China Telecom, China Unicom or China Mobile with a one-year contract and a passport. Piped gas is metered and billed quarterly; many older flats use induction hobs instead, which shifts the cost onto electricity.

The 24-hour police registration rule

Under China's Exit and Entry Administration Law, a foreigner staying in private accommodation must register at the local public security station within 24 hours of moving in, and the landlord is legally required to assist. Hotels and university dormitories file this automatically; a rented flat does not, and the fine for skipping it falls on the tenant.

Take your passport, visa or residence permit, the lease, and the landlord's ID and property certificate. The station issues a registration slip, which you will need again when you renew your residence permit or open a bank account.

What to bring to the station

  • Passport with a valid visa or residence permit
  • Signed lease showing the full address in Chinese
  • Copy of the landlord's ID card and property ownership certificate
  • A local phone number the officer can reach you on

Go within the first day, ideally with the landlord or a Chinese-speaking friend, and keep the slip with your passport for the rest of the tenancy.

Contract clauses that cost students money

Three clauses cause most disputes: early termination penalties, the definition of normal wear, and the repair threshold. A standard Chinese lease charges one month's rent for leaving before the term ends, and many add a per-incident deductible of 100 to 300 RMB that tenants discover only at move-out.

The inventory list

Ask for an inventory list as an appendix, and add anything missing before signing: appliance model numbers, furniture condition, existing stains and scratches. A lease without an inventory defaults to the landlord's memory of the flat, which is never favourable.

Subletting and guests

Most Chinese leases prohibit subletting outright, and some restrict overnight guests. If you plan to share the flat with a classmate, put both names on the contract; a verbal arrangement leaves the second tenant with no legal standing and no route to the deposit.

Moving out and getting the deposit back

Deposit return is settled at a joint inspection, usually within three to seven days of handover, and paid by bank transfer rather than cash. Photographs of every wall, appliance and meter reading from the day you moved in are the single most effective protection, because deductions are argued against the inventory, not against memory.

Give notice in writing 30 days before the end date, as most contracts require, and book the inspection for a weekday morning when the landlord is likely to attend. If a deduction is claimed, ask for the receipt for the repair; without one, the deduction is negotiable and often withdrawn.
