Graduation Requirements at Chinese Universities: Credits, Thesis and Defence

By ORIGIN Education · 2026-11-13

Graduation from a Chinese university is decided by credit count, a thesis and a public defence, not by attendance alone. A bachelor's degree takes 140–170 credits across four years, a master's 30–40 credits plus a thesis defended before three to five examiners, and a doctorate usually two indexed publications before the defence can even be scheduled. Each of those numbers is set by the institution, not by national law.

The Credit Requirement

Credit loads are set by the university rather than a national curriculum, so the total differs by institution and programme. A four-year bachelor's typically requires 140–170 credits, a master's 30–40 with a research-methods component, and a doctorate 12–20 taught credits followed by research. Students who transfer in or switch major mid-degree negotiate a conversion, and that negotiation is where credit shortfalls begin.

Where the credits actually sit

Roughly a quarter of a Chinese bachelor's degree is general education: 8–12 credits of Chinese language, 4–8 of physical education, and mandatory courses on Chinese culture or modern Chinese history. These are not optional for degree-seeking international students at most universities, and they are the courses that most often delay graduation, because they are scheduled in year one and repeated annually rather than offered in a catch-up block.

The Language Requirement Is a Graduation Requirement

Chinese-taught degrees require HSK 5 for humanities and business programmes and HSK 4 for science, engineering and medicine before the degree is conferred, and a growing number of English-taught programmes impose HSK 3 as a graduation condition. This sits apart from admission: you can enter on an English-taught track with no Chinese at all and still be blocked at graduation for want of the certificate.

The difference between 180 and 210

HSK 4 requires a total of 180 out of 300 across listening, reading and writing, while HSK 5 requires 180 out of 300 but with a much wider vocabulary list. Universities that ask for "HSK 4" on admission and "HSK 5" at graduation are not being inconsistent; they expect the language to improve during the degree, and the final certificate is checked at the point of degree conferral, not enrolment.

Thesis and Defence

Nothing about a Chinese thesis is informal. A master's thesis runs 30,000–50,000 Chinese characters, passes a similarity check before submission, and is defended orally before a committee of three to five faculty including at least one external examiner. A doctorate requires five to seven examiners, an external review stage, and a public defence that any faculty member may attend.

The similarity check

CNKI (中国知网) is the plagiarism system nearly every university uses. Thresholds run from 10% to 20% depending on the institution, quotations and references are normally excluded from the count, and a failed check means revision and a re-check fee rather than instant dismissal. Machine translation of an English source is detected reliably enough that it is not a shortcut.

Publication before defence

For doctoral candidates, many universities require one or two papers published or formally accepted in journals indexed by SCI, EI or CSSCI before the defence can be scheduled, with the student as first author and the university as first affiliation. The rule lives in the programme handbook rather than in national law, and it delays more PhD graduations than any other single requirement.

What Happens If You Fail

Failing a defence is not expulsion. Most universities allow a revision and a second defence within six to twelve months, and the residence permit is extended on the strength of a university letter. A second failure, or exceeding the maximum study period, converts the outcome into a certificate of completion rather than a degree, which is a different document and a different visa consequence.

  1. A credit shortfall from skipped physical education or politics courses, discovered in the final semester.
  2. No HSK certificate at the level the programme requires for conferral.
  3. Similarity above the institutional threshold and no time left to revise.
  4. A supervisor unavailable or transferred in the student's final semester, leaving no one to sign the submission.
  5. Defence scheduled after the university's degree-conferral meeting, which pushes the award to the next cycle.

The Degree Law change

The Degree Law of the People's Republic of China took effect on 1 January 2025, replacing the 1980 Regulations on Academic Degrees. It keeps the three-level structure of bachelor's, master's and doctoral degrees but gives universities more authority to set their own standards, which in practice means the programme handbook outweighs any national minimum when you are arguing about a requirement.

Getting the Degree Registered

The certificate handed over at the ceremony is not the end of the process. Degrees are registered in CHSI (学信网), the national student information database, and employers and other universities verify them there. Ask your international office when the registration is uploaded, because it can lag the ceremony by several weeks, and a verification report is what a foreign employer or a visa officer will actually accept.

Two practical checks are worth doing in your final year. First, request a transcript with the credit total printed on it before you leave, since chasing it from abroad takes months. Second, confirm in writing that your HSK certificate has been lodged with the registrar, because the certificate is a separate document from the degree and is not always filed automatically by the language centre.

China university graduation requirements are published in the programme handbook, and the handbook is revised between cohorts. Read the version issued to your intake, not the one a friend from two years earlier still has on their laptop.
