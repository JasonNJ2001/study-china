How to Read Your Chinese University Admission Letter and JW202 Form

By ORIGIN Education · 2026-11-06

The JW202 form is the visa-application document issued by the Chinese Ministry of Education system on the university's request, and it is what the embassy reads to decide your visa type and its duration. The admission letter is the university's offer and carries no visa weight on its own. You need both, they must match, and one field on the JW202 outranks everything else.

What the two documents do

An admission letter states that a university has accepted you, on what programme, at what tuition, and from which registration date. The JW202 form is a government filing that authorises a student visa, generated in the provincial education department's system and printed with a serial number and barcode. Embassies accept the letter as supporting evidence and the form as the trigger.

Why one is not a substitute for the other

Applicants regularly arrive at a visa centre with a signed offer and no form, and are turned away at the document check. The reverse also fails: a form without an admission letter leaves the visa officer unable to confirm the programme. Universities issue them together, usually within two to four weeks of a deposit or acceptance confirmation, and the form cannot be downloaded or self-printed.

The JW201 and JW202 difference

There are two versions, and which one you hold tells the visa officer how you are funded. JW201 is issued to Chinese Government Scholarship holders, whose tuition and stipend come from the state, and JW202 is issued to self-funded students, provincial scholarship holders and university-funded students. The visa outcome is the same X1 in both cases; the funding line changes what the university asks you to pay at registration.

Where the serial number leads

The serial number on the form is a record in the Ministry of Education's international student system, which is why universities cannot simply reprint it. Corrections are filed as an amendment through the same provincial system, and the old form is voided. This is the mechanism behind the two-to-four-week delay that ruins late-August travel plans.

The fields that cause rejections

Name spelling, passport number and date of birth are checked character by character against the passport, and any difference is treated as a fraudulent or incorrect document rather than a typo. The other frequent failure is the study duration: a form stating one year for a four-year programme produces a residence permit that expires while the student is still enrolled.

Fix errors before you leave home

Ask the admissions office for a scanned copy of the completed form before it is couriered, and compare five items against your passport: surname, given names, sex, date of birth, passport number. Catching a mismatch at that stage costs a week. Catching it at the visa centre costs the intake, because the September registration date does not move for paperwork.

Reading the registration window

The admission letter gives a registration date or a two-to-three-day window, and the JW202 records an intended arrival date that should fall inside it. Arriving early is usually tolerated because dormitories open a few days before; arriving late requires written approval from the international office, and unapproved late arrival can void the place.

  • Check the registration window against your flight before booking
  • Confirm the dormitory opening date, which is often earlier than registration
  • Tell the international office your arrival time if you land after 22:00
  • Keep the original JW202 in hand luggage, not checked baggage
  • Carry six passport photographs in the Chinese size, 33 by 48 millimetres

What happens at the border

The X1 visa in your passport is a single-entry visa valid for 30 days from arrival, and the border officer stamps the entry date that starts the clock. Within those 30 days you must complete the health check, register with the local police station and apply for a residence permit at the exit-entry administration. The JW202 is required at that appointment, so it stays with you, not in a suitcase.

The 30-day clock and the permit

The X1 visa is not your residence document, and treating it as one is the most expensive mistake in the file. A residence permit must be applied for at the exit-entry bureau within 30 days of entry, using the admission letter, the JW202, the health certificate and the police registration slip. Missing the window means a fine of RMB 500 a day up to RMB 10,000, or detention and deportation in serious cases.

Where the health check fits

The Foreigner Physical Examination Form completed at home is valid for six months, and students arriving with a complete form can usually convert it at a Chinese health centre for a verification fee. Students without one pay RMB 400 to 800 for a full examination in China, and results take three to five working days, which is time taken out of the 30-day window.

A checklist before you book the flight

Two documents, one window, and a 30-day clock after landing. The letter tells you when to arrive, the form tells the embassy what to issue, and the permit appointment closes the loop. Verifying the spelling on the form and the dates on the letter takes ten minutes and prevents the two failures that cost an intake. Applicants who want the document set reviewed against the programme before filing can have it checked line by line.
