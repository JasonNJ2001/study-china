Applying to Chinese Universities with A-Levels, IB or AP: Grade Requirements and Credit

By ORIGIN Education · 2026-11-06

A-level requirements at a Chinese university are set per programme, and the realistic band is CCC at provincial universities and AAB to AAA at the top ten. IB offers run from 24 points for a conditional place to 40 for Peking and Tsinghua, while AP applicants usually submit three to five subjects at grade 4 or 5. Grades alone do not decide the outcome: Chinese-taught programmes add an HSK threshold.

The grade bands by university tier

Universities in China publish international undergraduate requirements in their own prospectuses, and the bands are wide because admission is not centralised. The top tier asks for three A-Levels at AAB or above, the middle tier BBB to BCC, and provincial universities accept CCC with a foundation year. IB mirrors this at 36 to 40 points for the top, 30 to 34 in the middle, and 24 as the diploma floor.

What the top tier actually asks for

Peking, Tsinghua, Fudan and Shanghai Jiao Tong expect three A-Levels with at least one A*, plus an entrance examination or interview held between March and April. Tsinghua's international undergraduate route uses its own written test in mathematics and the chosen subject area, sat in person or at a partner centre. A strong IB score of 40 or above can replace the written test at some faculties but not at all of them.

Where the middle tier sits

Provincial key universities and the science-focused institutions accept BBB to BCC, and many will convert an IB score of 30 directly. These are the programmes where a student with a mixed A-Level profile, including a resit, is competitive. Their September intake deadlines usually fall between March and May, later than the top tier's December to February window.

IB and AP conversion in practice

IB Diploma holders are assessed on the total out of 45, with higher level subjects weighted for programme fit, and a 24-point pass is the minimum most universities will consider. AP applicants submit subject scores rather than a diploma, and three subjects at grade 4 satisfies most programmes while engineering asks for Calculus BC at grade 5.

The subject-mix rules nobody publishes

Engineering and medicine programmes specify required subjects: mathematics and physics for engineering, chemistry and biology for clinical medicine. A student with three essay-based A-Levels cannot be admitted to an engineering programme regardless of grades, and the refusal arrives without explanation. Check the subject list before shortlisting, because it eliminates more applicants than the grade band does.

The language requirement sits on top

A-Level, IB and AP qualifications waive the English test at most universities, but they do not waive Chinese. Programmes taught in Chinese require HSK 4 with 180 of 300 for science and engineering, and HSK 5 with 180 of 300 for humanities, economics and law. That is roughly two years of study from zero, and it is the single most common reason a well-qualified applicant loses a year.

  • HSK 4 at 180 of 300 is the floor for Chinese-taught science and engineering
  • HSK 5 at 180 of 300 is the floor for Chinese-taught humanities and law
  • English-taught programmes need no HSK, but the choice of universities shrinks sharply
  • A one-year language course at the target university counts towards the HSK route
  • IB Chinese B at higher level is accepted in place of HSK at some universities, not all

Timing the results against the intake

Cambridge International and Pearson Edexcel release A-Level results in mid-August, IB releases the May session on 5 July, and AP scores appear in early July. Chinese teaching starts in the first week of September, which leaves three to seven weeks to convert a conditional offer into a JW202 and a visa. Offers that depend on August results routinely slip to the following September.

Credit for prior study

Credit exemption exists, but it is discretionary and awarded after arrival rather than at admission. A student with A-Level Mathematics and Physics at grade A can usually be exempted from first-year calculus and mechanics, shortening a four-year degree by one semester. IB higher level subjects are treated the same way, and the decision is made by the faculty office during registration week.

How to ask for it

Submit the syllabus, not just the certificate, because faculties compare content hour by hour. Applications are filed in the first two weeks of term, and late requests are refused. A successful exemption removes tuition for the exempted credits, so the saving on a semester of a RMB 26,000 programme is roughly RMB 13,000.

Documents and the application file

The file is built around notarised copies, and Chinese universities do not accept a school-issued PDF in place of a notarised certificate. Predicted grades are accepted for a conditional offer but the final certificate must arrive before the JW202 is issued, which is the step that blocks most August results.

  • Notarised A-Level, IB or AP certificates, plus transcripts for each subject
  • Passport valid for at least six months beyond the intended stay
  • Personal statement of 500 to 800 words, in English for English-taught programmes
  • Two academic references on school letterhead
  • HSK certificate where the programme is taught in Chinese
  • Application fee, usually RMB 400 to 800 per university, non-refundable

Matching the qualification to the right route

Three A-Levels at ABB with no Chinese points to an English-taught engineering programme, and 36 IB points with HSK 4 opens the Chinese-taught humanities route at a stronger university than an English-taught programme at the same institution. The qualification is not the constraint; the language and subject mix decide the shortlist. Applicants comparing programmes on that basis should check entry requirements one by one before committing to a cycle.
