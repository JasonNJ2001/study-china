Proof of Funds for a Chinese Student Visa: How Much to Show

By ORIGIN Education · 2026-11-05

There is no published minimum balance for a Chinese student visa. The figure comes from the university: to issue the JW202 form that your visa application depends on, it will ask you to demonstrate one year of tuition plus living costs, and it will usually ask for a deposit as well. Embassies check that the file is coherent, not that it hits a number.

How much you actually need to show

Budget one academic year at the rate the university publishes, then add living costs for the city. Tuition at a public university runs RMB 20,000 to 30,000 for most humanities and business degrees and RMB 30,000 to 45,000 for medicine and engineering; living costs add RMB 2,000 to 5,000 a month depending on the city. Total first-year proof therefore lands between RMB 55,000 and RMB 140,000 for most applicants.

The upper end assumes a dormitory room rather than a private flat, and it assumes you cook some meals. Renting off campus in Shanghai or Beijing can add RMB 2,500 to 5,000 a month on its own, and a university that expects you to live off campus will raise the required figure accordingly.

Why the university sets the number, not the embassy

The X1 visa application requires a JW202 form, and only the university can issue one. Its international office applies an internal financial test before releasing the form, because it carries liability if a student arrives unable to pay. That test, published on the admissions page or buried in the offer letter, is the number your bank statement has to beat.

What the JW202 form is

The JW202 is the Visa Application Form for Study in China, issued through the provincial education department and tied to one institution, one programme and one intake. It carries your name, passport number, programme length and the university's seal, and the visa officer reads it as the authoritative statement of what you have been admitted to. A JW202 for a one-year language course produces an X1 visa; the same applicant with a four-month course gets X2.

What counts as acceptable proof

A bank statement in your name or a sponsor's name, printed on bank letterhead, stamped, and covering six months of activity is the standard document. Universities accept savings accounts, fixed deposits and, less often, investment accounts. They rarely accept cash, property valuations, undeveloped land or a business balance sheet without an audited statement, because none of these can be liquidated in time to pay a tuition invoice.

  • A stamped six-month bank statement, in English or with a certified translation
  • A sponsorship letter signed by the account holder, stating the relationship and the amount
  • Proof of the sponsor's income, such as payslips or a tax return, if the balance appeared recently
  • The university's own financial guarantee form, if it provides one
  • A scholarship award letter, which replaces the whole file for CSC and Confucius Institute students

The deposit that is not tuition

Most universities require a non-refundable or partially refundable deposit before issuing the JW202, typically RMB 3,000 to 10,000 or the first semester's tuition. This is separate from the proof of funds and is usually deducted from your first invoice. Wire it from an account in your own name where possible, and keep the SWIFT confirmation, because reconciliation delays are the most common reason a JW202 arrives late.

The six-month rule and how to prepare for it

Visa officers and university finance offices both look at the pattern of a statement, not just the closing balance. A large deposit that appears three weeks before the application, with no salary or sale behind it, reads as borrowed money and is the single most common reason a financial file is questioned. Funds should sit in the account for at least three months, ideally six.

If the money is genuinely new

Document the source. A property sale produces a contract and a transfer receipt; a bonus produces a payslip; a gift from a relative produces a signed letter and the relative's statement. Attach the evidence to the bank statement rather than waiting to be asked, since a query at the visa counter costs a two-week delay and a second appointment.

Scholarship students and the exemption

A full CSC scholarship, a Confucius Institute scholarship or a university presidential scholarship removes the financial test, because the award letter states that tuition and stipend are covered. Partial awards do not: if the scholarship covers tuition but not accommodation, the university will still ask for living costs, usually at the published dormitory rate plus a monthly allowance.

What a partial award still requires

Read the award letter for the words tuition, accommodation, stipend and medical insurance, and note which are missing. A tuition-only award at a Beijing university still requires roughly RMB 45,000 to 60,000 of demonstrated living costs for the first year, and the same six-month statement rule applies.

Timing: when the money has to be in place

Work backwards from the intake. For a September start, applications close between January and May, the JW202 is issued four to eight weeks after the deposit clears, and the visa application follows immediately. The bank statement should therefore be finalised in the December or January before you apply, which is also when exchange rates tend to be least favourable for no particular reason.

A realistic calendar

Run the calendar in this order: confirm the tuition figure in writing, open or consolidate the account, let the funds season for three to six months, request the stamped statement, pay the deposit, receive the JW202, then book the visa appointment. Compressing this into four weeks is possible but leaves no room for a single document error, and document errors are what most refusals consist of.
