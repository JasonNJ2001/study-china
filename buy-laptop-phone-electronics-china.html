Buying a Laptop, Phone and Electronics in China: Prices, Warranty and Tax Refund

By ORIGIN Education · 2026-11-18

Buying electronics in China is a settled question once you know three numbers: the 13 percent value-added tax built into every listed price, the seven-day and fifteen-day windows in the three guarantees regulation, and the 11 percent departure refund available on goods you take home. Everything else is choosing the right shop.

Is China Actually Cheaper for Electronics?

Usually not for imported brands. Mainland prices include 13 percent VAT, and an iPhone or a MacBook typically costs the same or slightly more than in the United States and noticeably more than in Hong Kong, because the tax is not offset by lower pre-tax pricing. Domestic brands are the exception: Xiaomi, Lenovo, Honor and Huawei undercut equivalent imported models by 20 to 40 percent.

Where the real savings are

Accessories, monitors, mechanical keyboards, cables, power banks and mid-range Android phones are genuinely cheaper, because they are made here and the retail margin is thin. Gaming laptops from Chinese brands also price well below the same specification sold in Europe.

What is not worth buying here

High-end imported laptops, professional camera bodies and anything you may need serviced in your home country. Cross-border warranty is limited, and a repair in Europe on a China-market machine often means waiting for a part.

Where to Buy Safely

Three channels cover almost everything: JD.com's self-operated listings (京东自营), Tmall brand flagship stores (天猫旗舰店), and official offline stores in major malls. Market halls such as Huaqiangbei in Shenzhen sell real stock and refurbished stock side by side, and telling them apart requires experience you probably do not have yet.

The channel test

  1. Buy only listings marked 自营 or 官方旗舰店, never a third-party seller on the same page.
  2. Check the 保修 field on the listing, which states whether it is national warranty (全国联保) or store-only.
  3. Pay inside the platform, never by direct transfer to a seller's personal account.
  4. Keep the electronic invoice (电子发票); without it, the warranty counter will refuse service.

Offline still matters for one thing

Try the keyboard, screen and weight in person, then buy the exact model online. Chinese retail staff work on commission and will steer you toward whatever is in stock.

Warranty and the Three Guarantees

The 三包 regulation, formally the Provisions on the Repair, Replacement and Return of Consumer Goods, gives every buyer a seven-day return window, a fifteen-day replacement window and a repair obligation during the warranty period, with the seller, not the manufacturer, as the responsible party.

Laptop or desktop7 days15 days1-3 years by brand
Mobile phone7 days15 days1 year

The invoice is the contract

The seven-day clock starts from the invoice date, not the delivery date, and a return requires the original packaging. Photograph the invoice and store it in cloud storage, because thermal receipts fade within months.

Repair centres are brand-run

Authorised service centres (售后服务中心) are run by the brand, are listed in the brand's mini-program, and require an appointment. Walk-in repairs at independent shops void the warranty on the spot.

Phone Compatibility and the 国行 Question

A mainland-market phone (国行) has no Google services, does not support eSIM for a local number, and ships with Chinese-language defaults and a domestic app store. A Hong Kong or international model works on Chinese 4G and 5G bands, keeps Google services, and usually accepts a physical SIM from China Mobile, Unicom or Telecom without modification.

What breaks and what does not

Banking apps, WeChat Pay, Alipay, Didi, Meituan and campus apps all work on either version. What breaks on a 国行 phone is your Gmail, Google Maps, Play Store and any app purchased abroad. What breaks on a foreign phone is warranty service inside China.

The practical recommendation

Most long-stay students buy the phone at home and the laptop in China, or buy both at home and use a Chinese SIM in a spare handset. Dual-SIM support is worth checking, because you will want your home number alive for banking codes.

Tax Refund Before You Leave

Overseas visitors can reclaim the 11 percent VAT on goods bought at designated tax-refund shops (退税商店), provided the purchase is at least 500 RMB in one store on one day and the goods leave China within 90 days of purchase, unused and in packaging.

How the refund actually runs

Ask for the tax refund application form and the invoice at the till, keep the goods accessible, and process the claim at the airport refund desk before checking in luggage. The refund arrives on a card or in cash, minus a handling fee, and the desk will inspect the goods.

What is excluded

Food, medicines, tobacco, alcohol and anything consumed in China are outside the scheme. Consumables and opened electronics are refused, so do not unbox the laptop you intend to claim on.

Voltage, Plugs and Power

China runs 220 volts at 50 hertz, with Type A, Type C and Type I sockets, so a laptop charger rated 100-240 volts needs only a plug adapter, while a single-voltage appliance from North America needs a transformer. Power strips with universal sockets are sold everywhere for about 40 RMB.

What to bring from home

One quality multi-plug adapter, one power strip with a surge protector, and a spare charging cable. Everything else is cheaper here, and a strip bought locally will have Chinese sockets that fit your adapter but not your home plug.

Second-Hand Buying and Selling On

The second-hand market runs through Xianyu (闲鱼), the resale platform inside Alipay, where prices for a two-year-old laptop run 40 to 55 percent of new. Selling before you leave is straightforward, but buyers expect an in-person handover, a working battery report and the original invoice.

The risk to price in

Refurbished devices sold as new, batteries replaced with third-party cells, and machines with a foreign warranty that cannot be serviced in China. Meet in a public place, test the battery health report on the spot, and never pay a deposit before seeing the device.

Students who plan the purchase around warranty rather than price usually come out ahead, because the repair you cannot get is more expensive than the 10 percent you saved. For families budgeting a first year abroad, the equipment line item belongs next to tuition and housing rather than after it, and it is worth costing before departure.
