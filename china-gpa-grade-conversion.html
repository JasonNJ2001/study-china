"Chinese University GPA and Grade Conversion: How Your Marks Are Read"

By ORIGIN Education · 2026-11-07

Chinese universities mark in percentages, not grade points, so a transcript from Nanjing or Wuhan shows 87/100 rather than 3.5/4.0. That single difference is where applications stall. The same 82 percent can read as a 3.3 at one institution and a 3.0 at another, and scholarship panels compare candidates across both systems. What follows is how the tables work, how the arithmetic works, and which paperwork decides the outcome.

What a Chinese transcript actually contains

A Chinese transcript lists every course with its credit hours and a percentage mark, then prints one average at the bottom, normally the credit-weighted average out of 100. Grade point averages appear only where the university computes them, and a large share of institutions do not. Foreign admissions offices therefore convert the percentage themselves, using either the issuing university's own formula or a published equivalence table.

The document is issued by the registrar (教务处) with a red seal, and the English version is normally a translation stamped by the same office. A transcript without the seal is treated as a photocopy and rejected at verification, which is the single most common reason a file loses four weeks.

Reading the Chinese grade labels

Chinese transcripts often carry a five-band verbal label alongside the number, and foreign readers routinely misread it. 优秀 means 90 and above, 良好 covers 80 to 89, 中等 is 70 to 79, 及格 is 60 to 69 and 不及格 is anything below 60. The pass line is exactly 60, with no partial credit at 59, so a transcript sprinkled with 58s indicates failed courses rather than near-misses.

The conversion bands most universities use

The common four-point table maps 90 to 100 as 4.0, 85 to 89 as 3.7, 82 to 84 as 3.3, 78 to 81 as 3.0, 75 to 77 as 2.7 and 60 to 63 as 1.0, with zero below 60. Bands are set by each institution, not by the Ministry of Education, so two universities in one city can differ by a full point on identical marks.

Where the 5.0 scale comes from

A second system is common in engineering and medical schools, where the grade point is calculated as the mark divided by ten minus five. That formula gives 5.0 at 100 percent, 4.0 at 90, 3.0 at 80 and 1.0 at the 60 pass line. When a transcript prints a GPA out of 5.0, do not divide by 1.25 to guess a four-point figure; ask the registrar which formula produced it, because the two methods diverge by up to half a point in the 70s.

Weighted average versus simple average

The weighted average multiplies each course mark by its credit hours and divides by total credits, so a six-credit thesis outweighs a one-credit elective. Chinese transcripts normally print this figure, and it typically runs one to three points below the plain average of the same marks. Applicants who quote whichever number looks better are usually quoting the one the verifier will not use.

A concrete case: three courses at 4, 2 and 1 credits marked 85, 70 and 95. The weighted average is 81.6, the simple average is 83.3. On the table above that is the difference between a 3.0 and a 3.3, and at the margin between scholarship shortlists it is decisive.

Retakes and how they are recorded

Retakes (补考) are the most misunderstood part of the document. Some universities print the original failed mark and the retake mark side by side, others overwrite the failure with a flat 60, and a few print only the higher result. A record that shows 60 for a course the student actually passed at 88 looks like a bare pass to any reader who does not know the convention, so an explanatory letter from the registrar is worth attaching.

Which courses count, and which are dropped

Grade rules are set faculty by faculty, and four categories are commonly excluded from the average: physical education, military theory, political theory modules and ungraded practical training. Some faculties also exclude the first attempt of a retaken course, which raises the average by a point or more. Ask for the written regulation (学籍管理规定) rather than a verbal answer, because the excluded list changes what your number means.

  • Confirm whether the printed average is weighted or simple
  • Confirm whether PE, military theory and political theory are included
  • Confirm how failed and retaken courses appear
  • Confirm the GPA formula if one is printed at all
  • Ask whether a class rank certificate (排名证明) can be issued

What foreign universities and panels look for

Most foreign institutions evaluate a Chinese degree through a credential service rather than a home-made table. WES, ECE and CHSI-based verification all convert to a four-point scale using their own published methodology, and UK admissions offices treat roughly 80 to 85 percent as a 2:1 depending on the institution's own country list. Chinese scholarship panels read the percentage and the class rank first.

The verification chain

The chain has three links and each one costs time. CHSI (学信网) issues the online verification report for the degree and the transcript, the China Academic Degrees and Graduate Education Development Centre handles degree authentication for overseas use, and a service such as WES then evaluates the verified documents. Budget six to ten weeks end to end, and note that WES requires CHSI to send documents directly rather than accept a student upload.

Documents to attach

  • Sealed transcript in Chinese with the registrar's red seal
  • Official English translation stamped by the same office
  • CHSI verification report with the QR code page
  • Degree certificate and graduation certificate, both scanned in colour
  • Class rank certificate, if the university issues one
  • A one-page note explaining the grading scale and any retake conventions

How to present your marks so nothing is lost

State both numbers, the percentage and the converted GPA, and say which table you used. Attach the class rank when it is good, because a 78 in a cohort averaging 71 is a different fact from a 78 in a cohort averaging 84. Never let an agent delete failed courses: universities reissue transcripts from a central database, and any mismatch with the CHSI record counts as falsification.

Applicants who want their percentage, their target programme and their funding options checked against the same tables can compare requirements before submitting anything.
