Changing Your PhD Supervisor or Research Group in China

By ORIGIN Education · 2026-11-26

Changing supervisors mid-PhD is more common in China than the official silence suggests, and it is handled as an administrative transfer rather than a personal falling-out. The students who succeed are the ones who understand that the decision is made by a committee, that the original supervisor's signature is one input among several, and that a PhD in China runs on a fixed four-year clock which a badly timed move can exhaust.

A supervisor change is an administrative transfer, not a resignation

In Chinese doctoral programmes the supervisor (导师) is recorded in the graduate school's register as the person responsible for your training, funding and thesis. Changing that record is a formal transfer: the old supervisor is released, a new one accepts responsibility, and the department, then the graduate school, approve. Nothing about it is informal, and nothing about it is confidential either.

Why the register matters more than the relationship

Your supervisor's name appears on your proposal approval, your funding allocation, your laboratory space and eventually your defence committee. Every one of those records is updated by the same office. A verbal agreement with a new supervisor that never reaches the register leaves you with two unhappy people and no change of status, which is the most common failure in this process.

The four-year clock never stops

Most Chinese PhD programmes are set at four years, with a hard limit that varies between six and eight. The clock does not pause for a transfer, and coursework, the proposal defence and the publication requirement all have deadlines inside it. This single fact decides most timing questions: a transfer in year one is routine, and a transfer in year three is a negotiation about whether you can still finish.

Legitimate grounds, and grounds that stall

The grounds a committee recognises are documented ones: a supervisor who leaves the university, retires, loses funding, or moves field; a research direction that no longer exists; a documented case of misconduct or of being assigned work unrelated to the degree. The Ministry of Education's code of conduct for postgraduate supervisors, issued in November 2020, explicitly bars supervisors from assigning personal errands and from abusive conduct — cite the clause, not the emotion.

Grounds that move a committee

Supervisor departure is the easiest case, because the university must reassign you anyway. Loss of a grant or laboratory is strong, especially if your project depended on the equipment. Documented misconduct moves quickly once it reaches the graduate school, though students often report it too late. A genuine mismatch of research direction is accepted if you can show a new supervisor and a coherent revised plan.

Grounds that do not

"I do not get along with him" stalls, because it gives a committee nothing to record. Poor communication, a harsh tone, or a slow response to drafts are real problems and weak grounds. So is a disagreement about authorship, unless you can produce the emails. Frame the case in terms of supervision quality and research feasibility, and the same facts become admissible.

The approval chain, in order

The sequence is fixed and each signature depends on the one before it, so a file that starts at the wrong desk loses two weeks. The order below is the standard one at Chinese universities; a few put the department meeting before the new supervisor's consent, which only changes who signs second.

  1. Write a one-page request stating the reason, the proposed new supervisor, and the revised research plan.
  2. Obtain the new supervisor's written acceptance, including confirmation of a place in the group and any funding.
  3. Ask the current supervisor to sign the release — this is the step that stalls, and it can be escalated.
  4. Submit to the department or school academic committee, which minutes a decision.
  5. Send the approved file to the graduate school for registration and record change.
  6. Notify the international students office, and the scholarship body if you are funded.
  7. Confirm the change appears in the system before you stop attending the old group.

Your funding is attached to the group, not to you

Doctoral funding in China usually arrives through the supervisor's project account, the university's doctoral stipend, or an external scholarship, and a transfer can interrupt all three. Stipend payments tied to a project stop when the project does, and a new supervisor with no grant may be unable to replace them. Ask what the new group pays before you sign anything.

CSC and university scholarships

Chinese Government Scholarship doctoral students must report a supervisor change to the university's scholarship office, which forwards it to the China Scholarship Council. The scholarship usually continues, because it attaches to the student rather than the project, but an unreported change can surface at the annual review as a discrepancy. Provincial and university scholarships follow the same logic with less paperwork.

Laboratory and equipment handover

Anything you built belongs to the group in the Chinese system: data, code, samples, and often the laptop. Negotiate the handover in writing at the same time as the release, listing what you take, what stays, and what you may still access. Students who leave this to goodwill usually lose access to their own experimental data at exactly the moment they need it for the thesis.

Timing: which semester to move

The practical rule is to move before the proposal defence, not after. A transfer during coursework costs a few weeks of adjustment; a transfer after the proposal means the new supervisor must adopt a project they did not design, and many will not. If your supervisor simply refuses to sign

A refusal is not the end of the process, because most universities provide an escalation route when a supervisor withholds consent without grounds. The usual path is a written application to the department head, then the graduate school, which can approve a transfer over the supervisor's objection. Bring documentation: supervision records, response times, and any written evidence of the problem. Emotion without a paper trail rarely moves the second committee either.

What the escalation cannot fix is time. Even a successful appeal costs a month or two, which is why the students who come out of this with a degree intact are the ones who raised the issue in year two rather than in the final semester, when the only remaining question is whether they graduate at all.
