Chinese University Libraries, Labs and Studio Access for International Students

By ORIGIN Education · 2026-11-11

Chinese universities concentrate almost everything on one campus: libraries, laboratories, studios, gyms, clinics and canteens sit within a ten-minute walk of the dormitory. That density is one of the strongest practical arguments for studying in China, and also the source of most first-month confusion, because each building has its own access rule, booking system and fee.

This guide explains how facility access actually works for international students: the campus card, the library database wall, undergraduate laboratory entry, studio space, and the costs that appear after enrolment rather than before it.

Your campus card is the key to every facility

A campus card (校园卡) is a single stored-value card that opens dormitory doors, library gates, laboratory booking systems, canteens and gyms at almost every Chinese university. It is issued during registration, usually within the first three days, and it is not optional: without it you cannot borrow a book, enter a laboratory building, or buy a meal on campus.

International students receive the same card as local students, with the same permissions, and the differences show up in two places. Some universities link the card to a local bank account for automatic top-up, which requires a Chinese phone number; others only accept top-ups through WeChat or Alipay. A lost card means a borrowing block until it is reissued, normally two to five working days, so report it the same day.

Library access: long hours, but a subscription wall

Most Chinese university libraries open from about 8:00 to 22:00 or 22:30 seven days a week during term, and extend reading-room hours during examination weeks. Access is free for enrolled students; what varies enormously is the collection. Peking University's library holds roughly 11 million items and Tsinghua's around 6 million, while a provincial teaching university may hold two million with far thinner English holdings.

The database subscription gap

Chinese-language research runs on CNKI and Wanfang, and both are almost always subscribed. English-language access is the weak point: a mid-tier university may hold IEEE, Springer and Elsevier packages yet not subscribe to Web of Science, Scopus or ProQuest. Ask the library for its A-Z database list before you accept an offer, and confirm whether off-campus access works through the campus VPN, because remote access is often restricted to staff and doctoral students.

Borrowing limits and fines

Undergraduates typically borrow 10 to 20 volumes at a time for 30 to 60 days with one renewal. Overdue fines run about 0.1 to 0.2 RMB per day per book and stop once they reach the replacement price. Reserved textbooks are usually reading-room only, so plan around a two-hour loan rather than a semester-long one.

Getting into a laboratory as an undergraduate

Undergraduate access to research laboratories in China is genuine but conditional. Most science and engineering faculties run an open laboratory programme (开放实验室) that lets second- and third-year students book bench time, but entry normally requires a completed safety course, a supervisor's signature and a fixed weekly slot. Scheduled coursework laboratories are open to anyone enrolled in the module.

Safety training is the actual gate

Chinese laboratory safety training is not a formality. Chemistry, materials and biomedical faculties require a written test and, in many cases, a supervised dry run before access is issued, and the whole induction can take one to two weeks at the start of term. Students who arrive mid-semester often wait until the next intake.

What supervisors expect

Open-lab places are limited and allocated as much by relationship as by grades. Email a professor in your second semester with a specific interest rather than a general request, and expect to commit eight to ten hours a week for a full semester before anything publishable comes out of it.

Studio and workshop access for design, art and architecture

Design, architecture and fine-arts students receive studio space as part of the programme rather than as an extra: a shared desk in a year-group studio, usually accessible 24 hours by card, plus wood, metal and digital-fabrication workshops booked by the hour. Materials are billed separately, and that bill commonly reaches 2,000 to 5,000 RMB a year.

Printing and model-making are the hidden lines. Large-format plotting at a campus print shop costs roughly 10 to 30 RMB per sheet, laser cutting is charged by machine time, and a single architecture review can consume a month of that budget in a week. Studios also tend to keep late hours before reviews, which is worth knowing if your dormitory has a curfew.

Sports facilities and the membership fee nobody mentions

Gyms, swimming pools and courts are almost always on campus but not always free. Many universities include basic gym access in the dormitory fee, then charge separately for swimming at roughly 10 to 30 RMB a visit and court bookings at 20 to 80 RMB an hour. Off-peak student rates are usually about half the evening price.

The badminton and basketball halls are the ones that require booking, often through the same WeChat mini-program used for canteen top-ups, and slots open a week ahead. In larger cities, university sports complexes also admit students from neighbouring institutions at a discount, which is useful if your own campus is small.

What facilities actually cost beyond tuition

Facility costs are small next to tuition but they are real, and they arrive at unpredictable moments. How to check facilities before you accept an offer

The reliable way to check facilities is to ask three specific questions by email before you pay a deposit: which laboratories undergraduates can enter, which English-language databases the library subscribes to, and whether studio or workshop space is included in tuition. Vague answers to those three questions tell you more than any campus video.

Questions worth sending to the international office

  • Which buildings can I enter with my card in first year, and which are restricted to postgraduates?
  • Does the library subscribe to Web of Science, Scopus or IEEE, and does off-campus access work?
  • Is studio, workshop or laboratory space included in tuition, or billed per semester?
  • What do gym membership and swimming cost for a registered student?
  • Is laboratory safety induction offered in English, and when does the next intake start?

Ask for the answers in writing, and compare them across your shortlist. Facility quality is one of the few variables that a prospective student can verify before committing, and the answers rarely change once term begins.
