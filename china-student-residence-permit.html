Residence Permit in China: The 30-Day Rule After You Land

By ORIGIN Education · 2026-11-02

Your X1 visa gets you through the airport. It does not make your stay legal for the year. Within 30 days of entry you have to convert it into a residence permit, and that permit — not the visa in your passport — is what allows you to remain in China, leave and re-enter, open a bank account, and register for classes beyond the first month.

Every year, a handful of students in every intake discover this late. The consequences range from an afternoon of queueing to a fine and a cancelled semester. Here is how the process actually works.

The 30-day rule, precisely

Why the clock starts at the airport

The clock starts on the day you enter China, not the day your programme starts, and not the day you finish registration at the university. Thirty days is the outer limit; the practical deadline is earlier, because the application must be submitted before the window closes, and processing takes additional working days.

Why filing early is not optional

A common and costly misreading: students arrive in late August, spend two weeks settling in, and file in mid-September assuming they still have time. They usually do. They also usually arrive at the office during the single busiest fortnight of the year, when every international student in the city is doing the same thing.

File within your first week. The permit has no advantage to being late.

What goes in the folder

Requirements vary slightly by city, but the core set is consistent across the country.

Add a photocopy of everything. Offices in different cities ask for different subsets, and returning the next day because you lack one page is the most common wasted trip in the whole process.

The accommodation registration form nobody mentions

This is the document that derails the most applications, because it is issued by the police, not the university, and it has its own deadline.

Three living situations, three different paths

If you stay in a hotel, the hotel registers you automatically. If you stay in a university dormitory, the dormitory office handles it, but you must confirm they have done so and collect the paper. If you rent an apartment, you and your landlord must register at the local police station within 24 hours of moving in, and you will need the landlord's ID and the lease.

The address on that form must match the address you write on the residence permit application. A mismatch triggers a reapplication, and a reapplication close to day 30 is how overstays happen.

Where to apply, and how long it takes

Applications go to the exit-entry administration hall of the municipal public security bureau, not to a police station and not to the university. Most cities have one central hall; larger ones have district branches.

Processing typically takes seven to fifteen working days. The fee for a study-related residence permit is generally in the region of 400 RMB for a stay of up to one year, with higher fees for longer permits.

What happens to your passport

You will be given a receipt and a collection date. Keep the receipt with your passport at all times while the application is pending, because it is your proof of legal status in the interim.

While the application is under review, your passport is held by the office. Do not book international travel during that window.

Renewing: set the calendar reminder now

A residence permit is issued for the length of your programme, up to a maximum, and it does not always cover your full course. If your programme runs longer than the permit, you renew before expiry — typically starting 30 days ahead, and no earlier than the office will accept.

Three habits prevent almost all renewal problems:

  • Photograph the permit's expiry date and set a reminder 45 days before.
  • Ask the international office each year whether your permit length still matches your enrolment.
  • Renew before you travel home for the summer, not after you return, if the expiry falls during the break.

An expired permit is not a formality. Overstaying can attract a daily fine that accumulates quickly up to a statutory cap, and in serious cases detention and a re-entry ban. A student who misses a renewal because of a holiday is treated the same as any other overstayer.

Leaving and re-entering mid-course

A valid residence permit allows multiple entries, so a trip home at term break needs no new visa. Two exceptions matter. If you switch universities, you generally need a new permit tied to the new institution. And if you lose your passport, the replacement process includes transferring the residence permit, which cannot be done at the airport.

Five things that delay an application

  1. The accommodation form shows a different address from the application.
  2. The physical examination was done at a clinic that is not on the designated list.
  3. The passport expires within six months, which caps the permit length.
  4. A photocopy is missing and the office has no copier.
  5. The applicant arrives after the daily quota of numbers has been handed out — common in large cities, where arriving before opening is normal practice.

The short version

Convert within the first week, not the fourth. Get the accommodation form before you go anywhere near the exit-entry hall. Match every address and every name exactly. Photograph the expiry date the day the permit is issued, and treat the renewal as a fixed appointment rather than a task you will remember.

Do those four things and the residence permit becomes a single morning of admin in your first month, then an annual housekeeping item. Skip them and it becomes the reason a semester ends early. If you are preparing an application and want the document sequence mapped against your intake date, this is the sort of groundwork that gets checked before arrival rather than after.
