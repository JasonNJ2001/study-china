Campus Sports Facilities in China: Gyms, Pools and Booking Systems

By ORIGIN Education · 2026-11-18

Universities in China invest in sport because physical education is a graduation requirement rather than an optional extra. That single fact explains why even a mid-tier provincial campus usually has a 400-metre track, a university gym with badminton courts and a pool, and why the booking rules are stricter than most foreign students expect.

What a Chinese Campus Gym Actually Includes

Most campuses split sport into two tiers: free outdoor facilities such as running tracks, basketball and volleyball courts and outdoor ping-pong tables, and paid indoor halls inside the 体育馆, where the weights room, badminton courts and usually the pool sit. The indoor halls are operated by the university sports department, not a private gym chain.

Free outdoor, paid indoor

Outdoor grounds open around 06:00 and close around 22:00 and need no reservation, which is why they fill at dawn with staff and local residents as well as students. Indoor venues require your campus card at the door and either a per-visit charge or a semester card bought at the sports department office.

Why the halls are still busy

Physical education is a credited course, normally two sessions a week in the first two years, so the halls serve teaching first and recreational use second. The 国家学生体质健康标准 national fitness test is sat every year and is a condition for graduation and for scholarship eligibility, which keeps demand high.

How Booking Works

Booking runs through a WeChat mini-program bound to your student number rather than a website or phone line. Slots usually open 24 to 72 hours in advance, are released in one-hour blocks, and are capped at one or two bookings per student per day; a no-show without cancellation is logged and can suspend the account for a week.

The three-step routine

  1. Open the sports department mini-program and bind your student number.
  2. Choose venue, date and a one-hour block, then pay from the campus card balance.
  3. Scan the QR code at the door within 15 minutes, or the slot returns to the pool.

Peak hours worth avoiding

Weekday evenings from 18:00 to 21:00 and the whole of Saturday are effectively full. Before 10:00 and between 14:00 and 16:00 on weekdays are the reliable gaps. The first week of semester is the worst of the year, because every new student activates an account at once.

Prices are set by each university and are low by international standards, but nothing indoors is free. The ranges below hold across most public universities; a 985 university in a wealthy coastal city sits at the top of each band, and a western provincial campus at the bottom.

Campus card only

Cash is almost never accepted. Campus card, WeChat Pay or Alipay are the only methods, and the card balance is topped up at the canteen counter or inside the campus app.

Swimming Pools: The Strictest Venue on Campus

Campus pools enforce a health-certificate rule that surprises foreign students: most ask for a valid health certificate or a recent medical clearance, plus a swimming cap, and refuse entry without both. Some also require a short swim assessment before allowing lane swimming at all.

The swim test is real

Tsinghua University requires undergraduates to pass a 50-metre swim test, or complete a remedial swimming course, before graduation, and several other universities have copied the rule for their own students. International students are frequently exempt from the graduation clause but not from the pool's own entry assessment.

Closures you cannot predict

Pools typically close one day a week for water treatment and two to four weeks in summer for maintenance. The schedule is posted on the sports department's WeChat account rather than the university website, so follow that account in your first week.

Varsity Sport and Inter-University Competition

If you already compete, the entry point is the university team (校队), selected by open trial in September and October. Team members usually get free hall access, a kit allowance and a coach, and competition runs through the provincial university games and the national 大运会 cycle.

What selection actually rewards

Coaches look for a measurable performance baseline first, then attendance and coachability. Bring times, distances or match footage; a verbal claim of experience carries little weight against a stopwatch.

Fitness Tests and Graduation

The annual fitness test covers sprint, endurance run, sit-and-reach, standing long jump and pull-ups or sit-ups, scored against national age and sex benchmarks. International students usually sit it too, though many universities waive the graduation consequence and only record the score.

What happens if you fail

A failed test means a retake, and a retake failure triggers remedial PE classes rather than expulsion. The practical risk is scholarship review, where a poor score can be raised in the annual assessment.

Practical Rules That Catch Foreigners Out

Four rules cause most of the friction: outdoor shoes are banned on indoor courts, so pack a clean pair; a towel is required on gym equipment; some halls close for two hours at midday; and photographing other students in the pool or changing area is treated as a serious conduct issue under the university's personal-information rules.

Plan for a term card if you train more than twice a week, keep the mini-program notifications on, and treat the booking QR code the way you treat a train ticket. Students who train regularly also tend to settle faster into campus life, because the gym and the pool are where language practice happens without a classroom.
