HSK 4 vs HSK 5: Which Level Do You Actually Need

By ORIGIN Education · 2026-10-20

Almost every applicant to a Chinese-taught degree reaches the same fork in the road, and the choice quietly decides the next twelve months of their life. HSK 4 is the level printed on most university websites. HSK 5 is the level that competitive departments actually enforce once your file reaches a human being.

The gap between the two is not one more grammar chapter. It is roughly a doubling of vocabulary, a jump in reading speed, and a different kind of writing task. Choosing between HSK 4 or HSK 5 is therefore a decision about your application route, not about how diligent you are.

What the two certificates actually prove

The test currently in use has six levels. HSK 4 assumes about 1,200 vocabulary words and asks you to handle daily situations, familiar academic topics and simple written passages. HSK 5 assumes about 2,500 words and expects you to read newspaper articles, follow a lecture, and produce a structured essay or presentation. Both are scored out of 300, with 180 as the pass line. A nine-band standard is being phased in with higher vocabulary targets, but entry requirements are still written in six-level terms; read the line printed for your intake year.

The score often matters more than the level

A candidate with 181 on HSK 4 and one with 270 hold the same certificate and are not the same applicant. Departments increasingly set a floor above the pass line, commonly 210 or 240. When a page says HSK 4, ask which score they treat as sufficient.

Writing is where the two levels separate sharply

HSK 4 writing means completing sentences and producing a short passage from prompts. HSK 5 writing means two longer pieces, one describing a chart or an argument. If your degree is assessed through essays, HSK 5 is the first rehearsal for that coursework.

Where HSK 4 is genuinely enough

HSK 4 is not a consolation prize. For a large number of routes it is the correct and sufficient target:

  • Chinese-taught bachelor's programmes at provincial and mid-tier universities, where HSK 4 at 180 or above is the stated entry line
  • Direct entry to a one-year Chinese language preparatory programme, with the degree place held while you complete it
  • Non-degree exchange, short-term study and summer programmes, which usually ask for HSK 3 or 4
  • Some professional master's programmes taught in Chinese that include language support in the first semester
  • One-year Chinese language study under Confucius Institute scholarship routes, where HSK 3 or 4 is the entry condition rather than the exit condition

The pattern is consistent: HSK 4 gets you into the system. It does not always get you into the classroom where your degree is taught.

Where HSK 5 is the real floor

The moment a programme involves competition, clinical work, or reading primary material in Chinese, the requirement moves up:

  • Chinese-taught master's degrees at ranked universities, where HSK 5 is the default and HSK 6 appears in humanities and social science departments
  • Medicine taught in Chinese, where patient contact and licensing rules make a higher level non-negotiable
  • Law, journalism, Chinese literature, and teaching Chinese as a foreign language, which may ask for HSK 6 outright
  • Programmes with an internship or hospital rotation built into the curriculum
  • Direct entry to year one of a competitive major without a preparatory year

Treat the middle column as the opening offer. The right-hand column is where applications are actually decided, and none of it appears in a ranking table.

The registration test nobody warns you about

Many universities re-test Chinese on arrival, regardless of the certificate in your file. The result drives class placement, and a student holding HSK 5 who performs badly can be moved into a language year before the degree starts. Prepare for that test by reading Chinese material in your own subject, and ask in writing whether placement results can delay the start date.

How scholarships read the two levels

Chinese Government Scholarship applications for Chinese-taught programmes are assessed against HSK requirements set by the receiving university, so the same file can be strong at one institution and weak at another. Where your level falls below the departmental line, the usual outcome is an offer of one year of Chinese language study rather than rejection. That is a workable fallback, but it costs a year and is rarely funded at the same rate.

Provincial and municipal scholarships vary more widely. Where the scholarship call and the programme page conflict, the page governs admission and the call governs money.

How much time the difference really costs

From zero Chinese to HSK 4 typically takes nine to twelve months at around twenty hours a week. HSK 4 to HSK 5 is commonly another six to nine months, because the vocabulary load doubles and reading speed becomes the bottleneck.

Studying outside China adds time. Without daily exposure, listening and reading speed improve slowly, and candidates who pass mock papers at home often lose marks in the listening section. If your target is HSK 5 and you are preparing abroad, add a term of buffer.

Three questions that settle it

Before you register for an exam, get written answers to these:

  1. Is the degree taught in Chinese or in English? If English, HSK is irrelevant and English proof is the requirement.
  2. Does the programme accept a preparatory language year in place of the higher certificate? If yes, HSK 4 with a conditional offer is a legitimate route.
  3. Will the department re-test on arrival and place students by result? If yes, the certificate is a ticket to the test, not a pass through it.

Chinese-taught degree, no preparatory year, and a placement test at registration: target HSK 5 and do not stop at 4. Chinese-taught bachelor's with a preparatory year available: HSK 4 is enough to start, and you will reach the higher level inside China more cheaply.

Bottom line

Collecting certificates you do not need is one of the most common ways applicants lose a year. HSK 4 opens most Chinese-taught bachelor's routes and the preparatory years attached to them; HSK 5 is the working level for master's study, medicine, and any department that will test you again in September. Decide which route you are applying for first, then pick the certificate that route requires. The applicants who struggle are rarely the ones with the wrong level; they are the ones who never asked the department which level it enforces.
