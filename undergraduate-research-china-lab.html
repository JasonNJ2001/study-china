Joining a Chinese University Lab as an Undergraduate Researcher

By ORIGIN Education · 2026-11-18

Undergraduate research in China is institutionalised rather than informal. Universities run a credit-bearing research training course, a national funding programme pays for student-led projects, and laboratories recruit undergraduates through a formal application rather than a professor's personal favour, which means a foreign student can enter the system without inside contacts.

How Undergraduate Research Is Organised

Chinese universities treat research as a taught component of the degree rather than a voluntary extra. Three routes exist: a 科研训练 credit course in the third year, a funded student project under the national innovation programme, and open recruitment into a supervisor's 课题组, where places are advertised and applicants are screened on transcript and interview.

The three routes compared

The course route is the easiest to enter and the least demanding, usually one semester and one report. The funded route carries money and a formal defence. The 课题组 route is the only one that reliably produces a publication, because you join an existing project with equipment already running.

Timing matters more than grades

Recruitment clusters in the second half of year two and the first half of year three, before students commit to a thesis topic. Asking in the final semester, when supervisors are closing their quota, is the most common reason applications fail.

Finding a Supervisor

Start from the faculty page, pick three professors whose last two papers you have actually read, and email each one a short message naming the paper, the method you found interesting, and the specific task you could contribute to. Expect a low reply rate; five emails producing one meeting is normal.

What the email must contain

A subject line that states your degree, year and interest in one phrase; two sentences on the paper; one sentence on your relevant skill, whether coding, wet-lab technique or statistical software; and a request for a 15-minute meeting. Attach a one-page CV and your transcript. Chinese professors read attachments before they read prose.

The interview is technical

Expect questions about the paper's methods, not about your motivation. A candidate who can explain why a control was included is treated as serious; a candidate who praises the professor's reputation is not.

The National Innovation Programme

The 国家级大学生创新创业训练计划, known as 大创, was launched by the Ministry of Education in 2012 and funds student-designed projects at three levels: national, provincial and institutional. National projects in science and engineering typically receive 10,000 to 20,000 RMB, enough to cover reagents, a device or fieldwork.

Application cycle

Applications open once a year, usually in March or April, with results in June and work starting in September. The proposal is written by students and countersigned by the supervisor, who is accountable for the budget.

Can international students apply?

Most universities allow it, but the project team normally needs a Chinese student as lead because the funding account and the reimbursement system run through the campus finance office. Join as a member first, then lead a project in your second cycle.

What the First Semester Actually Looks Like

The first month is administration and safety: a lab safety examination that must be passed before you receive card access, reagent and equipment training, and a data-recording standard that is stricter than most foreign undergraduates are used to. Real experiments usually begin in week five or six.

The weekly rhythm

  1. Attend the 组会, a group meeting where every member reports progress, usually weekly and two hours long.
  2. Read two to three papers a week and present one.
  3. Keep a bound paper notebook; electronic-only records are often refused at audit.
  4. Report failures openly, because a hidden failed batch costs the group a month.

Authorship and Credit

Chinese labs use a clear hierarchy: the student who did the work is 第一作者, the supervisor who funded and designed the study is 通讯作者, and contribution order is negotiated before submission rather than after. Undergraduates most often land second or third author, which still counts on a graduate application.

Ask before you start

Ask the supervisor in the first month what authorship you could expect and what would earn it. The conversation is normal in Chinese labs and is far easier before the work exists than after the paper is drafted.

What counts as a publication

For most Chinese universities, a paper in a CNKI-indexed Chinese core journal or an SCI journal both count, but only the SCI one travels well outside China. Conference papers count little unless the conference is a recognised international one.

Visa and Payment Rules

Research done for credit or as part of your degree is covered by the X1 visa and residence permit, and an unpaid role in a lab needs no extra permission. Paid work is different: a stipend or salary requires the university's international office to record it, and off-campus employment needs approval in the residence permit.

Stipends in practice

Undergraduate research is usually unpaid, sometimes with a meal or transport allowance. Paid research assistant roles exist mainly for postgraduate students, and where an undergraduate is paid, it typically comes out of the supervisor's grant as 助研费 at 300 to 800 RMB a month.

Does It Help Your Application

It helps in three specific ways: a supervisor's recommendation letter written from direct observation, a concrete research narrative for a master's or PhD application, and access to 推免 recommendation channels where domestic students compete. None of these can be bought with a summer course.

The honest limitation

A single semester in a lab produces competence, not results. Students who gain most treat it as a two-year commitment and accept that the first six months are mostly cleaning glassware and reading.

For applicants deciding whether a research-led university suits them, the practical test is whether undergraduates are allowed into labs at all, and that question is worth asking directly of any admissions office before you apply, because the answer varies sharply between institutions in China.
