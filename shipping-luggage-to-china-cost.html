"Shipping Luggage and Belongings to China: What It Costs"

By ORIGIN Education · 2026-11-09

Most international students arrive in China with two suitcases and a freight quote they never compared. The cheap option is slow and the fast option is expensive, so the decision comes down to what you genuinely cannot buy after landing. This guide covers what freight costs, what customs will release, and where shipping loses to shopping.

What does it cost to ship belongings to China?

Sea freight is the only economical way to move volume: expect USD 150-350 per cubic metre port-to-port from Europe or North America, plus RMB 800-1,500 for Chinese clearance, port fees and inland delivery. Air freight runs USD 6-12 per kilogram, so a 1.5 cubic metre student shipment costing about USD 700 by sea would cost USD 2,000 or more by air.

Those figures assume shared container space, known as LCL or groupage, where your boxes occupy part of a container alongside other shippers' cargo. You pay for the space you use, which is why 1.5 cubic metres is the practical minimum: below that, fixed fees dominate and the cost per kilogram stops making sense.

Why the quote you receive is not the price you pay

Almost every first quote is port-to-port: the forwarder delivers your boxes to a Chinese port and stops there. Clearance, terminal handling, storage after the free days, and trucking to your university city are billed separately and can add 30-60% to the headline number. Ask for a door-to-door figure in writing, naming the destination city.

How long it really takes

Europe to Shanghai or Ningbo is typically 30-45 days at sea, plus 7-14 days for booking, loading, clearance and delivery. United States west coast routes run 18-30 days. For a September start, ship in June, because a missed sailing adds two weeks of waiting for the next container.

Air freight becomes rational in one case: you are shipping something that cannot be replaced before term starts, such as a specialist instrument, a portfolio or prescription medicine. For everything else, the money is better spent on bedding and kitchenware bought in China.

What Chinese customs will release, and what it will not

China treats your boxes as unaccompanied baggage, and timing decides everything: the shipment must be declared at the port where you enter, on the same trip, and must arrive within six months of your entry date. Miss that window and the cargo is assessed as ordinary import goods, with duty and VAT applied to the full value.

You will need your passport with the entry stamp, the X1 visa or residence permit, the admission letter, the bill of lading or air waybill, and an itemised packing list. Customs releases personal effects in reasonable quantity for personal use duty-free; three laptops and ten identical jackets do not qualify, and the officer can reject the whole declaration.

Items that will be seized or destroyed

  • Meat, sausage, dried beef and dairy products, under animal disease controls
  • Fresh fruit, vegetables, seeds and soil, under phytosanitary rules
  • Loose lithium batteries in checked parcels, and power banks above the airline limit
  • Prescription drugs without a doctor's letter and original packaging
  • Counterfeit branded goods, which are destroyed and can trigger a fine

The allowances worth knowing

An arriving traveller may bring 400 cigarettes and 1,500 millilitres of alcohol above 12% ABV duty-free, and must declare cash above USD 5,000. List professional equipment on the customs form, because that form is also your proof when you ship things home again.

The 220-volt problem: what is not worth bringing

China supplies 220 volts at 50 hertz, with Type A, C and I sockets, so British three-pin plugs and North American 110-volt appliances need adapters and, in the second case, a step-up transformer. A transformer rated above 1,000 watts costs more than a new rice cooker in China and weighs several kilograms, so leave the appliance at home.

Laptops, phones, camera chargers and electric toothbrushes are almost all dual-voltage and need only a plug adapter costing RMB 10-20. Check the label on the power brick: 100-240V, 50/60Hz means you are fine; 120V only means you are not.

Bedding, kitchenware and the ninety-centimetre bed

Chinese dormitory beds are narrower than most Western students expect: 90 by 190 centimetres is standard, with 100 by 200 in newer international dormitories. Bed linen bought at home frequently does not fit and duvet covers sized for a 200-centimetre bed bunch up, so measure the room you have been allocated before you buy anything.

Kitchenware is the other category where shipping loses to shopping. A wok, rice cooker, kettle, bowls and chopsticks cost RMB 200-400 at any Carrefour, Walmart or campus supermarket, and the same set weighs 8-10 kilograms, which is USD 60-120 by air or a third of a cubic metre by sea.

How to choose a forwarder without getting burned

An agent who quotes a suspiciously low price per kilogram for sea freight is usually quoting weight instead of volume, and will re-invoice you at the port. Ask three questions before paying: is the quote door-to-door or port-to-port, are Chinese clearance and delivery included, and what happens if the container arrives after my programme starts.

Insure the shipment. Marine cargo insurance costs about 1-3% of declared value and covers total loss, which is rare but not unheard of with groupage cargo transloaded two or three times. Photograph every box before sealing it and keep the packing list with values in your email.

A realistic shipping plan for a one-year master's

  1. Decide what you cannot buy in China: prescriptions, glasses, specialist books, an instrument, family photographs.
  2. Weigh the rest against replacement cost. Anything under USD 100 is cheaper to rebuy than to ship.
  3. Book sea freight in June for a September start, door-to-door, with insurance.
  4. Declare the shipment at the port of entry and keep the customs receipt with your passport.
  5. Buy bedding, kitchenware and a desk lamp in week one, from Taobao or the campus supermarket.

Students who follow that order spend USD 400-700 and arrive with a room that works. Students who ship everything spend three times as much and still find the duvet does not fit.
