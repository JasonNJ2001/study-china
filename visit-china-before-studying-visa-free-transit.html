Visiting China Before You Enrol: Visa-Free Transit and Short-Stay Options

By ORIGIN Education · 2026-11-14

Choosing a university from photographs is how most international students end up transferring in year two. A four-day visit before you apply answers questions no brochure can: whether the campus is a forty-minute bus ride from the city, whether the dormitory corridor smells of cooking oil, whether the department has a laboratory or a photograph of one. Since December 2024 that visit has become dramatically cheaper for many nationalities.

Why a Pre-Enrolment Visit Changes the Decision

A pre-enrolment visit lets you test the three things that decide whether you stay: the campus location relative to the city, the condition of the accommodation you have been allocated, and whether the department staff can answer technical questions in English. None of these appear accurately in marketing material, and all three are expensive to discover after you have paid a deposit.

It also changes the conversation with the admissions office. Walking in with a passport and a list of questions produces a different answer from an email, because staff can see you are serious and will introduce you to a student from your country.

The timing question

Visit between October and January if you are applying for the following September. Teaching is in session, the weather is representative outside the northeast, and admissions staff are between intakes rather than buried in paperwork. A July visit shows you an empty campus.

The 240-Hour Visa-Free Transit Rule

The 240-hour transit scheme, in force since 17 December 2024 and replacing the earlier 144-hour arrangement, allows passport holders from more than fifty countries to enter China for up to ten days without a visa, provided they arrive at a designated port and hold a confirmed onward ticket to a third country. It is the cheapest route to a campus visit.

The rule is a transit rule, not a tourist rule. You must be travelling through China to somewhere else, and officers check the onward booking at the port. Ten days covers two cities and one campus, or three campuses in one city.

Who qualifies

Eligibility depends on your passport, not your residence. The scheme covers citizens of more than fifty countries, including most of Europe, Australia, New Zealand, Japan, South Korea and much of South America, and it is being extended country by country. Check the current list on the National Immigration Administration site, not a travel blog, because it changed three times in 2025.

The onward-ticket condition

Your ticket must leave China for a third country, not return to where you came from. Bangkok to Shanghai to Bangkok does not qualify; Bangkok to Shanghai to Seoul does. The booking must be confirmed and dated within 240 hours of arrival, and a standby ticket is refused at the counter.

Unilateral Visa-Free Entry for Ordinary Passports

Separately from transit, China grants 30-day visa-free entry to ordinary passport holders of more than forty countries, a list built through 2024 and 2025 and repeatedly extended, in several cases to the end of 2026. This is ordinary entry, not transit, so no onward ticket is needed and you may enter and leave from the same country.

For applicants from those countries the campus visit requires nothing but a flight and a hotel booking. For everyone else the transit route or an L visa remains the practical option, and the difference in preparation time is roughly three weeks.

When You Still Need a Tourist L Visa

You need an L visa if your passport is not on either list, if you want to stay longer than ten days, or if your itinerary does not include a third country. It is issued for 30 to 90 days, single or multiple entry, and requires an application form, photograph, flight and hotel bookings, and in some consulates a letter of invitation from a Chinese host.

Processing takes four working days in most consulates and longer in peak season. The visa is cheap by Western standards, but the appointment backlog in July and August can be three weeks, which is why a September applicant planning a January visit should apply in November.

What a Five-Day Visit Can Settle

Five days is enough if the days are planned before you land. Most applicants waste the first two on arrival logistics and the last one on shopping, which leaves too little time for the questions that decide the next four years, and those questions need appointments made two weeks in advance.

  1. Book a campus tour through the international student office, and ask for a current international student to accompany it.
  2. Ask to see the actual room type you have been allocated, not the show room kept for visitors.
  3. Sit in on one lecture in your intended department, and count how much of it is delivered in English.
  4. Walk the route from the dormitory to the department at 8am, since the distance on the map is not the distance in traffic.
  5. Meet the admissions officer who will read your file, and ask what the weakest part of a typical application is.
  6. Visit a supermarket, a bank branch and a hospital registration desk, because those three say more about daily life than any campus building.

What the visit cannot do

A visit does not create a legal basis for studying. You cannot enrol, register for classes or start a programme on visitor status; that requires an X1 visa for programmes longer than 180 days or an X2 for shorter ones, both issued against an admission letter and a JW202 form. Arriving on a tourist entry and expecting to convert it later is the most common costly mistake.

Preparing the Campus Visit Properly

Bring the documents that make the visit productive: transcripts, the admission letter if you have one, a question list per department, and the name of the person who arranged the tour. Chinese universities are bureaucratic in a predictable way, and a visitor who arrives with a printed appointment and a file is treated as a partner rather than a tourist.

Ask at the end for two things in writing: the module list for your intended programme, and the email of the staff member handling your application. Both beat any photograph, and both are hard to obtain by email from abroad.
