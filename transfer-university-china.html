Transferring Universities in China: Can International Students Switch Mid-Degree

By ORIGIN Education · 2026-11-04

If you want to transfer university in china, the first thing to understand is that the system has no transfer admission at all. There is no clearing house, no common application, and no credit portability agreement between institutions. Switching means withdrawing from one university and applying to another as a fresh applicant, which resets your year of study and often your scholarship.

Why there is no transfer route

Chinese higher education runs on a national student record system tied to the gaokao and to a fixed enrolment plan allocated to each institution by the provincial education authority. Admission is a placement into a quota, not a contract with a student, so moving between institutions requires the release of one quota and the allocation of another, a procedure the regulations reserve for exceptional cases.

The two exceptions written into the rules

Transfers between Chinese institutions are permitted for documented medical reasons or for a genuine hardship that the receiving and sending universities both certify, and both cases need provincial approval. International students sit outside that channel entirely, because their admission is governed by university-level agreements and the student visa rather than by the national quota.

What actually works: withdraw and reapply

In practice, an international student who wants a different university applies to the new institution with a fresh application, receives a new admission letter and a new JW202 form, and uses those to obtain a new X1 visa or to convert the existing residence permit. The old enrolment is cancelled, and the new programme normally starts in year one.

What happens to the year you already completed

Credit recognition is discretionary and rarely generous. A department may accept three or four courses where the syllabus matches closely, and it will ask for the module descriptions and your transcripts in Chinese or English. The default outcome is that you restart, which is why the decision is usually worth making only in the first year.

Changing major inside the same university

The cheaper move is to change major within your current institution. Most universities run an internal transfer window at the end of the first year, with a quota per receiving department and a grade requirement that in competitive departments reaches the top ten or twenty per cent of your cohort.

  • Ask the academic affairs office for the internal transfer policy in your first semester, not the second.
  • Confirm whether the receiving department requires a subject examination.
  • Check whether your scholarship survives a change of major, because many do not.
  • Confirm the graduation requirement for the new major, including any Chinese-language threshold.

The grade requirement in competitive departments

Computer science, clinical medicine and finance are the departments where the quota is smallest, and their published bar is often the top ten per cent of the cohort plus a written examination in the target subject. Below that bar, the realistic options are a related major in the same faculty or a minor, neither of which changes the degree certificate.

The scholarship problem

CSC and most provincial scholarships are awarded for a specific programme at a specific institution. A change of institution requires the China Scholarship Council's written approval, which is granted for documented reasons such as the closure of a programme or a supervisor's departure, not for preference. Changing major within the same university is easier but still needs the university's international office to amend the record.

What a provincial scholarship requires

Provincial and municipal scholarships, including the Jiangsu and Zhejiang schemes, are tied to the host university's own allocation and cannot follow a student to another province at all. Withdrawing forfeits the award and usually requires repayment of the current year's stipend, so read the award letter before starting the transfer conversation.

What it does to your visa and residence permit

Your residence permit is tied to the institution named in the record. Once the enrolment changes, the old permit must be cancelled and a new one issued against the new JW202, and the change has to be registered at the exit-entry administration within ten days of the enrolment change. Leaving it late creates an overstay, which is a separate and more expensive problem.

When switching is the right decision

Switch if the programme is materially different from what was described, if the language of instruction turned out not to be English, or if the department has no laboratory or clinical access for undergraduates. Do not switch for city preference or ranking position alone, because restarting costs a year plus tuition and the gain is rarely worth it.

International students who plan carefully usually do better by choosing correctly the first time than by transferring afterwards. Reviewing the programme's actual curriculum, its language of instruction for all four years, and its scholarship conditions before accepting an offer removes most of the reasons people later want to move.
