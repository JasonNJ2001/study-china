Architecture and Urban Planning Degrees in China for International Students

By ORIGIN Education · 2026-11-20

Architecture in China is taught the way it was taught in Europe a generation ago: five years, a studio that owns your week, and a jury that judges drawings in public. The system is cheap by Western standards and unusually rigorous about drawing, but it draws a hard line between an accredited professional degree and a general engineering degree that most applicants only discover in year three.

What an architecture degree in China involves

A Chinese architecture degree is studio-centred rather than lecture-centred. Students take structure, materials and history as courses, but the credits that decide whether they graduate sit in design studio, where a project runs eight to sixteen weeks and ends with a public review. Roughly half of a student's contact hours are spent in studio.

The workload is the part foreign applicants underestimate. Chinese studios meet three afternoons a week with a tutor who reviews drawings individually, and students who treat studio like a lecture course fail it, because attendance and weekly progress are graded.

Five years, not four, and why the distinction matters

The standard Chinese architecture degree runs five years, and the extra year is not padding: it is what makes the qualification a professional degree rather than a general one. Programmes accredited by the national architecture education evaluation committee award a Bachelor of Architecture (建筑学学士), while unaccredited programmes award a Bachelor of Engineering (工学学士) after four or five years.

That difference travels. Several countries recognise a five-year professional degree for licensure and treat a four-year engineering degree as pre-professional, requiring a further master's. If you intend to register as an architect at home, check both the Chinese accreditation status and your home regulator's list.

The accredited BArch

An accredited BArch requires a five-year curriculum with a minimum studio load, a construction technology sequence and a final thesis project. There are fewer than sixty accredited programmes in the country, and the list is published by the evaluation committee rather than by the universities themselves, which is why two similarly ranked schools can differ on this point.

The four-year Bachelor of Engineering

The four-year route covers architectural technology, building services and construction management rather than design authorship. It is a good degree for someone heading into engineering consultancy, and a frustrating one for someone who wants to design buildings, because the studio hours are a fraction of the BArch load.

The eight schools that dominate hiring

Chinese architecture has a canonical list, the 建筑老八校, and it still governs hiring at the large design institutes. Graduates of these eight schools fill the major state-owned design institutes in Beijing, Shanghai and Shenzhen, and the alumni networks function as a referral system.

Beyond the eight

The gap is narrower than the list implies. Shenzhen University sits inside the region with the most construction work in the country, and Hunan and Zhejiang both run accredited programmes with lower tuition than Beijing or Shanghai.

Portfolios, drawing tests and interviews

Most international applicants submit a portfolio of fifteen to twenty-five pages plus an interview, while Chinese domestic applicants sit a national drawing examination. The portfolio matters more than grades at the top schools, and it is judged on drawing ability and spatial thinking rather than on rendering polish.

What reviewers look for is evidence that you can observe. A sketchbook of buildings you have stood in front of outperforms a set of imagined villas, and one street documented over ten drawings outperforms ten unrelated projects. Include process, not only final images, and label everything in English.

Studio culture and how you are assessed

Assessment is by public jury, known as 评图, where students pin drawings and models and defend them in front of three to five tutors. Criticism is direct and often blunt; foreign students frequently read this as hostility when it is the standard pedagogical register.

Grades come from the jury's combined score plus weekly tutor assessments, and a studio failure usually means repeating the semester rather than sitting a resit. Attendance is recorded, and missing two consecutive reviews is treated as abandoning the project.

How to read a blunt jury

Chinese reviews reward a clear parti and a legible drawing over a beautiful render, and a tutor who says the scheme is confused is giving you the most useful information of the term. Answer with the drawing, not with an explanation.

Cost, materials and the hidden studio budget

Tuition at the eight schools runs CNY 26,000–40,000 a year; provincial universities charge less. The number that surprises students is the studio budget: model materials, printing, plotter paper and site visits add CNY 1,500–3,000 a year, and a final thesis model can cost CNY 1,000 on its own.

Accommodation in a dormitory costs CNY 800–1,500 a month in Shanghai or Beijing and less elsewhere, and studio space is free, including an assigned desk that you will use more than your bed. Budget CNY 1,200–2,000 a month outside tuition in a first-tier city.

English-taught master's options

Master's study is the realistic entry point for students who do not want five years in Chinese. Tongji's College of Architecture and Urban Planning, Tsinghua's School of Architecture and Southeast University all run English-taught master's programmes in architecture, urban design or heritage conservation, usually two years and often with a studio taught in English by visiting faculty.

Requirements are a portfolio, a bachelor's degree in a design field and English proficiency; Chinese is not required for these tracks. Tuition sits between CNY 45,000 and 80,000 a year, and CSC funding is available at most of these schools.

How to apply

Applications for September entry close between December and March at most architecture schools, and a portfolio takes longer to assemble than the forms. The sequence below reflects where applicants actually lose time, which is almost never the portfolio itself but the sealed transcripts and the interview slot.

  1. Confirm the programme is accredited and five years long before anything else.
  2. Build the portfolio over three months, including process drawings and a sketchbook.
  3. Get transcripts and degree certificates translated and sealed.
  4. Prepare for an interview that will ask you to explain one project in detail.
  5. Submit by the December deadline, since portfolio review slots fill early.
  6. Apply for the X1 visa once the JW202 form arrives, allowing three weeks.

The strongest applicants treat the portfolio as the application and the forms as administration. That is also how the schools read them.
