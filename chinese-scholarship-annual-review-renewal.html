Keeping Your Chinese Scholarship: Annual Review and Renewal Rules

By ORIGIN Education · 2026-11-14

A Chinese Government Scholarship is awarded for one academic year at a time, with renewal decided by an annual review that most recipients only think about in the week it happens. Students who lose funding rarely lose it for a dramatic reason. They lose it because a course was failed twice, a dormitory violation was recorded, or a review form was filed without the transcript attached.

The Annual Review Is a Gate, Not a Formality

The annual review is a formal assessment conducted every year, usually between March and May, in which the university certifies whether a scholarship holder's academic performance and conduct justify continued funding. It is not a registration step: the committee can reduce, suspend or cancel the scholarship, and roughly one holder in twenty receives something other than a straightforward renewal.

The review is administered by the university's international student office and forwarded to the China Scholarship Council, which holds the final record. Because the university writes the recommendation, the file that matters is the one assembled on campus, not the one sent to Beijing.

When it happens

Timing follows the academic year rather than the calendar year. Reviews open in the spring semester, results are usually communicated before the summer holiday, and the new funding year starts in September. A student who leaves for the summer without checking the result can return to a suspended stipend.

Who Sits on the Review Committee and What They Read

The committee is normally chaired by a vice-president for international affairs and includes the international student office, the student's department and a representative of the graduate school. They read a one-page self-assessment, the official transcript for the year, the dormitory conduct record and any disciplinary file, in that order of weight.

Academic performance

Passing is the floor, not the standard. The committee looks for a pass rate above 70 percent of registered credits, an average grade that does not fall into the bottom quartile of the cohort, and evidence of progress on the thesis for research students. Failing one course is survivable; failing the same course twice is the single most common trigger for suspension.

Conduct and legal compliance

Conduct carries more weight than applicants expect. A recorded dormitory violation, an unexcused absence of more than two weeks, a police warning or any immigration infringement is reported by the university and read by the committee. So is a public statement that causes a disciplinary case, because the university is the body that has to answer for it.

The Four Possible Outcomes

The committee returns one of four results, and only the first is neutral. Continue, reduce, suspend and cancel are distinct outcomes with different financial consequences, and understanding all four in advance is what turns a suspension into a recoverable year instead of the end of a degree.

Continue

Full renewal keeps the tuition waiver, the dormitory place and the monthly stipend unchanged for the next academic year. Most holders receive this, and it requires nothing beyond a submitted form and a transcript.

Reduction

A reduction trims the stipend, usually by 20 to 50 percent for one year, while keeping tuition waived. It is used where performance dipped but the case for continued support remains, typically after one failed course or a semester of weak attendance.

Suspension and re-review

Suspension withdraws the stipend for twelve months while tuition usually stays waived. The holder is reviewed again the following year, and a satisfactory second review restores the stipend. This is the outcome most often confused with cancellation, and it is recoverable.

Cancellation

Cancellation ends the award. It applies to two consecutive failed reviews, falsified application documents, a criminal penalty, or withdrawal from study without approval. Once cancelled, the scholarship cannot be reinstated, and the student continues as a self-funded student or leaves.

What the Stipend Covers and What Stops

The package has three parts, and a suspension hits only one of them. Tuition, accommodation and insurance are almost always retained when the stipend stops, because those are paid to the university rather than to the student, so the loss is monthly cash rather than the whole award.

The stipend is paid monthly into a Chinese bank account, and payment pauses during the summer holiday at some universities, resuming with the autumn semester. A student relying on the stipend alone should hold two months of living costs as a buffer for exactly this reason.

Preparing the Review File

The review rewards preparation more than merit, because the committee reads what is in front of it and has a week to read forty files. Six documents cover almost every case, and assembling them in April is worth more than any argument made afterwards.

  1. The self-assessment form, typed rather than handwritten, with the year's courses and grades listed.
  2. The official transcript stamped by the registry, not a screenshot from the student portal.
  3. A one-page research progress note for master's and doctoral holders, signed by the supervisor.
  4. Certificates for any competition, publication or language test taken during the year.
  5. A short explanation of any failed course, with the remediation plan already registered.
  6. The dormitory conduct certificate, which the international office issues on request.

Submit early. Files arriving after the internal deadline are still reviewed, but they are reviewed by a committee that has already allocated its attention.

Appeals and Second Chances

A suspension can be appealed within the period stated in the result notice, usually ten to fifteen working days, and appeals succeed most often on procedural grounds: a grade recorded in error, a conduct report filed without the student being heard, or a medical absence undocumented at the time. The appeal goes to the university first and to the Scholarship Council only after the university has answered.

Two practical points are worth remembering. A medical certificate obtained after the fact is still evidence if it covers the dates in question, and a supervisor's letter carries weight that a student's own statement cannot. Neither guarantee a reversal, but both convert an appeal from an assertion into a file.

What Happens to Your Visa If the Scholarship Ends

Losing a scholarship does not cancel a residence permit. The permit is tied to student status, not to funding, so a student whose award is cancelled can normally continue studying as self-funded, provided the university confirms enrolment and the permit is renewed on that basis. The exception is withdrawal from study, which obliges the university to report the change and shortens the permit.

That distinction matters for planning. A cancelled scholarship is a financial emergency, not an immigration one, and the fees owed from that point are published on the university's website rather than negotiated. Students who understand the review rules in their first semester are the ones who never need the distinction.
