"Getting a Driving Licence in China as an International Student"

By ORIGIN Education · 2026-11-09

China is one of the few major economies where a foreign licence does not simply transfer, and the International Driving Permit that works in 150 countries is legally worthless here. Students who plan to drive must choose between a temporary permit for short stays and full conversion with a theory test, and both routes start at the same office.

Can international students drive in China on a foreign licence?

Not on the foreign licence alone. China requires either a Chinese driving licence or a temporary driving permit issued by a vehicle management office, and a foreign licence only supports the application for one. Driving unconverted counts as unlicensed driving, carrying fines of RMB 200-2,000 and detention of up to 15 days.

The rule applies to every holder of a student residence permit, however many years you have driven at home, and there is no grace period after arrival. Rental companies check: no Chinese licence or temporary permit, no keys.

Why the International Driving Permit does not work

The International Driving Permit is issued under the 1949 Geneva and 1968 Vienna conventions, and mainland China is party to neither, so the booklet has no standing with Chinese traffic police. Agencies selling an "IDP for China" are selling a translation, and a translation is not what an officer at a checkpoint asks to see.

What does work is a notarised Chinese translation of your home licence, which many vehicle management offices accept inside a conversion application. It lets the office read your licence; it does not let you drive on it. Expect RMB 100-300 at a notary public, and confirm first, because some cities translate in-house.

The temporary driving permit: three months, no test

A temporary driving permit is issued to foreign licence holders staying for a short period and is valid for up to three months from the date of issue. You apply in person with your passport, visa or residence permit, home licence and translation, a medical certificate and two photographs, and most offices issue it the same day.

The permit mirrors the vehicle category on your home licence and cannot be extended past the three-month ceiling: you either leave, or convert. For a semester with a weekend rental car it is the fast route, and the fee is nominal, usually under RMB 100.

Converting your foreign licence: the theory test route

Holders of a valid foreign licence converting to a Chinese C1 or C2 licence sit only Subject 1, the computer-based theory test, and skip the practical subjects that Chinese learner drivers spend months on. The exemption exists because your home licence already proves you can drive; the test checks that you know Chinese traffic law, signs and penalty points.

You need a residence permit with enough validity remaining, generally at least 90 days in most cities, a medical certificate from a designated clinic, your passport, your home licence and a translation. Apply at the vehicle management office for the district where you are registered as living. The card is valid six years and renewable.

Documents to bring

  • Passport with a valid student residence permit
  • Home driving licence, original, plus a Chinese translation
  • Medical certificate from a designated hospital or clinic
  • Two passport photographs with a white background
  • Registration form of temporary residence, where still required

What happens on the day

Book through the local traffic police app or at the counter, pay the fee, and sit a 45-minute exam of 100 multiple-choice questions. The pass mark is 90 out of 100, and English, French, Japanese, Korean, Russian and Spanish versions are available in most provinces. Failures retake after a few days.

What the Subject 1 theory test covers

The exam is drawn from a bank of roughly 1,000 questions on signs, right-of-way, speed limits, penalty points, emergency handling and driver obligations. Chinese law is stricter than most students expect on points and fines, and the questions test numbers rather than principles, so working through the question bank is the practical strategy.

Every driver starts each 12-month cycle with 12 points. Reach 12 and the licence is suspended until you sit the theory test again, so for a one-year student a single drink-driving stop ends the driving plan for the rest of the degree.

Costs, timelines and where to apply

The conversion route costs RMB 300-700 in total: medical examination about RMB 50, translation and notary RMB 100-300, test fee under RMB 100 and the card about RMB 10. A driving-school course from scratch costs RMB 3,000-6,000 and three to six months, which is why almost no student takes that path.

Timelines vary by city. Where there is a foreigner service window, as in Shanghai, Beijing and Guangzhou, the process takes one to three weeks including the test booking. Smaller cities may handle a handful of foreign conversions a year, so allow a month and call ahead about documents.

Is it worth it for a one-year master's?

Usually no. A Chinese licence matters if you will stay to work, buy a car, or travel regularly beyond metro coverage, and it reads well on a CV for roles with site visits. For one academic year in a campus city with metro and ride-hailing, the temporary permit covers weekend trips at almost no cost.

When to start

Begin in your first semester, while the residence permit still has more than a year to run, because offices reject applications with too little validity left. A week of practice passes the theory test, the paperwork is one morning, and the result is a six-year licence that doubles as a second photo ID.

Rules of the road that catch foreign drivers

Traffic keeps right, and right turns on red are legal unless a sign or arrow forbids them, which surprises drivers from Europe and reassures drivers from North America. Speed cameras are dense, including average-speed sections on expressways that measure your speed over several kilometres rather than at one point.

Limits are 120 km/h on expressways, 100 km/h on national roads and 30-60 km/h in built-up areas. Licences from jurisdictions with mutual recognition agreements, which include France and Hong Kong, convert without the theory test, so check that list before booking anything.
