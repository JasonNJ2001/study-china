Business Schools in China for International Students

By ORIGIN Education · 2026-11-03

China has several hundred institutions offering business degrees to international students and perhaps a dozen that a serious recruiter will recognise. The gap between them is not teaching quality in the abstract. It is accreditation, the composition of the cohort, and whether the career office has relationships with the companies you want to work for.

Sorting the two groups takes about twenty minutes if you know what to check.

Check accreditation before anything else

Three international accreditations matter in business education: AACSB, EQUIS and AMBA. Schools holding all three are described as triple-accredited, and the list in China is short. Around fifteen schools hold AACSB, fewer hold EQUIS, and the overlap is smaller still.

AccreditationWhat it certifiesWhy it matters to you
AACSBInstitutional and programme quality, faculty research outputRecognised by employers and by doctoral admissions worldwide
EQUISEuropean-standard institutional review, internationalisationSignals a genuinely international faculty and student body
AMBAMBA-specific programme reviewOnly relevant if you are doing an MBA

A degree from an unaccredited programme is not worthless, but it is not portable. It may be well regarded locally and unrecognised outside China, which is a problem if your plan involves working anywhere else afterwards.

MBA, MSc or MiM

Three different products are sold under similar names, and choosing the wrong one wastes a year.

The MBA requires work experience, usually three years minimum and five on average, and teaches general management through cases and cohort work. The MSc in Management or in a specialism such as finance or business analytics admits fresh graduates and is more quantitative. The MiM is a pre-experience general management degree, common in Europe and offered by a smaller number of Chinese schools.

Which one fits which applicant

  • Two or more years of work experience, aiming for a management role: MBA.
  • Final-year student or recent graduate, quantitative background: MSc in finance, analytics or supply chain.
  • Recent graduate from a non-business background wanting a general commercial education: MiM or a broad MSc.

The classification also determines funding. MBA scholarships for international students are more common than MSc scholarships, because schools compete for candidates with work experience.

What it costs

Tuition for English-taught business programmes varies by an order of magnitude between schools, and the most expensive is not always the most useful.

Living costs add 60,000 to 110,000 yuan for two years in Beijing or Shanghai, and roughly half that in a tier-two city. A few schools offer full-tuition scholarships to international candidates, usually on the strength of GMAT score and work history rather than need.

Entry requirements

GMAT, GRE and the alternatives

Top-tier MBA programmes expect a GMAT of 650 or above from international applicants, with 700 or above typical for scholarship consideration. Some schools accept GRE, and a growing number accept their own entrance examination or a waiver for candidates with strong professional records. MSc programmes in finance and analytics often prefer GRE quantitative scores to GMAT.

Language: IELTS 6.5 or TOEFL 90 is the common floor, with 7.0 and 100 typical at the top schools. Applicants who studied their previous degree in English can usually obtain a waiver.

The interview and the essay

Chinese business schools weight the interview heavily, more than European schools of similar rank. Expect questions on why China specifically, not why a master's degree. An answer that could be given about any country is the most common reason a strong file is rejected.

The career outcome, realistically

This is where the marketing and the market diverge.

For Chinese-language roles in Chinese companies, a Mandarin-speaking graduate from a good local school is competitive. For roles in multinationals based in China, the school's brand and the alumni network matter more than the curriculum, and both are concentrated in a handful of programmes in Beijing and Shanghai.

For roles outside China, a Chinese MBA carries less weight than a European or American one from a similarly ranked school. Its value is specific: it signals China market familiarity, which is valuable to companies with China exposure and largely irrelevant to those without.

What the alumni network is actually for

In Chinese business schools the cohort is the product. Case discussions in a room where half the students have worked at manufacturers, platforms and state-owned enterprises produce a view of how Chinese business operates that no lecture delivers. Choose a programme by the profile of its recent cohort, published in the class profile, rather than by its ranking position.

Timeline for a September 2027 start

  • September to November 2026: shortlist six schools, check accreditation, book GMAT.
  • December 2026 to February 2027: submit round one applications, which carry better scholarship odds.
  • March to April 2027: interviews, offers, scholarship decisions.
  • May to July 2027: deposit, tuition, JW202 form, visa.
  • September 2027: enrolment and Mandarin placement test.

Who should not do this

Anyone whose career plan does not involve China should think carefully. The degree's premium is geographic. If you want a general management qualification for a career in Europe, Africa or Latin America, a Chinese MBA is a detour with a high price tag, and the exchange rate of its reputation does not travel as far as the tuition suggests.

For those whose plan does involve China, the calculation is different and often favourable: two years of Mandarin, a cohort of future business partners, and a credential recognised by every employer in the market you intend to work in. That combination is hard to assemble any other way.
