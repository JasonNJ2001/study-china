Online Shopping and Parcel Delivery in China: A Student's Guide

By ORIGIN Education · 2026-11-23

Online shopping in China is faster and cheaper than most new arrivals expect, and the delivery model is different from anything in Europe or North America. Parcels rarely reach your door: they land in a pickup station with a six-digit code, and if you ignore the message for a few days the parcel goes back to the seller.

The three platforms students actually use

Taobao and Tmall carry the widest range and the highest counterfeit risk; JD.com sells its own stock of electronics with next-day delivery; Pinduoduo is cheapest for dormitory supplies and small appliances. Most students end up with all three apps plus a Chinese bank card, and pay through Alipay or WeChat Pay.

What each is genuinely best at

Taobao wins on choice and niche sellers, including tailored dorm bedding. JD wins on anything with a warranty, because returns are handled by JD rather than by a third-party seller. Pinduoduo wins on price for items where brand does not matter, such as hangers, storage boxes and desk lamps.

Paying without a Chinese bank card

Alipay and WeChat Pay both accept foreign Visa and Mastercard. Alipay charges a 3% service fee on single transactions above CNY 200, and smaller payments are fee-free, so splitting a purchase or using the wallet balance avoids the charge. Opening a Chinese account with your passport and residence permit takes about an hour and removes the fee entirely.

The account worth opening in week one

Bank of China and ICBC both run campus branches in September, and the queue is shorter in the second week. Bring your passport, admission letter, residence permit and a Chinese phone number; the card arrives by post within a week.

Parcel stations, pickup codes and the three-day rule

Parcels go to a Cainiao station (菜鸟驿站) or a campus delivery centre rather than to your room. You get a six-digit pickup code by text or in the app, and uncollected parcels are usually returned to the sender after three to five days, so check your phone during exam weeks.

Why your parcel is not at your dormitory

Couriers are paid per delivery, and a station that accepts 400 parcels in one drop is far cheaper than 400 door visits. Campus security also bars courier motorcycles from dormitory areas at most universities, so the station is the only legal endpoint.

What to do when the code does not arrive

Call the courier number in the order page rather than the platform hotline, and quote the order number. Most missing parcels are sitting at the wrong station on the same campus, and the courier can move the record in a minute.

Delivery speed and the two sale seasons

JD's own logistics delivers within 24 hours in most provincial capitals and 48 hours elsewhere. Taobao sellers ship in two to five days, and everything slows during the two national sale periods: the 11 November Singles' Day event, which Alibaba started in 2009, and the 18 June anniversary sale that JD built.

Ordering around the sales

Discounts are real but delivery queues are longer, so order essentials two weeks before a sale and use the sale for anything you can wait for. A mattress ordered on 10 November may arrive after the month ends.

Returns, refunds and the seven-day rule

Chinese consumer law gives buyers seven days to return most online purchases without giving a reason, provided the item is unused and the packaging is intact. The clock starts when the parcel is delivered, and the return shipping is yours unless the seller pays for it or you bought return insurance (运费险) for CNY 1 to 3.

What cannot be returned

Customised items, fresh food, opened cosmetics, digital downloads and anything marked as final sale. Check the 7天无理由 label on the product page before ordering, because not every listing carries it.

How the refund actually arrives

Money returns to the original payment method, usually within 24 hours of the seller accepting the return and three to seven days for a foreign card. Keep the return tracking number until the refund posts.

Sending things home at the end of term

SF Express International and China Post both ship boxes abroad, and a 20-kilogram sea parcel to Europe costs far less than air freight but takes six to eight weeks. Book in the last week of June, before the graduation rush fills the collection points.

A five-step setup for your first week

Do these in order during your first week and the rest of the year runs itself, because each step unlocks the next one: a phone number before a bank account, a bank account before a wallet, a wallet before an order. Skipping a step is why new students end up paying cash for everything.

  1. Buy a Chinese SIM card and register it in your own name, since payment apps need it.
  2. Open a bank account and link it to Alipay and WeChat Pay.
  3. Set your delivery address to the campus station, not the dormitory building.
  4. Turn on delivery notifications, because the pickup code is time-limited.
  5. Buy one cheap item first to test the address before ordering anything expensive.

A student who does these five things in the first week spends the rest of the term buying what they need instead of hunting for a parcel that went back to Guangzhou.
