Roommate Conflicts and Changing Dormitories in China

By ORIGIN Education · 2026-11-25

Most international students in China are assigned a roommate before they arrive, on the basis of nationality, gender and programme, and most of those pairings work. The ones that do not follow a predictable arc: irritation in week three, a conversation that never happens because neither side wants to seem difficult, and a request for a room change in week ten that arrives after the office has already allocated every spare bed.

Most dorm conflicts are settled by the building manager

The first person with real authority is not your professor or the international office. It is the building manager, the staff member who sits at the entrance desk, holds the spare keys, runs the room inspections and knows which beds are empty. A calm conversation at that desk resolves more disputes than an email to the dean.

Who has authority over what

The building manager controls room allocation within the building and can authorise a temporary bed if a situation is genuinely unsafe. The international students office approves permanent swaps and updates your registration record. Your class teacher, the counsellor who handles your cohort, mediates and can escalate to the department. Going straight to the top without the first two steps usually delays things.

The conversation that actually works

Name the specific behaviour, not the person: noise after midnight, guests staying overnight, food left in the shared fridge. Ask for a concrete change and a date. Chinese dormitory culture treats shared space as genuinely shared, so a request framed around fairness lands better than one framed around personal preference.

When talking stops working: the formal swap request

A room change is a written request to the international students office, supported by the building manager, and it is granted against a free bed rather than against the strength of your argument. In practice that means timing matters more than the reason. Requests submitted after allocation week are usually deferred to the next semester.

The two swap windows

The realistic windows are the first two weeks of the semester, when no-shows and late arrivals leave gaps, and the two weeks before the semester ends, when students who are graduating or leaving give notice. Outside those windows a swap needs an empty bed in the same building, and empty beds in popular buildings are rare.

What to put in the request

Write one page: your name and student number, the room, the problem in behavioural terms, what you have already tried, and the specific outcome you want, whether that is a different roommate, a different room or a single room. Attach the building manager's note. Requests that specify a preferred room type are processed faster than open-ended ones.

What a room actually costs

Prices are set by the university and by the building's age and facilities, so a shared room in a 1990s block can cost a fifth of a single room in a new international apartment. The figures below are the common range at provincial universities and are quoted per person per academic year.

The rules you cannot negotiate

Dormitory regulations are fire-safety rules first and lifestyle rules second, and appeals against them rarely succeed because the university carries legal liability for what happens inside the building. The six rules below apply in almost every international student dormitory, and the penalties attach to the room rather than to the individual who broke them.

  • High-wattage appliances such as kettles, induction cookers and space heaters are banned, and inspections are unannounced.
  • Curfew is typically 23:00 to 23:30; the gate locks and the network may shut off.
  • Overnight guests are not permitted, and repeated violations reach your student record.
  • Rooms are inspected once or twice a semester, and damage is charged at replacement cost.
  • Men and women are housed in separate buildings, without exception.
  • Smoking indoors is prohibited and is treated as a fire-safety matter.

A violation is recorded against the room, not only the person, which is why a roommate who keeps a kettle can put your deposit at risk as well.

Moving day logistics

Once a swap is approved, three things have to happen in order, and skipping the middle one is a common and avoidable error. First, hand back the key and the card for the old room and get the inspection signed. Second, register the new address.

Chinese rules require foreign residents to register a change of residence with the local police station within 24 hours of moving. In campus housing the university usually files this for you, but you must confirm it was filed, because an unregistered address can surface months later during a residence permit renewal.

Third, move your registration in the dormitory system and check that the new room appears on your campus card. If the card is not updated, the gate reader at the new building will refuse you at eleven at night, which is exactly when you discover the problem.

What to do in the first 48 hours of a conflict

  1. Write down what happened, with dates and times.
  2. Speak to the roommate once, directly, about the specific behaviour.
  3. If nothing changes in a week, tell the building manager and ask for a mediation meeting.
  4. If the issue is safety, harassment or theft, escalate the same day to the international office and the campus security office.
  5. Ask for a room change in writing, with a preferred building and room type.
  6. Keep a copy of every message and note the name of every person you speak to.

Moving rooms is a normal administrative request in Chinese universities, not a complaint about the institution, and students who treat it that way get a decision in days rather than weeks.
