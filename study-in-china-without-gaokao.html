Admission to Chinese Universities Without the Gaokao

By ORIGIN Education · 2026-11-24

Every year a European or African applicant reads that a Chinese university "requires the gaokao" and stops. It is a misunderstanding with a specific origin: the same university publishes one admission page for Chinese citizens and another for foreign passport holders.

Why the gaokao does not apply to you

The gaokao is the national entrance examination for Chinese citizens applying to domestic universities, and about 13.4 million candidates sat it in 2024. Foreign passport holders are admitted through a separate review channel, so you can study in China without gaokao scores at essentially every university, including the most selective ones.

What replaces it: review-based admission

Chinese universities admit international undergraduates by document review, sometimes called the shenhe system, followed by an interview or an internal written test at competitive institutions. The review weighs your school transcript, diploma, language certificate, personal statement and references; there is no national score to clear and no provincial cut-off to meet.

What the review actually reads

Three documents carry most of the decision. The transcript, because reviewers compare your grades against your own school's grading scale rather than against a Chinese standard. The personal statement, because it is the only place you explain why this subject and this city. And the language certificate, because it predicts whether you will survive the first semester.

Where entrance tests still exist

A small group of universities runs its own examination for international applicants, usually in mathematics and English, and a larger group interviews every shortlisted candidate online. Peking University, Tsinghua University and Fudan University all interview, with deadlines in December or early January for a September start.

Language: the real filter

The language requirement eliminates more applicants than any academic criterion. Chinese-taught degrees generally ask for HSK 4 with a score of at least 180, humanities and law faculties usually ask for HSK 5, and a handful of programmes accept HSK 3 conditional on one year of preparatory Chinese.

Chinese-taught programmes

HSK 5 is roughly 2,500 vocabulary items and the ability to read a newspaper with a dictionary. Students who reach HSK 4 at home often spend a preparatory year in China before the degree, which is a normal route rather than a failure.

English-taught programmes

Bachelor programmes taught entirely in English exist in engineering, business, medicine and computer science, and their entry standard is IELTS 6.0 or TOEFL 80. A letter confirming that your secondary education was in English is accepted by many universities, but not by the ones that rank highest.

Standardised tests: when SAT, A-Level or IB help

Chinese universities do not require SAT, A-Level or IB results, and a file without them is complete. But at selective universities they change how your transcript is read: an A-Level grade in mathematics tells an engineering faculty more than a school report written in an unfamiliar system.

  • A-Level: three subjects at C or above is a strong signal; mathematics and physics are read closely for engineering.
  • IB: a total of 30 or above with higher-level subjects matching the intended major.
  • SAT: around 1200 or above is treated as adequate evidence for business and social science programmes.
  • AP: two or three subject scores are accepted but rarely decisive on their own.

None of these substitute for the language certificate. A 1450 SAT score with no HSK 4 does not get you into a Chinese-taught programme.

The timeline for a September start

Applications for the September intake open between October and December of the previous year, and the funded and selective programmes close first. Most universities accept rolling applications until April or May, and a few keep places open into July for programmes that did not fill.

December to January

This is the window for the universities that interview, for scholarship nominations, and for anything in medicine or design. Missing it usually means applying to a less selective institution rather than waiting a year, which is a worse outcome than it sounds only if prestige was the goal.

February to April

The main round. Documents are verified, admission letters are issued, and the JW202 form that you need for the visa application is generated. Allow four to six weeks between acceptance and receiving the JW202, because it is issued by the provincial education department, not by the university.

Where applicants get rejected

Rejections at review-based universities are rarely about grades. They are about documents that fail verification, and the failure is usually visible before submission: an unregistered school, an unexplained gap, a portfolio that arrives after the review round has closed. None of these can be argued with afterwards, because no appeal stage exists.

The diploma that is not recognised

A notarised copy is not the same as a verified one. Chinese universities check whether your school is recognised by the education authority in your country, and a diploma from an unregistered institution cannot be notarised in a way that satisfies them.

The gap year with no explanation

A gap of more than one year between graduation and application requires a paragraph explaining what you did. Reviewers do not penalise work, military service or language study, but an unexplained gap reads as an incomplete file.

The programme that requires a portfolio

Architecture, design, fine art and music require work samples, and the portfolio deadline is often earlier than the academic one. A portfolio submitted after the review round has closed is not evaluated, however strong it is.

Choosing universities for a review-based file

Weak applications are usually strong files sent to mismatched institutions, so the list matters as much as the file. Something has to keep it honest: the teaching language first, ambition levels spread across the list, and paperwork timed to the visa appointment rather than to the offer.

  1. Check whether the programme is taught in Chinese or English first, because that decides your preparation timeline.
  2. Apply to two ambitious universities, three matched ones and one safe one; the review system makes a wide list cheap.
  3. Confirm in writing that the JW202 can be issued before your visa appointment, and keep notarised scans valid six months ahead.

The gaokao is not a gate you failed to clear. It is an examination for a different population, and the channel open to you rewards preparation of a different kind: documents that verify, language that is certified, and an application filed before the round closes rather than after.
