Using Your Campus Card in China: Meals, Printing, Laundry and Top-Ups

By ORIGIN Education · 2026-11-17

Your campus card, or 一卡通, is issued on registration day at universities across China and is the single object that connects you to food, water, laundry, printing, library loans and dormitory doors. It is also the easiest system on a Chinese campus to misunderstand, because top-up methods, spending caps and replacement rules differ from university to university.

What the Campus Card Actually Is

The 一卡通 is a stored-value smart card tied to your student number, not a bank card and not a credit line. Money is loaded onto it in advance and deducted at the point of use, offline in most canteens. Because the balance lives on the card rather than in an account, a lost card means the money on it is at risk until you report it.

What is printed on the card

The front carries your name, student number, photograph, faculty and the card number. The number matters more than the photograph: canteen tills, library gates and dormitory readers all identify you by card number, and staff will check the photograph only in exams or when the card is used by someone else.

One card, several systems

Technically the card holds separate purses. The canteen purse pays for food, the water-control purse pays for hot showers by the minute, and the library system reads the card as a borrower ID. Topping up the canteen purse does not necessarily top up the water purse, which is why students who only use the recharge machine still run out of hot water.

Where the Card Is Accepted

A campus card covers more of daily life than most new students expect, and almost none of it can be paid for in cash on a Chinese campus. Canteens stopped taking cash years ago, and dormitory hot water is metered through the card rather than billed monthly. The list below is typical rather than universal, but you should assume every item exists somewhere:

  • Canteen meals, including breakfast stalls and late-night windows
  • Dormitory hot water, charged by the minute through a wall reader
  • Laundry rooms, usually ¥3–5 per wash and ¥2 per dry
  • Library borrowing, room booking and printing machines
  • Campus buses and shuttle routes between campuses
  • Gym entry and some sports facility bookings
  • The university clinic, for registration fees and basic medicines
  • Printing and photocopying shops inside the campus walls
  • Exam registration and re-sit fees at some universities
  • Dormitory building doors and room locks

How to Top Up Without Queueing

Recharge happens at self-service machines in canteens and dormitory lobbies, through the university WeChat account, or through Alipay's campus services. The machines take banknotes of ¥50 and ¥100 and give no change, which is why the first week of term produces a queue of students buying bottled water to break a ¥100 note.

The WeChat route

Most universities now bind the card to an official WeChat account or mini-program, where you can recharge, freeze the card and check the balance. Binding requires the card number plus the last digits of your passport number or your student ID, depending on the university. Do this in your first week: it is the only way to freeze the card at 11 pm when you realise it is missing.

The cash ceiling

Self-service machines typically accept a maximum balance of ¥1,000 per card. That ceiling exists to limit losses, and it is also a useful budgeting tool: students who keep roughly a week of canteen spending on the card and the rest in a bank account lose very little when a card disappears.

What Daily Life Costs on the Card

Canteen prices are the reason most international students cook less than they expected. A subsidised university canteen serves a plate of two dishes and rice for ¥8–15, and a bowl of noodles for ¥6–12. Losing the Card: Report, Replace, Recover

Report the loss immediately through the WeChat mini-program or the self-service machine, which freezes the balance within minutes. Replacement is issued at the campus card centre, normally costs ¥20–30, and takes between one hour and three working days depending on whether the card is printed on site. The old balance is transferred to the new card, but only after the freeze is processed.

What you need at the counter

Bring your passport and your student ID, and expect to fill in a one-page form. Some universities require a letter from your faculty confirming you are enrolled. Photograph the counter's opening hours on your first visit; many campus card centres close for lunch between 11:30 and 13:30, which is exactly when students discover the card is gone.

If the card was stolen

Report it to the campus security office (保卫处) as well as freezing it, and ask for a written record. Most universities will not investigate a stolen card unless the loss is reported to security, and the canteen transaction log can show where and when the balance was spent, which is often enough to identify the user through camera footage.

The Digital Campus Card

Since roughly 2020 most universities have added an electronic card inside WeChat Work (企业微信) or a campus app, showing a QR code that the canteen till can scan. It works for meals, library gates and building doors, but not everywhere: hot water readers and older laundry machines still require the physical card, and exam halls require the plastic version.

Keep both working

Students who rely only on the phone get stuck at the shower reader or at an exam door. The practical arrangement is to keep the physical card in a wallet and the digital card on the phone, and to leave a small cash balance on the card rather than the maximum, so a loss costs little while the digital backup covers meals.
