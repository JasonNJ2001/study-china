Study in China Without IELTS: Which Universities Accept It

By ORIGIN Education · 2026-10-19

Most international applicants treat IELTS as an unavoidable cost of studying abroad. In China it is frequently optional. To study in China without IELTS, you only need to establish one thing: the language your degree is actually taught in. Get that right and a large number of programmes open up with no test at all.

This guide covers the routes that actually work, the paperwork that gets rejected, and how to confirm the rule for your own intake before you pay for a test.

Why the requirement exists, and why it bends

An English-taught degree in China is audited on the assumption that students can follow lectures, write coursework and defend a thesis in English. Universities therefore need evidence. An IELTS band is the easiest evidence for an admissions officer to read, so it becomes the default line in the brochure. But the underlying requirement is proof of English proficiency, not an IELTS certificate specifically. Most international offices accept several forms of proof and print only the most familiar one on the website.

Chinese-taught programmes are a separate track

If the degree is taught in Chinese, IELTS is not part of the file at all. The language gate is HSK: usually HSK 4 for bachelor's programmes, HSK 5 for master's, and higher for medicine, law and Chinese literature. Applicants routinely spend six months preparing for IELTS when their intended programme only ever wanted an HSK 4 certificate.

English-taught programmes are where the waiver lives

Here the requirement is genuine but the evidence is negotiable. A waiver is granted by the university, not by a ministry, which is why two universities in the same city can give different answers to the same question. Always ask the specific programme, never the faculty homepage.

Four ways to meet the English requirement without IELTS

The four routes below cover almost every case of studying in China without IELTS. Each one replaces the certificate with a different kind of verifiable evidence.

  1. Medium of instruction letter. The most common route. Your previous school confirms on letterhead that your degree was taught entirely in English. It must name the degree, the dates, and the language of instruction, and carry a stamp plus a contact for verification.
  2. A prior degree from an English-medium institution. If you already studied in an English-taught programme abroad, the degree certificate and transcript usually serve as proof on their own.
  3. The university's own test or interview. Many provincial universities run an internal placement test or a short online interview for applicants who cannot supply a score. It is rarely advertised; ask for it by name.
  4. An alternative standardised test. TOEFL, PTE, Duolingo and Cambridge English are accepted at a growing number of schools. A minority still refuse anything except IELTS, so this is a question to ask, not an assumption.

Treat the table as the start of a conversation rather than a policy document. Requirements are set per programme and revised between intakes, so the 2027 catalogue can differ from 2026 even at the same university.

The MOI letter: where most rejections happen

A waiver that looks available on paper fails on the letter itself. The usual causes:

  • The letter is on plain paper with no letterhead, stamp or registrar contact, so it cannot be verified.
  • It says English is widely used at the institution instead of stating that the degree was taught entirely in English.
  • It covers one year of a multi-year programme, which leaves the rest unproven.
  • It arrives in a third language with no translation.
  • It names a department that no longer exists, or a signatory who has left.

A single page with the registrar's direct email and an institutional stamp resolves nearly all of these. If your school will not issue one, ask the department head for a signed statement and attach your transcript as supporting evidence. Translation and notarisation are required if the document is not in English or Chinese.

What to do when a university insists on a score

  • Ask whether conditional admission is available. Some universities admit you to a one-year Chinese language programme first, with the degree place held.
  • Request the internal English test explicitly. Say that you cannot sit IELTS in your country within the deadline and ask what alternative the programme accepts.
  • Check foundation or preparatory years, which often carry a lower language threshold than direct entry.
  • Plan the test early if it turns out to be unavoidable. Computer-delivered IELTS returns results in three to five days, paper-based in about thirteen, and booking slots in some countries run four to six weeks out.

A document checklist for an IELTS-free application

  • Passport valid for at least six months beyond the intended graduation date
  • Highest diploma and full transcripts, notarised
  • Medium of instruction letter or alternative English proof
  • HSK certificate, if applying to a Chinese-taught programme
  • Study plan and academic CV
  • Two recommendation letters on institutional letterhead
  • Foreigner Physical Examination Form completed within six months
  • Non-criminal record certificate, where the province requires one
  • Application fee receipt, kept as proof of submission

How to confirm the rule before you pay for a test

Open the programme page for the specific intake year rather than the faculty homepage. Write to the international students office, not the general admissions inbox, and ask a closed question: for the September 2027 intake of this programme, is a medium of instruction letter accepted in place of IELTS for an applicant of my nationality. A question that can be answered yes or no gets answered; an open one gets a link to the brochure you already read.

Keep the written reply. Attach it to the application and keep a copy for the visa stage, where an officer may ask why no test score appears in your file. It is also worth checking whether an intergovernmental cultural agreement covers your country, since some applicants are exempt from language testing under bilateral programmes, though the university may still interview them.

Bottom line

IELTS is a cost and a delay, not a legal condition of entry. If your degree is taught in Chinese, skip it and prepare for HSK. If it is taught in English, get written confirmation that an MOI letter or an internal test is accepted, and start that exchange three to four months before the deadline, because the answer decides whether your file is complete at all. Most rejections in this category are not about ability; they are about a letter nobody checked before the deadline closed.
