Winter Heating in Northern and Southern China: What Students Should Expect

By ORIGIN Education · 2026-11-23

Choosing a Chinese university by temperature is a mistake, because the thermometer and the indoor experience disagree. North of the Qinling-Huai line, buildings are heated to 18°C or more all winter; south of it, the same January outdoor temperature produces 9°C inside a classroom. What you pack, and what you pay, depends on which side of that line your campus sits.

Where central heating stops

Central heating in China follows a boundary drawn along the Qinling Mountains and the Huai River in the 1950s, roughly the January 5°C isotherm. Cities north of it receive piped hot water from mid-November to mid-March. Cities south of it, including Nanjing, Wuhan, Hangzhou, Changsha and Shanghai, receive nothing, however cold the winter turns.

The line was a Soviet-inspired planning decision made when coal was scarce: heat the places where pipes would not freeze, and let the rest manage. It has survived seven decades of debate, and every winter brings a new round of proposals to move it. For a student choosing a city, assume the line is still where it was in 1957.

The cities that straddle it

Xuzhou and Nanjing sit within 300 kilometres of each other and on opposite sides of the practical boundary. Xuzhou has central heating; Nanjing does not. Applicants who compare only latitude miss this entirely.

What northern winter feels like indoors

North of the line, indoor temperatures are regulated and dormitories are heated to 20°C to 24°C, so students wear T-shirts inside while it is minus 10 outside. Beijing's official heating season runs from 15 November to 15 March, and municipal rules require living rooms and bedrooms to stay at or above 18°C. The real shock is dryness and the walk between buildings, not the classroom.

Harbin runs a longer season

Harbin's heating season starts around 20 October and ends around 20 April, six months of piped heat, because the January average is close to minus 18°C. Dormitory windows are double-glazed and usually sealed, which keeps the heat in and the fresh air out.

Who pays for dormitory heating

Northern university dormitories normally include heating in the room fee, or charge CNY 300 to 800 for the season on top of an annual room fee of CNY 800 to 1,200. Off-campus flats bill the tenant by floor area at CNY 20 to 30 per square metre for the whole season, so a 60-square-metre flat costs CNY 1,200 to 1,800 in a single autumn payment.

The bill arrives before the cold

Heating fees are collected in October or November, before the season starts, and landlords usually ask for the whole season at once. Budget for it in your first autumn rather than your first cold snap.

Southern winter: 9°C indoors and no escape

In Nanjing, Wuhan, Hangzhou and Changsha, indoor temperatures of 8°C to 12°C are normal in January, because there is no central heating and the buildings are designed to shed heat rather than keep it. Students warm single rooms with split air-conditioners at roughly CNY 0.5 to 0.6 per kilowatt-hour, and the electricity bill becomes the winter budget.

Why 5°C in Nanjing feels worse than minus 5°C in Beijing

Humidity. Southern winters run at 70% to 85% relative humidity, so cold air conducts heat away from skin faster, and there is no dry indoor refuge to return to. Damp bedding and unheated bathrooms are the two complaints that dominate student forums every December.

What southern students actually buy

An electric blanket rated under 800 watts, a hot-water bottle, thick slippers, a dehumidifier if the room allows it, and a small oil-filled radiator. Most dormitories ban appliances above roughly 1,000 watts because the circuit breaker for a room is sized for laptops and kettles.

The dormitory rules that surprise people

High-wattage heaters, induction cookers and electric kettles above the room's rating trip the breaker, and repeated trips are reported to the dormitory office. Ask the building manager for the room's limit in watts before you buy anything.

What to pack for each zone

Packing for the wrong zone is the cheapest mistake to avoid, because almost everything on this list can be bought in China for less than the excess baggage fee. Read your campus row in the table first, then pack for the room you will sleep in rather than for the city on the map.

  1. North: one heavy coat, thermal base layers, lip balm, a humidifier, moisturiser.
  2. South: a waterproof windproof shell, several mid-layers, wool socks, an electric blanket, slippers.
  3. Both: an umbrella, because winter rain in the south and snow in the north both arrive sideways.
  4. Neither: a down jacket rated for the Arctic, unless you are going to Harbin.

Buying in China instead

Uniqlo, Decathlon and Taobao cover all of it, and a full winter kit in Nanjing costs CNY 600 to 1,000. Arrive with one warm layer and buy the rest after you have seen your room, because the room tells you more than the city's climate chart.
