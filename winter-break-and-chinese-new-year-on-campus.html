Winter Break and Chinese New Year on a Chinese Campus

By ORIGIN Education · 2026-11-12

Winter break at a Chinese university runs four to six weeks between the January examination period and the start of spring classes, and it is scheduled around the Spring Festival rather than around Christmas. That single fact explains almost everything that surprises new students: why the dates move every year, why the campus empties completely, and why the canteen you rely on may shut for two weeks.

When the Winter Break Actually Falls

Chinese universities run a two-semester academic year. The first semester ends with examinations in the first half of January, the break fills late January and most of February, and spring classes typically resume in the last week of February or the first week of March. The 2027 Spring Festival falls on 6 February, which puts most universities on a reopen date around 22 to 28 February.

Why the date moves every year

The Spring Festival is a lunar new year, so its Gregorian date shifts between 21 January and 20 February. Universities publish their calendar a year ahead, but the vacation start and end move with the lunar date, which is why a friend in another city may have two more weeks of holiday than you do.

Read your own calendar, not a forum post

Every university publishes a 校历 (academic calendar) as a PDF, usually on the registrar's site, showing teaching weeks, examination weeks, vacation dates and the make-up teaching days that follow public holidays. Download it in September. Guidebook dates and forum answers are routinely a year out of date.

Spring Festival Is the Reason the Break Is Long

The Spring Festival anchors the entire holiday. Around it sits chunyun, the 40-day travel period during which the country moves: hundreds of millions of people travel to family homes, rail and air capacity runs at its annual peak, and cities like Shenzhen and Guangzhou briefly lose a large share of their population. Universities schedule the break so students and staff can make that journey.

The two dates that matter

The festival itself, 6 February 2027, and the Lantern Festival on 20 February, fifteen days later, which closes the holiday season. Campus life stays quiet until the second of those dates has passed.

What the campus sounds like

Fireworks and firecracker bans apply in most urban districts and on most campuses, so the noise you may expect is often absent. What replaces it is empty roads, closed shops and a very quiet library — pleasant for two weeks, unsettling by the fourth.

What Happens to the Dormitory If You Stay

Many universities close or consolidate dormitory blocks during the break. Students who want to stay must submit a written application, usually called 留校申请, to the international students office before a published deadline, commonly two to three weeks before the last examination. Approved students are often moved into one designated building with reduced reception hours and a signed entry log.

If you leave without telling anyone, two things can go wrong: the building may be closed when you return early, and your residence registration may lapse. Tell the office your travel dates in writing, and keep the confirmation.

The application, in order

  1. Collect the form from the international students office, or the online portal if the university has one.
  2. State the exact dates you will be on campus and a contact number that works inside China.
  3. Get written confirmation before you book any travel.
  4. Register your absence with the local police station if you leave the city for more than a few days.
  5. Keep a copy of the approval; security guards work from a list, not from memory.

Food, Heating and the Empty Campus

Canteens run on reduced hours, often with a single hall open and a shortened menu, and campus shops and hairdressers may close for two weeks around the festival. Heating matters more than food in the north: municipal heating runs from 15 November to 15 March in most northern cities, and dormitory heating usually stays on, but hot water, laundry rooms and gyms often switch to a holiday timetable.

Travelling During Chunyun, and Whether to Try

Travelling in the ten days around the festival is possible but expensive and crowded. The 12306 rail platform releases tickets 15 days before departure, popular routes sell out within minutes of release, and stations operate at several times their normal volume. Domestic flights roughly double in price in the same window.

If you want to experience the festival in a Chinese family home or a smaller city, book rail and accommodation in November rather than January, travel a week before the festival or a week after it, and accept that the return leg is the harder booking.

Where students actually go

The three workable options are: stay on campus and treat it as a quiet study and language block; travel to a second-tier city where hotels stay open and prices stay sane; or accept an invitation from a Chinese classmate's family, which is the most memorable and the least predictable of the three.

Using the Quiet Weeks Deliberately

The break is the best language window of the year. Campus is empty, staff have time to talk, and there is no coursework competing for attention. Students who arrive at HSK 4 and use four quiet weeks for speaking practice consistently report a bigger jump than a semester of classroom hours delivers.

Concretely: book two language partner sessions a week, watch one Chinese drama with subtitles off by the end of the month, and do a daily fifteen-minute voice-message exchange rather than text. Speaking improves through speaking, and the empty campus removes the usual excuse.

The Administrative Tasks Worth Doing in January

Two deadlines sit close to the break and both are easier to handle before it starts. Residence permit renewals take seven to fifteen working days and the immigration office runs a holiday rota, so start the renewal at least three weeks before your permit expires. Library loans, laboratory access cards and scholarship paperwork all tend to require a staff signature that becomes hard to obtain in mid-February.

A Practical Checklist Before the Break Starts

Confirm your dormitory status in writing, download the academic calendar for the following year, renew any permit expiring before April, stock two weeks of food and medicine, save the international office duty number, and decide by early December whether you are travelling or staying.

The students who have a bad winter break are almost always the ones who decided nothing and assumed the campus would keep running. It does not. Plan the six weeks as you would plan a semester, and the quiet becomes an advantage rather than a problem.
