Chinese Public Holidays and Make-Up Class Days in 2027

By ORIGIN Education · 2026-11-24

A semester in China is not a flat run of eighteen teaching weeks. Seven public holidays cut through it, and at least three rearrange everything around them: a Saturday becomes a class day, a Monday becomes free, the canteen shuts without a sign, and the train you wanted sold out days ago.

The 2027 calendar in numbers

China grants 13 statutory days off in 2027 under the National Holiday and Memorial Day Regulations as revised with effect from 1 January 2025. Spring Festival takes four of them, National Day three, Labour Day two, and New Year, Qingming, Dragon Boat and Mid-Autumn one each. Weekends and make-up days are then arranged around those fixed points.

The lunar festivals move every year because they follow the agricultural calendar. Spring Festival 2027 lands on 6 February, Qingming on 5 April, Dragon Boat on 9 June and Mid-Autumn on 15 September. The exact make-up day schedule comes from a separate State Council notice, usually issued in November, so any Chinese public holidays 2027 calendar published before it estimates the pattern rather than stating the official arrangement.

What a make-up class day actually is

A make-up day, called tiaoxiu in Chinese, moves a working day onto a weekend so that a holiday can run in one continuous block. When a festival falls on a Tuesday, the State Council commonly declares the adjacent Monday free and the preceding Saturday a working day. Nothing is added or lost; the total is shifted.

The logic is travel: a single Tuesday off is useless to someone whose parents live 1,200 kilometres away, so the calendar is reshaped into three-day blocks. Everyone who then works six days in a row pays for it.

Why your university publishes a different version

Universities are not obliged to follow the national make-up days. Many keep the teaching week intact, cancel classes on the holiday itself, and recover the hours by extending the term. Others follow Beijing exactly, which means a Saturday lecture in a building where the canteen is closed.

Read the academic affairs notice, not the national calendar: the two differ more often than applicants expect.

How holidays collide with the teaching semester

Autumn term is where the damage happens. National Day absorbs a full week in early October, and Mid-Autumn sometimes sits within days of it, so October can lose eight teaching days. Spring term loses Spring Festival plus Qingming, but the five-week winter break normally swallows the festival whole.

The autumn term is the crowded one

A typical Chinese academic year runs 18 to 20 teaching weeks per semester, and students arriving in September meet National Day, its make-up Saturdays and a mid-term assessment inside the first eight weeks. Language courses meeting five mornings a week lose five sessions in one stroke, and the make-up class lands on a Saturday morning where attendance is taken as normal.

Trains: 15 days of presale and a waitlist

China Railway sells tickets 15 days before departure through the 12306 app, and seats for the first day of Spring Festival and National Day sell out within minutes of opening. If a train shows nothing available, join the waitlist rather than giving up: it holds your payment and issues a ticket automatically when a cancellation appears.

A few booking rules that catch foreign students out:

  • The 12306 app requires passport verification at a station ticket window before your first online purchase.
  • Student concession fares apply only on the route between your campus city and your registered home address, and only twice a year.
  • During chunyun, the 40-day travel rush around Spring Festival, roughly three billion passenger journeys are made, and standing tickets sell before seated ones.
  • Flights are usually cheaper booked 60 to 90 days ahead, well before the railway window opens.

What actually closes on campus

Canteens run reduced hours for the first three days of a holiday, and at least one hall in each dormitory cluster stays open. Libraries close on the statutory day itself and reopen the following morning. Campus clinics keep emergency cover but suspend routine outpatient appointments.

The services nobody warns you about

Dormitory maintenance stops taking requests. The campus card top-up desk closes, though the app usually works. Printing shops inside the library shut, which matters if an assignment is due the day classes resume.

Spring Festival is not a normal holiday

Spring Festival empties the cities. The 40-day travel rush known as chunyun moves the largest annual human migration on earth, factories and offices close for a week or more, and the campus you have learned to navigate becomes quiet enough to hear the heating pipes. Universities generally break for four to six weeks, longer than any Western winter holiday.

If you stay on campus

Expect a reduced canteen, a closed library for at least three days, and almost no Chinese classmates. The advantage is cost and quiet: this is the cheapest month to travel inside China. The disadvantage is loneliness, worth planning against by joining whatever the international office organises.

If you fly home

Book before the presale window opens, ideally 90 days out, and check whether your residence permit allows multiple entries. A single-entry visa used for a holiday trip needs a fresh application from abroad to return.

Which one costs less

Staying is cheaper on paper. A return flight to Europe in late January typically costs 6,000 to 11,000 RMB, against 2,000 to 3,500 RMB for four weeks of dormitory living and food. The calculation changes if your dormitory closes for the break, because then you are paying for a hotel or a hostel anyway.

Planning your first year around the calendar

Treat the holiday calendar as a budget line and a scheduling constraint rather than a footnote, because the costs of ignoring it arrive as a sold-out train and a locked canteen. Five habits cover most of the problem, and all of them are cheaper to adopt in September than to repair in February.

  1. Download the academic calendar in your first week and mark every holiday and make-up day on it.
  2. Ask the international office in writing whether your dormitory stays open during winter break.
  3. Verify your passport on 12306 in September, months before you need it.
  4. Book Spring Festival travel 90 days ahead, and never in the two weeks before the festival.
  5. Keep a photocopy of your residence permit and admission letter with you whenever you leave the campus city.

None of this is exotic. It is simply the difference between a semester that runs on schedule and one where you discover in October that the library is shut and your train left without you.
