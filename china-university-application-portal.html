Chinese University Application Portals: CUCAS, the CSC Online System and Direct Applications

By ORIGIN Education · 2026-11-13

There is no single official application portal for Chinese universities. The Chinese Government Scholarship runs its own system at studyinchina.csc.edu.cn, most universities accept direct applications through their own international student portals, and commercial aggregators such as CUCAS sit alongside both as paid intermediaries. Choosing the wrong channel costs money and, in scholarship rounds, costs the deadline itself.

The Three Channels and Who Owns Them

Only two channels are official: the China Scholarship Council's online system for government scholarship applications, and each university's own international student portal. Aggregators are commercial resellers that submit on your behalf; they can save time when you are comparing many universities, but they cannot improve your chances and they often add a service fee on top of the university's application fee.

The Scholarship System, Step by Step

The Council's portal handles the Chinese Government Scholarship and several provincial programmes that borrow its forms. You create an account, select the programme type — Type A through an embassy, Type B directly through a university — and the system generates a numbered application form that you must download, sign and upload again with your supporting documents attached.

Type A versus Type B

Type A applications are nominated by the Chinese embassy or a bilateral programme in your country, and the embassy sets its own deadline, often in January or February. Type B applications go directly to a university, which ranks candidates internally before forwarding them to the Council; university deadlines for Type B frequently close in December or January, well before the umbrella cut-off published for the scheme.

The documents the portal will ask for

The system requests a passport scan, notarised degree and transcript, language score report, a study plan in Chinese or English, two recommendation letters, the Foreigner Physical Examination Record, and the signed application form. Uploads are capped at a few megabytes per file, so compress scans to PDF at 200 dpi rather than photographing pages with a phone.

Direct Applications: What the University Portal Wants

University portals are simpler than the national system but differ from each other in irritating ways: some require registration before you can see the fee, some accept only PDF, and some route payment through a gateway that rejects foreign cards. Check whether the fee is refundable before paying, and never pay an agent to submit an application you could submit yourself in twenty minutes.

Fees and deadlines

Application fees run from 400 to 800 RMB per university and are non-refundable in almost every case. Deadlines for autumn intake cluster between 31 December and 30 April, with the more competitive programmes closing in the earlier half; spring intake, where a university offers one, usually closes between September and November.

Using an Aggregator Without Being Overcharged

Aggregators are legitimate businesses, and for an applicant comparing fifteen universities across three intake cycles they can be worth the fee. The risk is misaligned incentives: an agent may submit a weak application to a partner university that pays commission, so insist on seeing the exact programme name and the university's own published entry requirements before any payment.

  1. The agent will not tell you which university pays commission on your application.
  2. The programme quoted does not appear on the university's own website.
  3. You are asked to pay the university application fee to the agent's personal account.
  4. The agent offers to write your study plan or supply a language certificate.
  5. Deadlines quoted are later than the ones printed on the university portal.

A Realistic Application Calendar

Build the calendar backwards from the deadline rather than forwards from today. Notarisation and apostille take two to three weeks, the medical record is valid six months, a university needs two to four weeks to issue an admission letter and JW202, and the visa appointment and residence permit come after all of that.

The eight-month version

Start in April for an autumn intake. Certificates and transcripts first, because registry offices are the slowest link. Notarisation and apostille in May. Programme shortlist and portal accounts in June. Applications submitted July to October. Admission letters and JW202 between November and January. Visa in June or July of the following year, once the certificate of eligibility is in hand.

Portal Mistakes That Cost Places

Every intake produces the same failures. Files uploaded in the wrong section are not read, an application paid but never submitted sits in draft, and an email address typed with a typo receives none of the deadline reminders. The portal is not a formality; it is the record the university will rely on when your documents are checked.

Keep your own copy

Download the generated application form as a PDF and store it with your receipts. When a university loses a file — and some do — the numbered form with its submission timestamp is the only evidence that settles the argument. It also tells you the exact programme code, which differs from the programme name on the website more often than applicants expect.

The China university application portal landscape is really two decisions: official channel or reseller, and one university or five. Most applicants are best served by applying directly to three to five universities through their own portals, using the Council's system only if they are pursuing a government scholarship, and treating any aggregator as a convenience they pay for rather than a channel that improves their odds.
