Transcripts and Credit Transfer From Chinese Universities

By ORIGIN Education · 2026-11-11

Leaving a Chinese university with a transcript a foreign admissions office will accept is a document problem before it is an academic one. The grades are rarely the obstacle. The format, the verification chain and the credit arithmetic are what get applications rejected, and each of them has a fix that has to be started months early.

This guide covers what a Chinese transcript contains, how credits convert, where CHSI reports fit, and how transfers work in both directions.

What a Chinese transcript actually contains

A Chinese university transcript lists every course by semester with credit hours, a percentage grade and a grade point average, plus a page explaining the grading scale. Most institutions grade out of 100 with 60 as the pass mark, but the transcript may also show 优/良/中/及格 descriptors, and the GPA may sit on a 4.0 or a 5.0 scale depending on the university.

Grade points versus percentages

Chinese GPA scales are not standardised. Some universities use 4.0 where 90 and above equals 4.0; others use 5.0 where 90 and above equals 4.5 or higher. A 3.8 on a 5.0 scale is a very different student from a 3.8 on a 4.0 scale, which is why the registrar issues a scale explanation page alongside the transcript. Send both, always.

What is not on it

Chinese transcripts usually omit class rank, course descriptions and contact hours. Foreign universities ask for all three, so request a separate sealed course syllabus document from your school's teaching office at the same time. Asking later, after you have left the country, is far more expensive.

How Chinese credits convert

One Chinese credit represents roughly 16 to 18 classroom hours across a semester, and a four-year bachelor's degree requires about 160 to 180 credits in total. The European ECTS system assigns 60 credits per academic year, with one ECTS credit standing for 25 to 30 hours of total workload, so a Chinese credit typically converts to about 1.5 to 1.8 ECTS.

One credit is not one ECTS

The conversion is not a fixed ratio, and applying 1:2 mechanically will understate or overstate your workload. A Chinese physical education course worth one credit, meeting once a week, is not equivalent to a one-credit laboratory module that meets six hours a week. Convert by contact hours and assessment weight, and document the calculation in a one-page table when you apply.

CHSI verification and why foreign universities ask for it

CHSI (学信网, chsi.com.cn) is the Ministry of Education's student records platform, and it issues the two documents foreign universities most often demand: the online verification report for higher education qualifications and the online verification report for degrees. Each is generated in Chinese and English, carries a QR code for third-party checking, and is valid for a limited period, usually six months.

How to get the reports

Register with your passport number, complete identity verification, and the reports download as PDFs within minutes if your graduation data has been uploaded by your university. Data upload can lag by a semester, so check the record exists before a deadline rather than after.

Transferring credits out of a Chinese university

Credit transfer out of China depends on the receiving institution, not the Chinese one. Chinese universities will issue sealed transcripts and syllabi, but they do not award ECTS and cannot promise that a module will be recognised abroad. The practical work is matching your Chinese syllabi, hour by hour, against the learning outcomes of the receiving programme.

Building the equivalence file

For each course you want recognised, assemble four items: the syllabus in English, the textbook used, the assessment breakdown, and the number of contact hours. Universities in Germany and the Netherlands routinely reject applications that supply only a transcript, and accept the same application when the syllabus shows topic-level overlap.

Transferring credits into a Chinese university

Transferring into a Chinese university mid-degree is possible but narrow. Most institutions accept transfers only at the start of an academic year, require each course to appear in their own catalogue, and cap recognition at roughly half of the graduation credits. Entry into year two is far more common than entry into year three.

Who decides

The receiving department, not the international office, makes the final call on which courses count. Get the decision in writing before you withdraw from your current programme, and confirm whether the recognised credits carry grades or only a pass notation, because that difference affects your GPA for every later application.

Apostille, translation and sealed envelopes

China joined the Apostille Convention on 7 November 2023, so Chinese public documents, including notarised transcripts, now carry an apostille instead of consular legalisation when presented in the other member states. Translations must still come from an agency the receiving country accepts, and the original transcript has to arrive in a sealed envelope stamped across the flap.

The document checklist

  • Sealed original transcript, stamped by the registrar's office
  • Grading scale explanation page from the same office
  • Course syllabus document, sealed separately
  • CHSI qualification and degree verification reports, downloaded as PDFs
  • Notarised translation plus apostille, if the receiving country requires it
  • Passport name page, for when the transcript uses a Chinese transliteration

Timeline and what to do if a course is rejected

Start eight to ten weeks before the receiving university's deadline. Transcripts take three to five working days to issue, CHSI reports are instant once your records are live, and notarisation plus apostille can take two weeks in a busy provincial capital. Late starts are the most common reason credits are simply written off.

If a course is rejected, the syllabus wins the appeal, not the grade. Resubmit with a topic-by-topic comparison against the target module, a note on contact hours, and the assessment weighting. Escalate to the department's credit transfer officer rather than admissions, and keep the whole exchange in email so the decision is documented for the next institution that asks.
