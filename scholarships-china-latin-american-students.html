"Scholarships in China for Latin American Students"

By ORIGIN Education · 2026-11-10

China funds more international students than any other country outside Europe, and Latin American applicants are eligible for almost all of it, but the money arrives through channels that are rarely explained in Spanish or Portuguese. The difference between a funded place and a rejected file is usually which door you knocked on, not what your grades were.

Which scholarships in China are open to Latin American students?

The Chinese Government Scholarship, known as CSC, is the main route: it covers tuition, dormitory accommodation, medical insurance and a monthly stipend of RMB 2,500 for bachelor's, RMB 3,000 for master's and RMB 3,500 for doctoral students. Latin American applicants enter either through bilateral nomination by their own government or through a Chinese university under the Type B channel.

Those two doors have different deadlines, different paperwork and different competition. Applying to both is allowed and sensible, because a rejection from one route does not affect the other, and neither costs an application fee.

The four funding families

  • CSC bilateral programme, nominated by your national government and processed through the Chinese embassy.
  • CSC Type B, applied for directly to a Chinese university that holds a CSC quota.
  • Provincial and municipal scholarships, funded by Jiangsu, Zhejiang, Shanghai, Hubei, Guangdong and others.
  • Agency schemes: the Confucius Institute Scholarship for Chinese language, and MOFCOM for development-related master's degrees.

Bilateral nomination: how your government fits in

The bilateral route is a two-step process. Your national agency shortlists candidates, and the Chinese embassy's education office forwards the shortlist to CSC in Beijing. Both steps must be completed inside the same window, which is why applicants who only register on the CSC portal and never contact their own ministry usually fail.

Embassy and ministry channels in Latin America

Each country runs its own call, and the Chinese embassy education office coordinates with the national scholarship agency. Peru's PRONABEC, Colombia's ICETEX, Mexico's science and higher education ministry and Brazil's CAPES have all included Chinese universities in postgraduate calls, each with its own eligibility rules and its own calendar.

These calls change every year: a programme that funded ten students last cycle may fund three now. Check the agency page directly rather than relying on a university's marketing page, which often lists a partner agency that has already closed its call.

What the embassy actually needs from you

  • Passport valid for at least six months beyond the intended graduation date.
  • Notarised bachelor's degree and transcript, with Spanish or Portuguese originals translated.
  • Two academic recommendation letters on institutional letterhead.
  • A study plan in Chinese or English, 800 words or more, naming your target university and supervisor.
  • A physical examination form completed by a recognised hospital.

Deadlines: the two calendars that matter

CSC runs on two clocks. Bilateral nominations through an embassy usually close between December and March, while the Type B university channel typically closes between January and April. Missing the window costs a full academic year, because CSC funds one intake per year and does not accept late files.

Universities that hold a Type B quota often open their own portal in November, before the CSC system accepts submissions, so you can prepare documents early.

Language requirements and the HSK threshold

Most CSC programmes are taught in Chinese and require HSK 4 with 180 points for science and engineering, or HSK 5 for humanities and medicine. A one-year Chinese language course can be funded inside the scholarship, which is why many awardees start with a language year before the degree.

English-taught programmes waive HSK but usually ask for IELTS 6.0, TOEFL 80 or a letter confirming your previous degree was taught in English. Spanish and Portuguese are not accepted as a medium of instruction for degree study, even at universities with strong Latin American partnerships.

Provincial, municipal and university awards

Provincial governments run their own schemes, and they are less competitive than CSC because they are less advertised. Jiangsu, Zhejiang, Shanghai, Hubei and Guangdong all fund international students at roughly RMB 20,000-30,000 a year for master's and doctoral study, with tuition waivers at the lower end.

Shanghai's municipal scholarship splits into Type A, which is full funding including stipend, and Type B, which is a partial tuition waiver. Universities in the same city often layer their own president's scholarship on top, and stacking a provincial award with a university award is permitted at several institutions.

Confucius Institute and MOFCOM scholarships

The Confucius Institute Scholarship funds Chinese language study and teaching degrees rather than engineering or business, and it requires an HSK score at the point of application. Host universities administer it directly, and the deadline usually falls between March and May for a September start.

The MOFCOM scheme, run by the Ministry of Commerce, funds master's degrees in development, public policy and economics for candidates nominated by their government's commerce ministry. It is small, it follows bilateral trade relationships rather than open competition, and it is one of the few fully funded routes that accepts applicants without an HSK score.

What the annual review can take away

CSC is renewed year by year, and the annual review (年度评审) can suspend or cancel funding if your GPA falls, you fail a course or you are disciplined. The review scores academic performance, attitude and conduct, and a failed review ends the stipend even if your student visa remains valid.

That asymmetry surprises awardees: the visa is tied to enrolment, the stipend is tied to performance, and losing one does not automatically cancel the other. A student who fails the review keeps studying and starts paying, which is a harder position than being sent home.

How to build a competitive file

  1. Pick three target universities and read their international programme pages before writing anything.
  2. Email a potential supervisor with a two-paragraph research interest note, in English, with your CV attached.
  3. Get a pre-admission letter if the university offers one; Type B files with a pre-admission letter clear faster.
  4. Translate and notarise every document in September, not in January, because translation queues are real.
  5. Apply to both CSC routes and to one provincial scheme, in that order of effort.
  6. Keep scanned copies of everything in one folder, named consistently, because you will be asked for them repeatedly.

Funding a degree in China from Latin America is an administrative project more than a merit contest. The stipend is real, the tuition waiver is real, and the number of applicants who miss the deadline or the nomination step is larger than the number who are academically rejected. Read the calendar first, then the requirements, then start writing.
