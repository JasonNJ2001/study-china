Online and Part-Time Degrees from Chinese Universities

By ORIGIN Education · 2026-11-23

Online study in China is sold as one product and is really four: free Chinese MOOCs, part-time master's degrees, ministry-approved joint programmes, and foreign degrees marketed with a Chinese partner. Only one of those four leads to a qualification the Chinese ministry will verify, so the label on the brochure matters far more than the price on the invoice.

What counts as an online degree from a Chinese university

Chinese universities do not award fully online bachelor's or master's degrees of their own. What exists online is course content: XuetangX, launched by Tsinghua University in October 2013, hosts thousands of courses and sells verified certificates, but no XuetangX certificate carries academic credit toward a Chinese degree. Anything advertised as a Chinese online degree is in fact a foreign degree delivered through a partnership.

The distinction is not pedantry. A Chinese degree means a record on 学信网 (CHSI) plus a certificate signed by a university president; a foreign degree delivered online means a certificate whose value in China depends on whether the Chinese Service Center for Scholarly Exchange recognises it.

The four products people actually mean

  1. Chinese MOOC certificates, bought for a few hundred yuan.
  2. Part-time master's degrees (非全日制) taught on weekends inside China.
  3. Sino-foreign joint programmes approved by the Ministry of Education.
  4. Foreign degrees delivered remotely and branded as Chinese because a Chinese university appears in the marketing.

Will the CSCSE certify a degree studied online?

No. The Chinese Service Center for Scholarly Exchange (CSCSE), the only body that issues the 国外学历学位认证书 accepted by Chinese employers, universities and city governments, excludes degrees obtained through purely online or distance study. A temporary waiver covered students stranded abroad between 2020 and spring 2023, and that waiver has ended.

Why the residency record decides it

CSCSE cross-checks immigration records (出入境记录) from the exit-and-entry administration against the dates printed on your transcript. A two-year master's with three months of physical presence in China normally fails, even when the foreign university is highly ranked. Approved joint programmes pass because the ministry has already accepted their delivery model.

The one exception written into the rules

If any part of the programme required on-site teaching in China and your immigration record shows it, the case is assessed normally rather than rejected outright. Keep boarding passes, dormitory contracts and the university's attendance letters.

Part-time master's degrees: the 非全日制 route

Since the 2017 intake, part-time master's programmes have sat inside the same national entrance examination as full-time ones: identical papers on the same December weekend, the same cut-off lines in most disciplines, and two certificates at the end. The only difference printed on the documents is the study mode, and recruiters do read that line.

Why foreign applicants rarely get in

Part-time enrolment assumes an existing Chinese student record or an employer sponsor, and a student visa is issued only for full-time registration. An X1 holder therefore cannot register as a part-time student, so this route is effectively closed to anyone who needs a visa to be in China at all.

What it costs

Part-time MBA fees in Shanghai and Beijing run from CNY 80,000 to CNY 300,000 for the whole programme, usually payable in annual instalments, with weekend classes. Engineering and education part-time master's degrees are cheaper, typically CNY 30,000 to 60,000.

Chinese MOOCs and micro-credentials

XuetangX, the national 学银在线 platform and the Chinese courses on Coursera are useful for language preparation and placement tests, not for degrees. A verified certificate costs roughly CNY 100 to 300, proves that you finished one specific course, and does not appear on CHSI or on 学位网. Treat it as evidence of preparation in a scholarship interview, not as a qualification.

What a MOOC is genuinely good for

Placement. A student who completes a calculus or programming MOOC before arrival can sometimes skip a remedial course, and admissions tutors read the certificate as evidence that you can study in a structured online format.

Joint programmes delivered partly online

Sino-foreign cooperative programmes approved by the Ministry of Education are the one route where a foreign degree earned with limited time in China can still be certified. Every approved programme appears on the ministry's 中外合作办学监管工作信息平台 with an approval number, the foreign partner, the annual intake and the permitted delivery mode.

How to check approval in five minutes

Search the platform by the Chinese university's name, confirm that the approval number starts with MOE or 教外综, and check that the delivery mode includes on-site teaching inside China. If a sales page cannot produce an approval number, the programme is a course rather than a degree.

Costs and timelines compared

The four routes differ by an order of magnitude in price and by years in what they deliver, so the honest comparison is a table rather than a paragraph. The certification column is the one that decides whether your money buys a qualification an employer can verify, and it is the column brochures leave out.

The residency arithmetic

CSCSE compares the study period on the transcript with immigration records, so plan for the majority of the programme on site. A 24-month master's delivered as 18 months online plus one summer in China fails on the numbers alone.

Language and entry requirements on these routes

Part-time and joint programmes still apply normal admission standards: a bachelor's degree for master's entry, notarised transcripts, two references and, for Chinese-taught tracks, HSK 4 at 180 points or above. English-taught joint programmes usually accept IELTS 6.0 or a medium-of-instruction letter instead, which is why they attract applicants who have no Chinese.

Documents that take the longest

Notarised degree certificates and transcripts take two to four weeks in most countries, and the CSCSE certification of your previous degree, if you already studied abroad, can take a further month. Start both before you apply rather than after you are accepted.

Who an online or part-time route actually suits

It suits two groups: professionals already working in China who want a domestic part-time master's, and students who want a foreign degree through an approved joint programme with a short residency. It suits nobody who wants a Chinese degree without moving to China, because that product does not exist at any price.

Five questions to ask before paying

  1. Is this a Chinese degree or a foreign degree?
  2. What is the Ministry of Education approval number, and does it appear on the regulator's public platform?
  3. How many months must I be physically in China, and who records them?
  4. Will the certificate be eligible for CSCSE certification — will you put that in writing?
  5. Which visa does the programme sponsor, and for how long?

If the answers are vague, you are buying a course. If they are specific, you are buying a degree, and the price difference between the two is usually a factor of ten.
