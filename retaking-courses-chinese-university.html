Retaking Courses and Supplementary Exams at Chinese Universities

By ORIGIN Education · 2026-11-15

Failing a course in China is not the end of a degree, but the recovery path has fixed rules and a hard ceiling on the grade you can recover. The difference between a student who graduates on time and one who loses a year is usually whether they understood the make-up system in the week the result was published, rather than in the week before registration.

The Three Chances, and the One That No Longer Exists

Originally there were three: a make-up exam (补考), a retake of the course (重修), and a final clearance exam (清考) sat just before graduation. The Ministry of Education required universities to abolish clearance exams in a 2019 reform of undergraduate teaching, and most had removed them by 2020. Assume two chances.

That change matters because the clearance exam was the safety net that let weak students graduate on time. Without it, a failed make-up leads directly to a retake, which means attending the course again and paying for the credits.

How the Make-Up Exam Works

A make-up exam is held at the start of the following semester, usually within four weeks of the results being published, and it is free. The mark is capped: pass or fail, and a pass is recorded as 60 out of 100, or a 1.0 grade point on a 4.0 scale, no matter how well you actually perform.

Registration is not automatic at every university. Some require the student to confirm the make-up in the academic system within a stated window, and a missed confirmation counts as a fail. Check the school notice board and the system in the same week the grade appears.

What the paper covers

The make-up paper covers the same syllabus as the original, set by the same course team, and it is usually marked more strictly because the cohort is small. Continuous assessment marks from the semester normally carry over, so a student who attended and submitted work but failed the final has a real advantage over one who did not.

Retaking a Course for Credits and for Grades

A retake means enrolling in the course again: attending lectures, submitting the coursework and sitting the final with the new cohort. The recorded mark is the new one, uncapped, which is why students retake courses they have already passed when a graduate application needs a higher average.

Retakes are not free. Universities charge per credit, commonly 100 to 200 yuan per credit for international students, so a four-credit course costs 400 to 800 yuan. The retake also occupies a slot in the timetable, which can collide with a compulsory course in the same semester.

When a retake is refused

Some courses cannot be retaken for grade improvement, only to clear a fail, and clinical or laboratory courses may require repeat attendance with a full practical component. Ask the teaching office which category the course falls into before paying.

What a Fail Does to a Scholarship

For scholarship holders the consequence arrives at the annual review rather than at the exam. Chinese Government Scholarship reviews consider the year's transcript, and a failed course is a documented negative that can reduce or suspend the award, even where the student later passes the make-up.

The review is conducted by the university and reported to the scholarship council, so the international student office already knows the result before the student does. If a fail is unavoidable, tell that office early: a documented medical or family reason, filed at the time, is treated very differently from an explanation offered at the review.

Academic Warning and Dismissal Thresholds

Universities monitor cumulative failed credits, and most issue a formal academic warning (学业警示) once a student fails a set number of credits in one year, commonly 15 to 20, or a percentage of the semester load. The warning goes to the student and the guardian contact on file.

Repeated warnings can lead to dismissal or to a change of programme, and the thresholds are published in the student handbook rather than negotiated case by case. A student who receives a warning should request the written rule from the teaching office, because the recovery plan has to satisfy a specific number, not a general promise.

How to appeal a warning

An appeal is possible within the semester, and it works on procedure rather than on sympathy: a mark that was never entered, a course taken twice in the record, or an examination missed for a documented medical reason. Submit it in writing to the teaching office with evidence attached, and keep the receipt. Appeals that arrive after the semester closes are usually refused on timing alone.

Reading the Transcript Afterwards

The transcript records the highest mark achieved, with the attempt history available on request. Employers and graduate schools abroad usually ask for the full record, so a pattern of retakes stays visible even when the final grade looks respectable. Request the English transcript early, because the translation and the seal take a week at most universities.

Planning a Recovery Semester

A recovery plan works when it subtracts load rather than adding effort. Five steps cover the cases that appear every year, and only the first one carries a deadline measured in days rather than weeks, which is why it should be done in the week the result is published.

  1. Confirm the make-up registration in the academic system before the stated deadline.
  2. Request the mark breakdown to see whether coursework or the final paper caused the fail.
  3. Check whether the course is a degree requirement, because a failed degree course blocks the diploma even with enough total credits.
  4. Reduce the next semester's load by dropping an elective rather than adding a retake on top of a full timetable.
  5. Put the scholarship office in writing if the fail was caused by illness, with the medical certificate attached at the time.

The attendance trap

Attendance is the silent multiplier in all of this. Most universities bar a student from sitting the final when attendance falls below two thirds, which converts a recoverable grade into an automatic fail and a retake fee. Sitting at the front of a large lecture hall is not the point; signing the attendance sheet is, and the signature is what the teaching office reads.
