PhD in China for International Students: Funding and Supervision

By ORIGIN Education · 2026-11-03

Doctoral study in China is unusual in one respect: the decision is made by a single professor, not by an admissions committee. Once a supervisor agrees to take you and the school has a quota left, the paperwork follows. Once a supervisor says no, no amount of strong grades fixes it.

That single fact reorganises the entire application, and most rejected applicants never learn it.

The supervisor decides first

A PhD in China is conferred by the university, but it is granted through a professor. Chinese doctoral admissions run through a supervisor quota system. Each professor has a fixed number of doctoral places per year, often one or two, sometimes none. The university's graduate school processes applications, but it processes the ones a supervisor has already claimed. Emailing the graduate admissions office with a general enquiry produces a brochure. Emailing a professor with a specific research question produces a conversation.

What to put in the first email

Three paragraphs. Who you are and your degree background in one sentence. What you have read of their recent work, named specifically, with one concrete observation or question. What you propose to work on and why their lab is the right place for it. Attach a two-page research proposal and a CV. Do not attach fifteen documents, and do not write a mass mail with an obvious template.

Expect a low reply rate. Ten to fifteen well-targeted emails is a normal campaign, and replies often take two to four weeks.

How doctoral study is funded

The CSC route

The Chinese Government Scholarship covers tuition, dormitory accommodation and a monthly stipend of 3,500 yuan for doctoral students, plus medical insurance. Applications go through your home country's Chinese embassy or through the university itself under the high-level postgraduate programme. The two channels have different deadlines and different competition, and you can only use one.

The weakness is timing. Embassy channels close in January to March for a September start, and results often arrive in July or August, which is late for visa paperwork. University channels sometimes run later but are more competitive because applicants apply directly.

Supervisor-funded positions

In engineering, computer science and the life sciences, a professor with a funded project can pay a doctoral student from the grant. This is the fastest route and the least visible: it rarely appears on a university website, and it comes from the email conversation rather than the application portal. If a professor replies that they have funding, treat the admissions process as a formality and move quickly.

What the money actually buys

A 3,500 yuan monthly stipend in a tier-two city covers a dormitory room, food and transport with some margin. In Shanghai or Beijing it covers food and rent and very little else, particularly if you live outside campus. Doctoral students in laboratory fields often receive a top-up from the supervisor's project on top of the scholarship, which is why the same scholarship produces very different living standards in different labs.

Budget for two costs the stipend does not cover: international conference travel, and the publication charges some journals levy.

The application package that gets read

The research proposal is the decisive document

Two to four pages. A defined question, a short literature position, a method, a realistic timeline and a statement of what the result would contribute. Chinese supervisors read for feasibility: can this be done in three or four years with the equipment in my lab and a student who does not yet speak Chinese?

Proposals that fail usually propose something the lab cannot support, or propose a topic so broad that no result could ever close it.

Publications and references

For science and engineering, one or two published papers, even in a mid-tier journal, moves an application from possible to likely. For humanities and social sciences, a strong writing sample carries the same weight. Two academic references, in English, from people who can describe your research rather than your attendance.

Language

Doctoral programmes in science and engineering are frequently taught and supervised in English. Humanities doctorates usually require HSK 5 or above, and some require HSK 6. Confirm the language of supervision with the supervisor directly, not from the programme page.

Graduation requirements are stricter than admission

Admission is a conversation. Graduation is a rule. Most universities require a defined number of publications in indexed journals before the defence, commonly one or two SCI papers for science and engineering doctorates, and the requirement is enforced by the school rather than negotiated by the supervisor.

Ask about the specific publication requirement in your first email. A supervisor who cannot state it precisely is either new or evasive, and either answer is useful.

The realistic timeline

  • Months 1 to 3: identify twenty supervisors, read their recent papers, send targeted emails.
  • Months 3 to 5: refine the proposal with feedback, secure a supervisor's informal agreement.
  • Months 5 to 8: formal application, CSC or university funding, documents and interviews.
  • Months 8 to 10: admission letter and JW202 form, then visa.
  • Year one: coursework, language training, proposal defence.
  • Years two to four: research, publication, dissertation, defence.

Three reasons applications fail

Applying to a university instead of a person. Applying with a proposal the lab cannot execute. Applying after the supervisor's quota is already committed, usually in the autumn before the intake year.

Doctoral study in China is a workable and well-funded path for international students, but it is a relationship before it is an application. Getting the relationship right takes three months of reading and writing; getting it wrong costs a year.
