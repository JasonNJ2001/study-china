Chinese Student Visa Medical Check: The Foreigner Physical Examination Record

By ORIGIN Education · 2026-11-13

Every X1 student visa file in China rests on one A4 sheet: the Foreigner Physical Examination Record (外国人体格检查记录), a six-month-valid medical certificate completed at a hospital in your home country, stamped across your photograph, and verified again after you arrive. Get the format wrong and the consulate returns the file without reading your admission letter. This guide covers who needs it, what the tests screen for, and where applicants lose weeks.

Who Needs the Medical Check Before Departure

The Foreigner Physical Examination Record is mandatory for X1 visa applicants, meaning anyone staying in China longer than 180 days, and most universities now require the completed form at application stage, months before the visa window opens. X2 applicants staying 180 days or less are exempt at the consulate, but individual universities may still ask for a check on arrival, so confirm with your international office rather than assuming.

The 180-day dividing line

The dividing line is programme duration, not degree level. A one-semester exchange of 150 days falls under X2 and needs no record; a ten-month language course falls under X1 and does. The number that matters is the programme length printed on your admission letter and JW202 form, not the length you assumed when you applied.

What the Form Actually Contains

The record is a standard two-page Chinese-English form, downloadable from your target university or the Chinese embassy, covering personal details, vaccination history, and five examinations: general physical, blood pressure and pulse, chest X-ray, electrocardiogram, and blood and urine tests. It must carry a photograph stamped with a cross-page seal, a physician's signature on every page, and the hospital's official stamp.

Where to get the form

Download the current version from your target university's international office page or from the Chinese embassy website in your country. Versions circulate that predate the 2018 merger of the quarantine authority into China Customs, and a form bearing an obsolete issuing authority is a common reason for a query at the visa counter.

How to Fill the Form So It Is Accepted

Four formatting errors account for almost every rejected record, and all four are fixable at the hospital counter rather than later by post. Deal with them while you are still standing in front of the physician, because a corrected form usually means retaking the tests.

  1. Attach a passport-format photograph and ask for the hospital's cross-page stamp (骑缝章) over the photo edge; a loose or glued photograph is invalid.
  2. Have the physician sign and date every page, not only the summary page.
  3. Get the hospital's red official stamp (公章) on the final page; department and clinic stamps are routinely refused.
  4. Request the original plus two photocopies, since the university keeps one and the visa centre keeps another.
  5. Keep the laboratory printouts. Some consulates ask to see the raw results beside the summary form.

Doing the Check in China Instead

If you are already in China, or your home hospital cannot issue the Chinese form, the examination can be done at an International Travel Healthcare Centre (国际旅行卫生保健中心) operated by China Customs in most provincial capitals, where the same panel costs 400–700 RMB and results are issued in two to five working days. The certificate issued there replaces the foreign record for residence permit purposes.

The verification certificate

Students who complete the check abroad must have the record verified in China. Within 30 days of arrival, take the original form, passport, admission letter and JW202 to the local travel healthcare centre; they issue a Verification Certificate of Physical Examination Record for Foreigners (境外人员体格检查记录验证证明), which the exit-entry bureau requires before granting the residence permit.

What the Tests Screen For

The screening targets conditions named in Chinese immigration law rather than general fitness. Article 21 of the Exit and Entry Administration Law, in force since 1 July 2013, allows refusal of entry to foreigners with serious mental disorder, infectious tuberculosis or other infectious diseases that may seriously harm public health, and the record exists to evidence the absence of those conditions.

HIV, tuberculosis and the treatment route

HIV positivity is the single most consequential result. Chinese regulations list AIDS among the diseases that bar entry, and a positive HIV test on the record ends the visa process; there is no waiver route for students.

Active pulmonary tuberculosis produces the same outcome until treatment is complete and a negative sputum test is documented. Applicants diagnosed during the check can usually proceed a semester later with a treatment certificate and a repeat film, which is why the examination should not be left to the final fortnight.

Hepatitis B carriers are not barred. A positive surface antigen with normal liver function is normally accepted, although a few universities ask for a hepatology letter confirming the carrier state is inactive.

Pregnancy does not disqualify an applicant. Chest X-rays are deferred and a physician's note is attached, and most centres will issue the record with the radiology section marked as deferred rather than as a failure.

Timing, Validity and Cost

The record is valid for six months from the examination date, which creates a timing problem that applicants solve badly. Visa processing, registration and the residence permit application can consume four months between them, so schedule the examination after the admission letter arrives and no earlier than 90 days before departure.

Cost by country

Cost varies enormously by country. A complete record costs roughly $80–180 in India, Nigeria, Indonesia or Vietnam, $200–400 in the United States or Australia, and 400–700 RMB if done in China. Embassy-designated clinics in some countries charge a premium for same-week turnaround, which is worth paying only if your deadline is already tight.

Five Reasons Records Get Rejected

  1. The photograph has no cross-page stamp, so the page is treated as unauthenticated.
  2. The examination is older than six months at the visa appointment, even though the admission letter is new.
  3. HIV or chest X-ray results are missing because the clinic omitted them unless specifically asked.
  4. The name is spelled differently from the passport, including omitted middle names and hyphens.
  5. The stamp belongs to a private clinic rather than a hospital with an official seal.

None of this is difficult, it is simply sequential: notarised documents, then the medical record, then the admission letter and JW202, then the visa appointment, then the residence permit. Applicants who start the sequence three months before the intake rarely have a problem, and applicants who start it three weeks before the intake almost always do.
