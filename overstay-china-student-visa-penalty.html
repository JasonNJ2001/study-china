What Happens If You Overstay Your Chinese Student Visa

By ORIGIN Education · 2026-11-24

Most visa problems are paperwork problems, and paperwork gets fixed at a desk. An overstay is different: the clock keeps running while you look for the desk. A one-day lapse and a three-week one are not the same category of mistake.

The overstay China visa penalty is a daily rate that stops at a ceiling and then hands the decision to an officer.

What the law says about overstaying

Article 78 of the Exit and Entry Administration Law of the People's Republic of China, in force since 1 July 2013, sets the penalty: a fine of 500 RMB for each day of overstay, capped at 10,000 RMB, or detention of five to fifteen days where the circumstances are serious. The fine accrues from the day after permitted stay ends.

Two things follow. The cap is reached on day twenty, so anything beyond adds no further fine but raises the odds an officer treats the case as serious. And the choice between fine and detention sits with the officer.

How the clock actually runs

Your permitted stay is written on a specific document, and which one depends on your visa type. An X1 visa is valid for entry only, for 30 days from arrival, during which you must convert it into a residence permit. An X2 visa carries a stay period printed on the sticker, usually 180 days or fewer, and cannot be converted.

The residence permit is what matters after the first month. Its expiry date is on the front of the card, and it is usually set a few weeks before your programme ends rather than after it.

The 30-day rule people miss

An extension application must be filed at least 30 days before the permit expires, under Article 32 of the same law. Not "within 30 days" — at least 30 days before. Students who read that as a grace period arrive eleven days before expiry, are turned away, and return after the expiry date with a case rather than an application.

The 24-hour registration nobody remembers

Article 39 requires foreigners staying anywhere other than a hotel to register the address with the local police station within 24 hours. A missed registration does not create an overstay, but it surfaces in any later check.

Deportation and the re-entry ban

Deportation is a separate decision from the fine, taken under Article 62. It is recorded, executed at your own expense, and under Article 81 it can carry a re-entry ban of five to ten years for illegal residence or employment. It is not negotiable at the airport.

What "deported" leaves on your record

A deportation stamp and a ban are visible to every Chinese visa officer afterwards, and they follow the passport, not the application. A student deported at 22 for a three-week overstay may find a work visa refused at 28 on the same record.

Detention is not a holding room at the airport

Administrative detention of five to fifteen days is served in a detention centre, with notification to your embassy, and deportation follows.

Three situations that cause most overstays

The same three situations produce most overstays every semester, and none of them involves a student deciding to stay illegally. All three involve assuming that a document was handled by somebody else, usually the university, usually correctly, and never verified afterwards. Each one is preventable with a single phone call.

1. The residence permit you never collected

Applications are often submitted by the university's international office on the student's behalf, and the card is collected weeks later from a different desk. Students travel during the wait, return, and never check whether the card was issued. The passport then shows an X1 visa that expired weeks ago.

2. The programme that ended earlier than the permit assumed

A one-year language course finishing in June sometimes carries a permit valid to September, and a student who stays through the summer assumes the permit covers it. It does, if the date says so. But permits are often set thirty days after the final examination, not after graduation, and the two dates differ by months.

3. The internship that changed your status

Part-time work during term and internships in the holidays are permitted within limits, but a full-time internship after graduation requires a different status entirely. A student who signs a contract while still on a student permit is both overstaying the purpose of the permit and working without authorisation, which count as two violations.

How to fix an overstay before the airport

Voluntary disclosure is treated far more leniently than discovery at a border checkpoint, because the officer can see you are trying to regularise rather than evade. The order of steps matters more than the apology, and the paperwork you carry decides whether the visit ends with a receipt or with a case file.

  1. Go to the exit-entry administration hall of the public security bureau in the district where you live, not the airport.
  2. Bring your passport, residence permit or visa, the registration slip from your accommodation, and a letter from your university explaining your status.
  3. Expect to pay the fine on the spot and to receive an official receipt; keep it, because it is your proof of settlement.
  4. Ask explicitly whether the case has been recorded as a warning or as a violation, and get the answer in writing if you can.
  5. If your stay is continuing, apply for the residence permit in the same visit rather than in a second one.

What to do if the officer proposes detention

Ask to contact your embassy and your university's international office before signing anything. A letter confirming enrolment and attendance changes outcomes often enough to be worth the delay.

Preventing it: run a two-date system

Keep two dates in your calendar for the whole of your stay: the document expiry date, and a reminder twenty days before it labelled renewal. The second date is the one that protects you, because the law's real deadline is thirty days before expiry, not the date printed on the card.

  • Photograph the front and back of every card the day you receive it, so the date is never a guess.
  • Check the expiry date at the start of each semester, not at the end of the year.
  • Confirm in writing with the international office that a renewal has been filed, and ask for the receipt.

A student who knows that the fine is 500 RMB a day and that the thirtieth day before expiry is the real deadline does not overstay by accident. The rule is short, the consequence is not.
