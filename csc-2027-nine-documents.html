CSC 2027 Opens in 13 Days: The Nine Documents That Decide Your Application

The Chinese Government Scholarship portal opens on **1 November**. That is 13 days from today.

Most applicants will spend those 13 days collecting documents. That is the wrong order. Nine documents go into a CSC file, and they are not equally important — three can end your application before anyone reads it, three decide whether you are funded, and three only need to be adequate. Treating all nine the same is how people run out of time and lose the ones that matter.

Here is the split.

Tier 1: the three that end an application outright

These are pass/fail, and none of them can be fixed after submission. Do them first, before you write a single word of your study plan.

1. Passport validity that covers your entire stay

Your passport must be valid for the whole scholarship period, not just for the application. A passport expiring in 2028 with a four-year PhD starting in 2027 is a disqualifying document, and it is the single easiest failure to prevent.

Check the expiry date today. If it falls before your intended graduation, renew now and apply with the new number. Do not submit with an old passport and plan to update it later — the number is bound to your application record.

2. The Foreigner Physical Examination Form, within six months

The physical examination must be done at a designated hospital and the form must be current — normally within six months of submission. Two things go wrong here constantly:

  • **The form expires mid-process.** You get examined in June, submit in November, and the form is stale. If your exam is already more than four months old, redo it.
  • **The wrong form.** Every year applicants submit a domestic health certificate from their own country. It must be the Chinese Foreigner Physical Examination Form, with the blood test and chest X-ray results attached.

3. Academic certificates that can actually be verified

A degree certificate that cannot be checked is treated as absent. That means apostille or notarisation where required, and a certified translation if the original is not in Chinese or English.

There is a specific trap for final-year students: if you graduate after the application window closes, you need a **pre-graduation certificate** (在读证明 / 预毕业证明) from your university stating the expected graduation date. A missing one reads as "no degree" and the file stops there.

Get these three moving in the first 72 hours. They involve other people — hospitals, notaries, your registrar's office — and other people are the reason 13 days is not enough time if you start in week two.

Tier 2: the three that decide whether you are funded

Everyone who clears Tier 1 is eligible. Eligibility is the floor, not the competition. These three are where a file moves from "compliant" to "worth paying for".

4. The study plan — the only document where you are visible as a person

This is the most under-invested document in the entire application. Most study plans are 500 words of general enthusiasm, which tells a reviewer nothing.

A study plan that works does four things:

  • Names a **specific research question or specialisation**, not a field
  • Names **specific faculty or a research group** and shows you have read their recent work
  • Explains **why this has to be done in China** — access to data, a particular lab, an industry context. If the answer is "because the scholarship is there", the reviewer will notice.
  • States what you will do **after graduation**, in a way that connects back to the programme

Two pages is enough. Depth beats length.

5. The supervisor's acceptance letter — the single highest-leverage item for graduate applicants

For master's and doctoral applications, an email from a professor who has agreed to supervise you changes the review materially. It is not required for every route, but where it is accepted, it is the strongest signal in the file.

The mistake is timing. Professors go quiet in November and December. If you are applying for 2027 entry and have not emailed anyone yet, do it in the next 72 hours, with your CV, transcript, and a two-paragraph research interest — not a generic "please supervise me" letter sent to twenty people at once.

6. Recommendation letters that contain specifics

Two letters from associate professors or above. The ranking of the writer matters far less than whether the letter contains a concrete anecdote: what you did, in which project, with what result.

If your referee only knows your GPA, the letter is decorative. Give them a one-page summary of your work and your plan, and ask them to write about a specific piece of it. This is a request, not an imposition — and it takes them ten minutes instead of forty.

Tier 3: the three that only need to be adequate

These consume a disproportionate share of applicants' attention. Stop at "correct and complete".

7. Translated transcripts

Complete, stamped, translated. Nobody is reading your marks line by line in the first pass — the GPA and the trend are what register. Do not pay for a premium translation service and do not agonise over a 78 versus 80 in one elective.

8. Language certificates

HSK for Chinese-taught programmes, IELTS or TOEFL for English-taught ones. Meet the published threshold and move on. A 7.5 IELTS does not compensate for a vague study plan, and a 6.5 does not block an otherwise strong file.

9. Police clearance certificate

Needed for some programmes and some countries, not all. Check your specific route before spending two weeks obtaining one. Where it is required, start it early because it is slow — but do not chase it if your programme does not ask.

The mistake almost everyone makes: treating the portal date as your deadline

The portal opens 1 November. Your **real** deadline is the one set by your receiving institution — the embassy, the Chinese university, or the ministry in your country. Those internal deadlines routinely fall **weeks before** the system closes, because the institution has to review and forward files upward.

Which means: the deadline that matters may be closer than 13 days, and you cannot see it on the CSC website. Find your designated receiving institution and read their own notice today. This single check reorders everything else you were planning to do.

A 13-day schedule that actually fits

DaysWhat you do
1–3Tier 1 only. Passport check, book the physical exam, request transcripts and certificates. Email three potential supervisors.
4–7Draft the study plan. Two pages, four sections, named faculty. Ask two referees, give them your one-page summary.
8–11Fill the portal. Upload everything. Translate what needs translating. Chase the documents that depend on other people.
12–13Read the whole file as a reviewer would. Check every date, every name spelling, every attached file opening correctly. Submit — do not leave it to the last hour.

If you are missing something at day 11, you do not have a document problem. You have a priorities problem, and the fix is to cut the Tier 3 items that your specific programme does not require.

One decision worth making in the next hour

There are two main routes into the CSC, and which one you take changes your document set and your deadline:

  • **Through your national receiving institution** (embassy or ministry route) — broader programme coverage, single national competition, earlier internal deadline
  • **Through a Chinese university** (university route) — narrower but often more competitive per place, and the university sets its own terms

Applicants who try to prepare for both at once usually do neither well. Pick one this week based on which institution has already published a 2027 notice, and build the file for that route only. Our breakdown of the two routes is here: [CSC Type A vs Type B](https://jasonnj2001.github.io/study-china/csc-scholarship-2027.html).

What this comes down to

Thirteen days is enough time if you spend it on the right three documents, and nowhere near enough if you spend it evenly across nine.

The three that end your application are all errands — other people's schedules, other people's queues. Start them now. The three that decide funding are all writing — your plan, your supervisor's letter, your referees' letters. Those need your attention, not your errands.

Everything in Tier 3 can wait until the file is otherwise complete. It usually does, and it usually turns out fine.
