English-Taught Bachelor's Degrees in China: Universities, Majors and Costs

By ORIGIN Education · 2026-11-14

China now runs several hundred bachelor's programmes advertised as English-medium, and the quality range inside that number is wider than any ranking suggests. Some are staffed by faculty who publish in English and teach cohorts of forty; others are Chinese-taught degrees with an interpreter in the room. The difference shows in the curriculum document, not the brochure.

What an English-Taught Bachelor's Degree in China Actually Is

An English-taught bachelor's in China is a standard four-year undergraduate degree, three years of coursework plus a final thesis or clinical year, delivered in English to a cohort that usually mixes international and Chinese students. The award is the same bachelor's degree a Chinese student receives, with no "international" or "English-medium" label printed on it.

That last point matters more than applicants realise. The diploma does not disclose the language of instruction, so a transcript from an English-medium programme reads identically to one from a Chinese-taught programme at the same university. Recognition abroad depends on the university's standing and the transcript.

Which Majors Are Genuinely Taught in English

Medicine, engineering and business account for the overwhelming majority of English-medium undergraduate places, and each has a different quality story. Medicine is the most regulated and standardised, engineering the largest and most variable, and business the easiest to enter and least distinctive in the job market.

Medicine and allied health

Clinical medicine in English is the oldest and most tightly controlled category. The Ministry of Education issued interim provisions for English-medium MBBS programmes in 2006, and the field has carried a designated national list ever since, which is why a Bachelor of Medicine and Bachelor of Surgery in China is recognisable in India, Nepal, Nigeria and Zimbabwe.

Engineering and computer science

Mechanical, civil, electrical, software and artificial intelligence degrees are the volume category, and the teaching language is usually genuine because the textbooks are English. What varies is laboratory access: at better universities the English-medium cohort shares equipment with Chinese students, while at weaker ones the slots are timetabled separately and less often.

Business, economics and international trade

International economics and trade, business administration and accounting are the most common English-medium degrees and the least differentiated. Coursework leans on case studies and group presentations, assessment is continuous, and the mathematics is lighter than in a Chinese-taught equivalent. They suit students heading for trade or family business rather than a quantitative master's.

Entry Requirements When You Do Not Speak Chinese

You do not need Chinese to be admitted to an English-medium bachelor's programme, but you do need a certificate proving English ability: IELTS 6.0 overall, TOEFL iBT 80, or a school-leaving qualification taught in English, with higher scores demanded by medical programmes. Chinese proficiency is not tested at entry, and most universities admit at HSK zero.

The academic side is a completed secondary school certificate with transcripts, notarised and translated, plus a passport valid for at least six months. Medical programmes add a chemistry and biology requirement, and a few universities interview by video before issuing the admission letter and JW202 form that the visa application depends on.

Documents and timing

Applications open roughly a year before the September intake and are processed on a rolling basis, so early files are read by staff with time to spare. Application fees run 400 to 800 RMB per university and are non-refundable. Universities need two to four weeks to issue the admission letter and JW202, and the X1 visa follows only after both exist.

Tuition, Living Costs and What a Scholarship Covers

Budget 18,000 to 45,000 RMB a year for tuition, with medicine at the top of that range and business at the bottom, plus 1,500 to 2,500 RMB a month for accommodation, food and transport outside Beijing and Shanghai. A Chinese Government Scholarship covers tuition, dormitory and a monthly stipend, but undergraduate awards are the scarcest category in the scheme.

The Honest Limits: Faculty Depth and Internships

An English-medium label promises a language, not a standard. Some programmes have twenty years of graduates and a stable faculty; others launched three years ago with borrowed staff and a curriculum copied from a partner university. The gap shows up in years three and four, when specialist modules and laboratory work require teachers who can supervise in English.

Checking whether a programme is really English-medium

Ask three questions by email and keep the answers. Which percentage of the modules in years three and four are taught in English? How many full-time faculty in this department hold a doctorate from outside China? Can I see the module list with credit values for all four years? A programme that answers all three with documents is real; one that answers with adjectives is not.

Recognition at home

Check your own country's recognition rules first, because the degree is a Chinese bachelor's degree regardless of language. Medical graduates face licensing examinations at home, engineering graduates may need accreditation review, and some professional bodies require a transcript showing specific module content.

The Chinese You Will Need Anyway

English-medium teaching does not remove the need for Chinese, it postpones it. Hospital rotations, laboratory safety briefings, internship placements and everyday administration run in Chinese, and several universities now require English-medium students to reach HSK 4, 180 points on the 300-point scale, before graduation.

That requirement is a graduation condition, not an entry condition, which is why year one is the right time to start. A student who reaches HSK 4 by the end of year two has a materially easier final two years.

How to Shortlist Programmes

Shortlist six programmes rather than one, and rank them by what you can verify rather than by what the brochure claims. Five filters do most of the work, and all five can be answered by email in a single message to the international office, which means the comparison takes a week rather than a semester.

  1. Confirm the programme appears on the university's own international admissions pages, not only on an agent's site.
  2. Request the four-year module list with credit values and the percentage taught in English.
  3. Ask for the graduating cohort size and where the last two cohorts are now.
  4. Check tuition in writing, including laboratory, insurance and accommodation charges.
  5. Confirm the campus city, since several universities teach international undergraduates at a suburban campus.

None of this is exotic diligence, but it separates a degree that opens a licensing examination at home from one that needs explaining in every interview.
