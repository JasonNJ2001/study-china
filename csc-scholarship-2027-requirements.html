CSC Scholarship 2027 Requirements — What Actually Decides the Outcome

Every year thousands of applicants meet every published CSC requirement and still get rejected. The requirements are necessary — but they are not the decision. This article separates the two.

The published requirements (necessary, not sufficient)

  1. **Nationality and age** — non-Chinese citizen, generally under 25 for bachelor's, under 35 for master's, under 40 for PhD.
  2. **Academic record** — high school diploma for undergraduate; a bachelor's for master's; a master's for PhD.
  3. **Language ability** — HSK 4–5 for Chinese-taught programs; IELTS 6.0+ or TOEFL 80+ for English-taught ones.
  4. **Health** — the Foreigner Physical Examination Form, completed within the last six months.
  5. **No concurrent scholarship** — you cannot hold another Chinese government scholarship at the same time.

Meet all five and your file enters the pile. That is all it means.

What the committee actually weighs

1. Fit with a specific supervisor or program

For graduate study, the single strongest signal is whether a professor has agreed to take you. A study plan that names a research group, references their recent papers, and explains what you would contribute reads completely differently from a generic statement.

2. A study plan that answers "why China, why this school, why now"

Most rejected study plans describe the applicant. Strong ones describe a problem in the field, the specific lab or curriculum that addresses it, and a concrete plan for the two or four years. If the same essay could be submitted to a university in any country, it is not specific enough.

3. Academic trajectory, not just GPA

Committees read transcripts for direction: an upward trend, relevant coursework, a thesis topic that connects to the proposed research. A 3.4 with a coherent research narrative often beats a 3.8 with none.

4. Recommendation letters with substance

A letter that says "excellent student, hardworking" adds nothing. A letter that describes a specific project, your contribution, and why you are ready for graduate research is worth more than three generic ones.

5. Timing and channel discipline

Apply through **one** channel — Type A (embassy) or Type B (university direct). Duplicate applications are rejected without review. And deadlines are earlier than most applicants expect: several embassies close in February, and universities often close their Type B portal in March or April.

The document details that quietly disqualify people

  • **Notarized transcripts** — scanned copies are not enough; notarization plus translation can take 3–4 weeks.
  • **Physical examination form** — must use the official template, with the hospital stamp on the photo.
  • **Passport validity** — at least six months beyond the intended end of study.
  • **Name spelling** — must match the passport exactly across every document. Mismatches trigger manual review and delay.

Bottom line

The CSC checklist gets you into the review. What gets you the scholarship is evidence that a specific program wants a specific version of you. Build the file around that, and the requirements stop being a barrier.
