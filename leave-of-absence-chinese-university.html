Taking a Leave of Absence from a Chinese University

By ORIGIN Education · 2026-11-26

A leave of absence at a Chinese university is not a gap year and not a quiet disappearance. It is a formal change of student status that freezes your place for a fixed period and obliges you to apply back in. Students who treat it as an email to a teacher usually find out at the exit-entry office, when their permit no longer matches their status.

A leave of absence is a status change, not a break from class

A leave of absence (休学, xiuxue) suspends your enrolment without ending it. The university keeps your student record on file, tuition and dormitory charges stop, and you may not attend classes or sit examinations during the period. Short absences of a few weeks are handled as ordinary leave (请假) and never touch your status; anything longer needs the formal approval.

休学, 请假 and withdrawal are three different things

请假 is granted by your class teacher for days, rarely more than two weeks, and the semester keeps running without you. 休学 is approved by the school and the academic affairs office, appears on your record, and stops the clock. Withdrawal (退学) ends the relationship: your student number closes, the permit is cancelled, and re-entry means a fresh application.

Who actually signs it

The chain is your supervisor or class teacher, then the school meeting, then the academic affairs office, or the graduate school for postgraduates. The international students office is a registrar here, not a decision-maker. What you receive is a written decision with a start and end date, and those dates govern everything that follows.

The grounds that get approved

The reasons that pass review are the ones a Chinese university can document: illness that needs treatment, a family emergency, pregnancy and childbirth, compulsory military service at home, or a genuine financial collapse. Academic difficulty is not a ground for suspension — failing courses leads to retaking, repeating a year, or withdrawal, never to a leave.

Medical grounds

A diagnosis from a hospital at or above county level, translated into Chinese, plus a treatment plan with an expected duration, is the strongest file you can build. Universities rarely argue with clear medical cases; they argue with vague ones. If the condition is psychological, ask the campus clinic which hospital it refers to: self-reported distress without a clinical record is the file most often sent back.

Non-medical grounds

Family bereavement, a parent's illness, military service, childbirth and documented financial hardship are accepted with evidence: a death certificate, a conscription notice, a birth certificate, or bank records. "I need time to think about my direction" is not a ground in any Chinese university.

How long you can suspend

Ministry of Education Order No. 41, in force since 1 September 2017, sets the norm at one year per leave with a cumulative ceiling of two years. The clock starts on the approval date printed on your decision letter, not the day you stopped attending, and a second leave needs a fresh application with the same standard of evidence.

What retained status covers

It keeps your student number, your place in the cohort and your right to return without re-entering admissions. It does not keep your campus card active, your dormitory bed, your library access, or your scholarship. Treat the letter as a promise about one thing: a seat if you return in time.

Your residence permit does not pause with your enrolment

The student residence permit is issued against your enrolment, so suspension puts it at risk. Once the university reports the status change to the local exit-entry administration, the permit is normally curtailed, and you either leave China before it lapses or convert to a stay visa (停留签证), which under the Exit and Entry Administration Law can run for up to 180 days.

If you stay in China

You need the university's written support, a 停留签证 application filed before the residence permit expires, and a local address registration. A stay visa does not let you study or work. Overstaying one day produces a fine of RMB 500 per day up to RMB 10,000, plus a record that follows future visa applications.

If you leave China

Cancel the permit at the exit-entry office before you fly and keep the cancellation slip. Re-enter later on a fresh X1 visa built on a new JW202 form issued for your return. Never leave with a valid permit and let it expire abroad: closing your file from overseas is slow.

Tuition, dorm fees and scholarships

Money follows status, not the calendar. Tuition already paid for the suspended year is generally carried forward to the year you return rather than refunded, dormitory charges stop on the date you hand back the key, and scholarship stipends stop in the month the suspension takes effect. Ask for the carry-forward in writing: the default remedy is a credit note, not cash.

Chinese Government Scholarship holders

CSC-funded students need written approval from both the university and the scholarship body before the leave starts, and the stipend is suspended for the whole period. Leaving without that approval counts as abandoning the scholarship, which is harder to reverse than a university decision, because the quota has already been reallocated.

The application, step by step

The paperwork is not complicated, but it is strictly sequential: each office signs only after the previous one has, and one missing document sends you back to the start of the queue. Allow two to three weeks, and file before the registration window closes rather than after you have stopped attending.

  1. Ask your school office for the suspension form and the exact document list for your case.
  2. Collect the evidence — hospital record, certificate, or family documentation — with a Chinese translation.
  3. Get your supervisor or class teacher to sign first, with a written opinion.
  4. Submit to the school meeting and collect the minutes or stamped approval.
  5. Take the file to the academic affairs office, or the graduate school, for the formal decision letter.
  6. Register the change with the international students office so the police record is updated.
  7. Settle the dormitory checkout, the library and any lab equipment before you leave the campus.

Returning: the window most students miss

Reinstatement is not automatic and it is not a conversation. The decision letter states a return date, and most universities require a formal application at least 30 days before the semester begins, with fresh evidence that the original ground has ended — a discharge summary, for example, or a birth certificate.

Missing that window creates no penalty, but it pushes your return to the following intake, which can cost a full year. Students who file properly are usually back on schedule; students who treat suspension as a private decision tend to lose the year anyway.
